# Echoes of the Abyss — World Overview

## Setting

Echoes of the Abyss takes place in the Pelagion Rift, a vast underwater region formed after the Salinity Collapse destabilized global ocean chemistry. Humanity retreated into deep-sea habitats, forming isolated city-states, industrial mining colonies, and bio-engineered enclaves.

Two centuries ago the Salinity Collapse poisoned the surface ocean. Humanity fled downward. The Rift is the last place with heat, chemistry, and shelter together — and every war here is over those three nouns.

The geography those wars are fought across — the regions, the named places, and where the campaign happens — is in [world-map.md](world-map.md). This document is the setting itself: what broke, why down was the answer, who the descent turned into, and why nobody has gone back up.

---

## One Valley Holding Everyone

The Pelagion Rift is a branched tectonic scar **roughly ninety kilometres end to end and never more than forty wide** — one deep valley system holding, as far as anyone can demonstrate, the whole of surviving civilisation ([world-map.md](world-map.md) §1). Whether anyone survived somewhere else, in some other refuge, is a question the Rift has stopped funding.

Two facts follow from the size, and everything social in the setting is downstream of them.

**Nobody is far from anybody.** The Rift's wars are neighbourhood disputes. The Commune's food and the Consortium's poorest berths share a fence — a flood front crossed from one to the other in 197 PC. Knight crystal country ends where Directorate water begins. Sorrowgate sits within a long day's transit of everyone, which is the entire reason the court works ([world-map.md](world-map.md) §4). There is no strategic depth here in the surface sense: there is nowhere to retreat *to*.

**The valley runs north-shallow to south-deep, and that one gradient organises everything.** The Commune's kelp plateaus are in the first clear water under the Lid; the Consortium's vertical city hangs down the west wall; the Knights' chapter-houses seam the eastern slope below the thermocline; the Directorate's trench country steps down to the south; and below all of it, at 4,410 m, is the Mouth. **Depth is status**, so the map is also a social diagram, and an insult in the north is a compass direction everywhere else.

## The Salinity Collapse

This section is design canon — the bible's record of what actually happened. **No character knows all of it.** The records were lost in the descent, the Rift is not sentimental about the Surface Age ([timeline.md](timeline.md)), and two centuries of retelling have worn the event down to a name. Characters may repeat fragments of the truth, garble it, or contradict each other; narration never corrects them.

What happened, in order:

1. **The ocean stopped turning over.** The global circulation that carried oxygen from the surface into the deep — and nutrients from the deep back up — weakened over roughly two generations, then failed. Stratification became permanent: warm dead water sat on cold dead water, and nothing mixed.
2. **The middle of the ocean suffocated.** Oxygen minimum zones — the low-oxygen bands that had always existed at mid-depth — expanded upward year by year toward the light.
3. **The water went sour.** In water without oxygen, a different chemistry wins: bacteria that breathe sulfate instead, and exhale hydrogen sulfide. *Sour* is the old industry word for sulfide-laden water, and it is the word that survived. When the suffocating middle ocean reached the sunlit surface layer, the sour chemistry came with it. The surface ocean turned pale, milky, and lethal — to fish, to plankton, to the food web, and to anything that breathed the air just above it on a bad day.
4. **The land starved beside a dead sea.** The land did not drown and did not burn. It lost the ocean — most of what fed the coasts, most of what steadied the weather, and, in sulfide weather rolling off the water, whole shorelines in an afternoon. The interior went slower and no better. By the time the shore cities emptied, nobody was keeping records that survived.

The sour chemistry is not a wound that is healing. It is an ecosystem, it is stable because it won, and it has held for two hundred and fourteen years.

**Why down had a destination:** the Pelagion Rift was already lit and already working. The Ninefold Vein was producing decades before year 0, and the Surface Age's one attempt at a *permanent* deep colony — a civic city with a transit line, a passenger terminus, and the largest hydrophone array ever built — stood half-finished in the Rift when the Collapse arrived ([timeline.md](timeline.md)). The descent was a rout, but it was a rout toward the only door with a light behind it.

## The Lid

The Rift's name for the sour layer overhead: roughly the top **150 metres** of the ocean, the old sunlit zone, poisoned since the Collapse. The boundary has held at 150 m for two centuries. The Commune measures it every tide anyway.

- The Lid is why *shallow* is an insult with a floor under it ([culture.md](culture.md)). Status runs downward because safety does: the shallows are not merely poor, they are the closest inhabited water to the poison.
- The Lid is **a mechanic now, because it always was a fact**: a hull that climbs into the top 150 m is in sour water, and after a short grace the water starts keeping the hull — unhealable, like the crush at the other end of the column, and at the same rate for every navy, because the Lid predates all of them ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md) §2 has the numbers). No map invites anyone up there and no rule forbids it; the door up is still not locked — the water itself is simply honest about what it is. This is not the Directorate's shallow-water penalty, which is their own physiology above 400 m and theirs alone ([factions.md](factions.md)); a Directorate hull in the Lid is deep in a cost it chose twice.
- Under the Lid, the water is the Rift's: the Shelf from the first clear metres down to 400 m, Mid-Water to 1,800 m, the Abyssal below that, and the thermocline at 1,200 m dividing the whole column into two acoustic worlds ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md), [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md)).

## Why Nobody Goes Back

The honest answer is that the question stopped being asked, and the Rift can date when.

**The Sounding of 141.** In 141 PC, the only joint expedition in the Rift's history — Consortium hulls, Commune provisions, Directorate listeners, Knight instruments — went up through the Lid in sealed pressure vessels to answer the question the first century kept asking. They spent three tides on the surface. They found the sky, which the report describes in one sentence and does not revisit. They found white water to the horizon, air that could be breathed on a good wind, and nothing to eat, nothing to burn, and nothing to hear. The expedition's hydrophone record — four hours of instruments listening to a dead ocean — is the real report, and the reason there was never a second expedition. The Directorate keeps a copy. Nobody plays it twice.

After 141, *when do we go back* left the Rift's politics and never returned. Three things closed the door, and none of them is a lock:

- **There is nothing up there.** The surface is not forbidden; it is finished. Heat, chemistry, and shelter — the three nouns every Rift war is fought over — all live at the bottom of the water column now. The surface has none of them, and a civilization built on vent heat and mineral chemistry cannot bring its own.
- **The Rift is no longer built for it.** Every hull, every skill, every debt, and every institution in the setting is a deep-water thing. The Directorate's cohorts physically cannot make the trip — a fourth-generation Deep Cohort officer cannot survive above 400 m unsuited ([factions.md](factions.md)) — and for everyone else the surface is a place their grandparents' grandparents fled, with nothing at the top of the climb but the recording.
- **Home moved.** Two centuries is eight generations. The Rift does not experience itself as being in exile, and this is the setting's quiet, load-bearing fact: nobody down here is waiting for the water to clear. The dead are committed *downward* ([culture.md](culture.md)). Whether anyone survived somewhere else, in some other refuge, is a question the Rift has stopped funding.

**Writing rule:** the surface is never a goal, a hope, or a plot device. No faction wants it back, no mission goes there, and no character pines for the sun — a character who did would be making a category error every culture in the Rift grew out of a century ago. The one emotion the Surface Age is allowed to evoke is the one the Sounding brought home: the Rift listened to the world it came from, and the world had stopped saying anything.

## The Three Cultural Facts

Three facts about how the Rift speaks, and this document is where they are established. [culture.md](culture.md) is the working reference for everything downstream of them — the five registers, names, titles, rituals, and the writing guide — and this section deliberately stops before it starts.

- **Status runs downward.** *"Shallow"* is a universal insult, in every faction, including the one that lives at 320 m and formally denies that depth-status exists. The deeper you can live, the higher you stand. The Lid is why: safety runs downward, so status followed it.
- **Water is free but dry is a luxury.** A berth without ambient damp, a room where paper survives, a bed that is never cold — dry space is the Rift's real currency of comfort, and *dry* is the adjective every faction uses for wealth.
- **Nobody says *look*.** The verb of attention across every faction is *listen*. You do not *see* someone's point, you *hear* it. This is not decoration and not a figure of speech: it is what a society sounds like after two centuries in which every important fact arrived as noise ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md)).

## The Four Powers

Four institutions came out of the descent, and each is a different answer to the same problem — **noise**. Their politics, their technology and their economics are all restatements of that one argument, which is why the game can make a faction's worldview into a mechanic without inventing anything. [factions.md](factions.md) is the institutional detail; this is who they are in the world.

**The Bathyarch Consortium** — chartered 19 PC as a vent-field concession, on the strength of the salvage that recovered Halvard's dead, and never formally acknowledged becoming a government. It owns the thermal grid, the freight corridors, and through debt-berth contracts roughly 40% of the Rift's population. It is also the reason there is a Rift civilisation at all: it built the hulls and it runs the air. Its founding field is dying, and it has eleven years.

**The Pelagia Commune** — formed 33 PC when the kelp plateaus refused Consortium grain-debt terms and fed themselves instead. Within a generation they fed everyone, which makes them beloved, indispensable and militarily contemptible in the same breath. They cannot mobilise quickly because mobilising requires everyone to agree, and they will not treat that as a defect. In 204 PC they proved habitable depth can be *manufactured*, and the Directorate has said in writing what a second seeding would be.

**The Abyssal Directorate** — descended from the 52 PC Deep Cohort Programme, which answered pressure with germline modification instead of hull engineering. Eight generations later they are not quite the same species, and they are also the Rift's most functional society: no debt, no wage, universal berth, and nobody chose any of it. They listen better than anyone, and what they listen to is the Mouth.

**The Hadron Knights** — founded 118 PC by schismatic Consortium acoustics engineers and the disgraced Sull family, and the least populous power by an order of magnitude at perhaps 30,000 souls. Entry is by acoustic aptitude testing at age nine, so the Order cannot grow, and it knows exactly how many years of relevance that leaves it. They alone can work resonant crystal, which is why they are disproportionately powerful and why they raised the First Chord.

**No faction is written as the villain**, and all four campaign endings are coherent, costly and irreconcilable ([game-identity.md](game-identity.md)).

## The Drift

The Rift is inhabited by something other than people. **The Drift** is the living layer, and the Rift's own word for it: crews say *the Drift is up tonight* the way surface people once talked about weather. It is a fifth participant in the setting — owned by nobody, hostile to nobody in particular, and driven by exactly the input the four powers are driven by, which is noise ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md)).

Two things about it belong in the world rather than in the ruleset. It **punishes loudness impersonally** — a commander can out-position an opponent, and nobody out-positions a Sounder that heard them mine for six minutes. And it **can be killed**: over-extraction, noise and hunger degrade a region permanently, so the winner of a long, loud match can inherit a corpse of a biome. The map being alive is not decoration; it is the one participant that keeps score across a whole match and does not negotiate.

## The Mouth

At **4,410 m**, at the southern terminus of everything, sits the Mouth: an eleven-kilometre depression that returns sonar pings *before they should arrive*, on a cycle that shortened from 43 hours to 39 in 213 PC. It is the unresolved centre of the setting's cosmology, and each faction has a different, incompatible relationship to it:

- The **Bathyarch Consortium** files for mineral rights.
- The **Pelagia Commune** calls it a wound.
- The **Abyssal Directorate** worships it — the state religion of the Deep Choir is built around *attending* to it, never explaining it.
- The **Hadron Knights** are building something to answer it: the Second Chord.

**The standing rule, which binds this document as much as any other:** the Mouth is described, measured, and never explained ([culture.md](culture.md) §6). No character is ever authoritatively correct about it, and neither is narration. See [characters.md](characters.md) and [factions.md](factions.md) for how each faction's crisis connects back to it, and [timeline.md](timeline.md) for the anomaly log all four powers keep and none of them share.
