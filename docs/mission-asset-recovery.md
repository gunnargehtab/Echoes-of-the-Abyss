# The Ledger 1 — Asset Recovery

> The first mission of the Consortium campaign ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §4), specified. The
> second mission document the bible carries, written to the pattern
> [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) sets: everything here is authored — the forces,
> the water, the beats, the numbers and the text — and code transcribes this document.

**Setting:** Face Six of the Ninefold Vein, the Consortium's founding field, 214 PC
([world-map.md](world-map.md); [timeline.md](timeline.md)).

**Mission id:** `ledger-asset-recovery` — namespaced by campaign, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §1.

---

## 1. What Face Six Is

The Ninefold Vein is nine vents on a fault line at 900–1,400 m, producing since before year 0,
on the corporate seal, and in terminal decline since 209 PC ([world-map.md](world-map.md);
[factions.md](factions.md)). A dying field is not a quiet one — it is worked harder, shored
faster, and inspected less, because every tide of downtime is on the insolvency projection.

At the turn of the second tide, Face Six stopped transmitting. The vent line under the face blew
out, the gallery came down, and the blowout tore a raw channel through the field's masking
ground. Three facts about that water decide the whole mission:

**The field masks; the wound does not.** The Vein is Thermal Vein biome, PF 0.45 — the vent roar
that has hidden Consortium industry for two centuries ([environments.md](environments.md)). The
blowout channel is raw rock, and raw rock carries: the Scar around the fallen face reads as
trench acoustics, PF 1.6. The one place the column must work is the one place the whole field
hears it. Nobody arranged that. It is what a wound in masking ground is.

**Something is alive in the fall.** Face Six's crew reached the refuge chamber, and the chamber
is rated for four tides. The third began when the recovery writ was signed.

**The Drift follows industry.** Draymaw packs have shadowed the Vein's working faces for two
centuries — the staple predator, the animal most likely to answer a careless economy
([bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §4) — and an Ashgrazer herd feeds on the upper terrace, because
Consortium mining sits directly on top of their feeding grounds, which is a joke the Consortium
has stopped finding funny. The salvage will be the loudest thing on the field. Everything that
listens will attend it.

Nobody in the column says any of this. It is the ground the mission stands on.

## 2. Whose Hulls the Player Commands

**The player commands a Board-chartered salvage column: three combat hulls and three lift
barges, dispatched under a recovery writ signed for the Ninth Board.**

Asset recovery is the oldest thing the Consortium does. The salvage combine that recovered
Halvard's dead in 14 PC was chartered as the Consortium five years later
([timeline.md](timeline.md)) — the corporation is, at its founding, the people who go into
failed structures and bring things out. Two centuries on, that work has a manifest format, an
escort budget, and a register that prices everything it touches, and this mission is played
inside all three.

The column's hulls carry asset numbers, because everything the Consortium owns does. The lift
barges are **Lift One** through **Lift Three**; the escorts are the **flagship** and two
corvettes. Foreman Corwin Osk, Ninefold's site chief ([characters.md](characters.md)), is on the
face and on the channel for the whole mission. He does not command the column. He knows the
ground, and the column is told to listen to him, in a register where "listen to the site chief"
is a clause in a writ.

**Engine bound, stated so nobody corrects it into a bug.** The column carries the Consortium
faction value, and the mission seats no other player-faction party. The Drift is not a party —
fauna are owned by the slot no player can hold ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md)), and this mission,
like every mission, does not change that.

### What the column does not carry

1. **No active sonar.** [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 withholds the ping until mission 3, and
   the manifest is the diegetic form: work hulls do not carry the survey array, because an
   array on a salvage column is an unfunded line item. The player spends this mission being
   attended by things they cannot resolve on demand, which is the state the ping will later
   end — and the ping's cost has already been taught, once, at Sorrowgate.
2. **No base, no economy.** Nothing is built and nothing is mined. The map carries no resource
   fields, the build pages are not offered — the pattern
   [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §3 set, with its reason attached: *the field
   is under a recovery writ, not a works order*. The lift figure above is authored to the
   throttle table's loudest row, but the throttle itself never appears; the economy and its
   four-position lever are mission 2's system, taught by the man standing on this face.
3. **Silent Running is present, and it is wrong here.** Nothing diegetic strikes the button, and
   the mission does not fence it. An escort that runs silent has its weapons offline
   ([glossary.md](glossary.md)) while the lift beside it holds 68, so the pack commits to the
   barge and the silent hull watches. The mission is playtested against a player who tries it
   once — most will, because every stealth instinct the genre teaches points that way — and the
   Drift's answer arrives with its own four-second tell ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §2). The
   lesson is survivable and specific, which is the only kind this campaign teaches.

Nothing in this mission outguns the player. Two Draymaw packs and a reluctant herd cannot
destroy three escorted combat hulls run loud; they can and will destroy a lift barge whose
escort is somewhere else, or quiet. The player is safe exactly as long as they are loud in the
right place, and the stake is the manifest.

## 4. The Klaxon Posture

The system this mission teaches, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2: one system, introduced in
the first three minutes, load-bearing by the last five. The system is the Consortium doctrine
itself — **fight loud, survive it** ([factions.md](factions.md)) — and it lands in four
movements, each a fact the player cannot route around:

1. **Loud is the nature of the work.** A lift runs at 68. The column moves at cruise through
   ground that hears. There is no quiet way to do a salvage, so the meter this campaign's
   player watches is not "how quiet am I" but "where is my loudness standing".
2. **The loudest hull is the target.** Fauna commit to the loudest entity in range, not the
   nearest ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §2). This single rule makes SIG a *taunt*: the flagship
   with systems live, at 65, out-shouts a lifting barge at 68 only when it stands close enough
   for the pack's ears to read it as the bigger noise — position is what aims your loudness.
3. **Loud hits harder.** The Klaxon Doctrine's +12% damage while SIG > 60
   ([factions.md](factions.md)) belongs to the hull that keeps its systems live. The flagship
   dark is quieter, safer-feeling, out of the band, and wrong three ways at once.
4. **Loud survives.** Heaviest armour, best repair, and an escort built to absorb the
   commitment it invites. Being found is survivable for exactly one faction, and the player is
   holding it.

Sorrowgate's escort was made of listening; this one is made of loudness. The prologue taught
that hearing is worth everything, and this mission begins the Consortium's seven-mission
argument that *being heard* is a price you can budget for — which is the faction's whole
politics, taught through the hands ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §2, rule 2).

### The SIG budget, inverted

**SIG budget: 65** — and the mission is playtested against a player who stays *under* it, per
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's rule read in the direction this faction requires. The budget is
the loudness the mission is tuned to expect; the failure it anticipates is not the player who
exceeds it but the tutorial-trained player who creeps, splits quiet, and leaves the barges to be
the loudest things on the field. The prologue's budget was a ceiling with a courtesy attached.
This one is a working level with a doctrine attached, and the campaign alternates between those
two readings for seven missions.

## 6. The Taps

**The chamber transmits the only way a sealed chamber can: someone inside strikes the hull, in
a worked pattern, on the interval. It has not stopped since the fall.**

Struck iron carries. In the Scar's raw water it carries far — a small, periodic, patterned
emission from inside the fall, present from the mission's first tick, audible to the column
from the moment it crosses into the channel. It is the mission's beacon, its clock, and its
dread, all in one diegetic sound:

- **Beacon** — the taps are how the column knows where in the fall the chamber sits, and how
  the player knows the objective is still an objective.
- **Clock** — the chamber is rated for four tides and the third has run. The taps are the
  schedule, audible.
- **Dread** — the Drift hears exactly what the column hears. Seventeen people are ringing a
  dinner bell with their own hull because the alternative is not being found, and every one of
  them knows what else is listening. Osk says so, once, in the register that can say it (§12).

Nobody is wrong. The crew transmits because rescue requires being findable. The Drift attends
because attending noise is what the Drift is. The column is loud because salvage is loud. The
prologue built its disaster out of three parties being severally correct; this mission builds
its pressure the same way, and the campaign never builds it any other way
([campaign.md](campaign.md) §2, rule 1).

**When Lift Three rigs the chamber, the taps stop** — struck iron from inside a chamber under
tow would foul the rigging survey, and the crew knows the procedure. The last four minutes of
the mission are played without the sound the player has steered by for fourteen, and the silence
is worse. [audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md) owns the principle: the mix's absences are
information.

## 7. What Answers

**Draymaws, mostly. The herd, once. Nothing else — and the mission does not pretend otherwise.**

The packs are the pressure: working depth 900 m, pursuit band 500–1,300 m — the workings, the
roads, and the Scar are all theirs to reach ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §4). They shadow at the
edge of hearing, grow interested when the lift noise runs, and commit when it peaks, which is
the species' own doctrine quoted back at it. Every commitment is preceded by four seconds of
audible tell, and every commitment goes to the loudest hull in reach — which is the whole game
of §4, played over and over with rising stakes as the lifts stagger.

The herd is the weather: Ashgrazers feed on the upper terrace (350–850 m band), reluctant,
deaf, and dangerous only when something detonates near the ground they graze. At 07:30 one of
the vent line's published eruption intervals arrives ([hazards.md](hazards.md)) and the herd
stampedes across the terrace — station-breaking, road-closing, and *not an attack*. There
is nothing to shoot and shooting is worse than nothing: a stampede is spent by holding the road
and letting it pass, and Osk says exactly that, because a man who has worked this field for
twenty-nine years prices its weather correctly.

No Sounder. The basins are far away, nothing here transmits a challenge, and the campaign does
not spend the colossus twice in two missions. The pressure in this mission is smaller, plural,
and economic — the Drift as a running cost of loudness, not as a catastrophe — because that is
the shape of the Drift a Consortium player will live under for six more missions.

## 8. The Objective

**Recover the manifest: three lifts from Face Six to the Rail Head.**

Each lift is the same shape: a barge holds at the fall while the cut runs — two minutes at 68,
under the Scar's acoustics — then hauls loaded up the Works to the Rail Head, at a barge's
pace, with the pack pressure its own noise has earned. The lifts are staggered by the writ's
order, so from the second cut onward the column is stretched: a loaded barge on the road and a
cutting barge at the face, 1,500 m apart, and one flagship.

**One flagship, two ends of a road, and the loudest hull in reach is a choice.** Anchor the
face and the road's barge is the loudest thing in its own reach; ride the road and the cut
stands alone. Four escorts could cover both and the writ funded three, which the writ would
call an exposure priced against the schedule. That is the mission's single decision, made
continuously, and the player makes it with the SIG meter — position is what aims your loudness
(§4), and this objective is that sentence made of ground.

### Results

| Result | Condition | The Board's reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recovery complete** | All three lifts reach the Rail Head | "Three assets returned to the registry. Contents of 9-06-200: seventeen, alive, reassigned. The face is closed. The ledger notes no exceptions, and notes the cost in the usual place." |
| **Write-down** | 9-06-200 reaches the Rail Head; machinery lost | "The chamber is recovered. The remainder is written down. A write-down is not a tragedy; log it as an exception and continue." |
| **The number stays** | 9-06-200 does not come out | "The chamber did not come out. The registry keeps the number. The Board does not strike assets, and it will not strike this one." |

The middle reading exists because the machinery can genuinely be lost — a loaded barge whose
escort was elsewhere is the Drift's to take — and a campaign that says *losing is content*
([campaign.md](campaign.md) §2) has to mean it in mission one. A partial result ends the mission
and is a result; the player is not asked to replay it. The third reading is the Consortium's
whole memory in two sentences: Sector Kell is sealed, unlit, and still on the registry
([world-map.md](world-map.md)), and the register's refusal to strike what it killed is the
nearest thing to grief the language of instruments allows.

### The failure, and the sounds that precede it

There is one failure condition and it is not a timer. **The fall is still falling.** Face Six
has been coming down in stages since the blowout, and at 18:00 the last of it goes. A chamber
still inside the fall at 18:00 is inside it when it closes.

The precursor is diegetic, continuous, and older than the mission:

| When | What the player hears |
| --- | --- |
| 00:00 | The fall's complaint — a wounded gallery under 1,150 m of water complains continuously, from the first tick, under everything else the mission plays |
| 11:30 | **The fall shifts.** A ground beat closes part of Face Six; the complaint climbs a register and stays there. Osk names it for what it is (§12) |
| 17:00 | **The complaint's terminal note** — the sound of shoring finishing. A full minute before the end, for a player who ignored eleven |

Eighteen minutes of warning against a lift sequence that needs fourteen, with the last-minute
call [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 requires delivered by the ground itself. The taps stop when
the chamber rigs (§6); the complaint stops for nobody.

## 10. What It Teaches

One system, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10: **the Klaxon posture** — SIG as a resource you
spend by standing somewhere, on a hull built to afford it. The four movements of §4 land in
order across the beat table: loud is the work's nature (03:30), the loudest hull is the target
(05:00), loud hits harder (every exchange after), loud survives (the haul, entirely).

What this mission deliberately does not teach:

- **The throttle and the economy** — mission 2, *Shift Change*, where Osk is the commander and
  the hum is the subject. This mission's lift figure is the throttle table's loudest row used
  as an authored constant; the lever stays out of the player's hands so that when mission 2
  hands it over, it is new.
- **Masking as a tool** — mission 3, *Baffle*, whose whole subject is bending PF on purpose.
  This mission *shows* masking — the Works are quiet ground and the Scar is loud ground, and
  the player crosses the line at 02:30 with the meter steady — but never asks the player to
  operate it. Shown before operated, exactly as the ping was shown at Sorrowgate before it is
  handed over in mission 3.
- **Depth** — everything here sits in Mid-Water and the column is rated for all of it. The
  workings continue below the face toward 1,400 m and the mission never sends anyone there;
  depth is felt as unvisited water, the prologue's own treatment, until mission 5 makes it a
  bleed and mission 6 makes it the point.

## 12. The Briefing

The recovery writ, read to the column at 00:00. Signed for the Ninth Board. The Consortium's
register is defined in [culture.md](culture.md) §3.

> Face Six stopped transmitting at the turn of the second tide. The seismic record is attached
> and is not ambiguous. The face is closed. This is a recovery, not a reopening.
>
> Three assets are listed. Asset 9-06-114, the cutter head. Asset 9-06-181, the walking frame.
> Asset 9-06-200, the refuge chamber — contents seventeen, condition transmitting. The chamber

>

- *Recover Asset 9-06-181. The frame held; bring it home in the condition it earned.*
- *The chamber transmits. Treat the interval as schedule.*
- *Return the column to the Rail Head. The writ closes at the count.*

Directorate would render in the passive and the Commune could not render at all; Vail's is an
instruction phrased as an invoice, and no other faction bills its own escort.
