# The Second Chord 1 — Aptitude

> The first mission of the Knight campaign ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §7), specified. The fifth
> mission document the bible carries, to the pattern [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md)
> sets: everything here is authored — the forces, the water, the beats, the numbers and the text —
> and code transcribes this document.

**Setting:** the Sixth Seam, the Resonance Fields' middle slope between the Ninth Chapter-House
and the Third, 214 PC ([world-map.md](world-map.md); [characters.md](characters.md)).

**Mission id:** `second-chord-aptitude` — namespaced by campaign, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §1.

---

## 1. What a Sounding Is

The Order does not survey crystal by looking at it. A resonant seam is measured by **striking it
and listening to what comes back** — a hull aims its emission at the seam's face, holds the note
for thirty seconds, and a second hull standing off the beam reads the return. The interval
between the strike and the reply, and the way the reply decays, is the tonnage. This is
crystal-craft, taught in a chapter-house from the age of nine, and it is the oldest thing the
Order knows how to do.

Four facts about that practice decide the whole mission.

**A sounding is the loudest thing a Knight hull does.** It runs at **SIG 80** — not a new figure,
but the Sounding Spire's own active number ([units.md](units.md)), because a hull sounding a seam
is a hull doing a Spire's work at a Spire's price. There is no quiet way to measure crystal. The
Order's own name for the structure says so.

**A sounding is aimed.** The emission is directional like everything else the Knights emit
([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8), and at SIG 80 the difference between its two ends is the
difference between 2,593 m and 792 m of Tier-3 classification. Where the beam goes after it
passes through the seam is the entire mission.

**The hull that strikes cannot hear.** A sounding deafens its own emitter — the note is on top of
its hydrophones for the full thirty seconds — so a seam takes **two hulls**: a voice and an ear.
That is not a rule invented for this mission. It is why the Order counts its field strength in
pairs and always has.

**The Fields are not silent ground.** Crystal country rings continuously under any pressure
change ([world-map.md](world-map.md)), bearings lie at PF 0.70, and pings return phantoms. A
Knight reads that ambient chime the way the Directorate reads silence. Everybody else hears a
haunted room, and this mission never leaves it.

## 2. Why Sull Wants a Number

The plan for a raid on the Mouth's rim deposits is on Choirmaster Ivane Sull's desk, and she has
not authorised it ([characters.md](characters.md)). The reason is not squeamishness. It is that
she does not yet know she has to.

The Second Chord needs more resonant crystal than exists in Knight territory
([factions.md](factions.md)). *More than exists* is a claim, and Sull is the
great-great-granddaughter of a surveyor who was fired for a claim about this Rift and turned out
to be right
([characters.md](characters.md)). She will not repeat the family's mistake in the other
direction. Before she spends the Order on the Rim she wants the Fields' own seams sounded and
totalled, honestly, by somebody who will bring back the number that is there rather than the
number she needs.

So the mission is a survey, and the campaign that follows it is what the survey proves. The
player is not being asked to find enough crystal. The player is being asked to find out.

**The mission never says this.** Sull's brief (§12) asks for three seams and a figure, in the
Order's register, and the register does not editorialise about its own motives. What the player
carries out of the mission is a number, and the number is the argument.

## 3. Whose Hulls the Player Commands

**The player commands Voice Ren Kalliso's flight: six hulls, identical, and no reinforcements.**

Kalliso is the Order's best field officer and its most ordinary person — a soldier who joined at
nine because she was good at hearing and stayed because she was good at fighting
([characters.md](characters.md)). She is the right officer for this because she is the one who
will report the figure that is there. She is also, three missions from now, the one who loses
nineteen Knights and has to be told the arithmetic out loud; this mission is where the player
first counts to six and finds nothing after it.

| Property | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hull | Corvette ×6, Knight fit | SIG 28 / 28 / +25 · HYD 50 · PR-2 ([units.md](units.md)). Six identical hulls, deliberately: the only variable in this mission is where each one is pointed, and a mixed flight would give the player a second thing to think about |
| Formation | **Three pairs** — a voice and an ear each | §1. Three seams, three pairs, six hulls. The brief is scored for six and there is no seventh part |
| Depth | 1,450–1,800 m throughout | PR-2 covers Mid-Water to 1,800 m ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md)). The Fields continue to 2,900 m and this mission never goes there; depth is felt as unvisited water below, the prologue's own treatment |
| Weapons | Carried, and never used | Below |
| Active sonar | **Withheld** | [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 withholds it until mission 3 of each campaign, and for the Knights it is the sharpest form of that rule: an omnidirectional 95 would erase the mission's entire teaching target with one button press |

**The hulls are named for parts, not numbers.** The flight is **First Voice** through **Sixth
Voice**, paired as First/Second, Third/Fourth, Fifth/Sixth. The Order does not number hulls; it
scores them.

### What "weapons carried, never used" means, exactly

Unlike the prologue's escort flight, this one is armed. Nothing in the mission asks it to fire,
nothing in the water can be usefully shot, and firing is strictly a mistake — a Corvette's
discharge burst puts it at **SIG 53**, and it discharges along the bearing it is aimed down, so a
shot is a sounding the player did not intend, at the target and at everything behind the target.

That is stated as a fact about the flight rather than enforced as a lock. Sorrowgate disabled
weapons because a court order disabled them and the mission was about obeying it
([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §4). Here the guns work, and the reason not to
use them is arithmetic the player can read off their own meter. A mission that teaches facing by
removing the loudest thing a hull can aim would be teaching it with the lesson taken out.

**Engine bound, stated so nobody corrects it into a bug.** The flight carries the Knight faction
value, and so does every array in the water (§5). They are two parties with one faction, which is
legal because a mission seats its own parties and does not go through the lobby — the same
arrangement Sorrowgate uses for the Commune ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §2).
No other player faction is seated. There is no Drift in this mission (§5).

## 4. Facing, as the Only Lever

The system this mission teaches, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2: one system, introduced in the
first three minutes, load-bearing by the last five. The system is **directional SIG**
([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8), and it lands in four movements.

1. **Your SIG meter stopped being the answer.** The number on the HUD is the *on-axis* figure and
   it does not move when the hull turns. Six hulls at a steady 28 are, depending on their
   headings, either invisible or fully classified. The first three minutes exist to break the
   habit the prologue built.
2. **Heading is a choice you make separately from where you are going.** A hull is aimed
   ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8, "what a hull is facing"), and the aim is not the course.
   The flight's default — six bows pointed at the objective — is the single worst configuration
   available to it, and the mission opens by demonstrating that at no cost.
3. **The beam does not stop at the thing you aimed it at.** A sounding passes through the seam and
   keeps going. This is the mission's real puzzle: not *where do I stand* but *what is behind what
   I am pointing at*.
4. **Some geometry has no solution.** The third seam cannot be sounded from any bearing without
   being classified, and no amount of skill changes that (§8). The lesson closes by being a wall
   rather than a puzzle, which is the only honest way to teach that positioning has limits.

### The two budgets

[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 requires a stated SIG budget, and this is the first mission where
one number cannot carry it — a hull's loudness now depends on where the listener is standing. So
the mission states two, and the pair is the format's finding, not a local convenience (§13).

**Emission budget: 80** — the sounding, on-axis, and the loudest number the flight may show. The
mission is playtested against a player who exceeds it, which here means one who fires: 28 → 53 is
the only unbudgeted emission available, and it is available at every moment.

**Exposure budget: Tier 2** — measured, not emitted. No array in the water may hold better than
Tier 2 on any hull. This is the budget that actually governs play, it is already a shipped
readout ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §9, "the resolution runs both ways"), and it is the
right shape for a directional faction because it is stated from the listener's end. A Knight's
loudness is not a property they own. It is a fact about a bearing.

Tier 2 rather than Tier 3 is the whole of §8's failure condition, and §5 says why the Third's ears
care about the difference.

## 5. The Third's Tuning Marks

**Three fixed listening arrays, on the Third Chapter-House's approaches, belonging to
Chapter-Master Halden Vrey's chapter.** They are the mission's only opposition and they are not
an enemy.

| Mark | Position | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **the Fourth** | 900, 2250 | The western approach. The Third's oldest mark |
| **the Fifth** | 3100, 2250 | The eastern approach, over the Sixth Seam's shoulder |
| **the Octave** | 2000, 2750 | The deepest and nearest the house. The one that matters |

Each is a fixed array at **HYD 60** ([units.md](units.md): a base mounts the largest fixed arrays
a faction owns) with a **SIG 35** idle hum, omnidirectional — a mark is an ear, and the hum is
incidental to it rather than aimed. They do not move, do not shoot, and have no orders. A mark
holds no rank in the mission's water at all.

**They are also the reason the mission works without a villain** ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §2).
Vrey is the Order's conservative — a master crystal-wright who believes the First Chord's reply
was a warning and that Sull is about to knock twice ([characters.md](characters.md)). He is
entirely within his rights. Marks on a chapter-house's approaches are what a chapter-house has.
Nobody in this mission is doing anything wrong, and that is exactly the problem: the Ninth is
surveying seams in water the Third listens to, for a plan the Third has not been shown, and the
Order's whole grammar means a thing overheard becomes a thing that must be answered.

### What being heard costs, and why the tier is three

**A mark that resolves the flight to Tier 3 learns the count.** Tier 3 is Classification — unit
type and count estimate ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §4) — and the count is six.

A Chapter-Master who learns that the Ninth put six hulls into his water at one time knows exactly
what fraction of the Order that is, which is the one arithmetic the Knights do not say out loud
([culture.md](culture.md) §3: any suggestion the Order can be replaced is taboo). He will not
attack. He will **hail**, courteously, and ask what the Ninth is doing. And a Knight cannot
decline to answer a courteous question — *it is discourteous not to reply* is the Order's founding
sentence and it does not have an exception for embarrassment.

So the failure state of this mission is not a loss. It is a **conversation**, and it is the one
that becomes *Conclave* four missions later, where Vrey has the standing to call a vote and wants
Sull to hear him not call it ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §7). Mission 1 is where he acquires the
grounds.

Tier 2 — bearing and rough distance — costs nothing. The Fields are full of hulls, the Third
knows the Ninth uses this water, and a smudge on a bearing is not a count. The line is exactly
where the information becomes a number.

## 6. The Three Seams

Each seam is a crystal **face** — a wall, not a point — sounded from in front at up to 400 m
standoff, with the emission aimed square into it. Which side the face opens is authored map data
and it is the whole of each seam's puzzle.

| Seam | Face | Sound it from | The beam then travels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **the Shoulder** | Opens south, along y = 1000 | The water south of it, ~2000, 1300 | **North** — up the Ninth's own road, into empty water |
| **the Sixth** | Opens west, along x = 3000, y 1500 → 2250 | The water west of it, ~2800, 1500 | **East** — across the Fifth's arc and off the map |
| **the Low Sixth** | Opens north, along y = 2000 | The water north of it, ~2000, 1700 | **South** — down the Octave's throat |

Read the fourth column as the mission. The seams are sounded in the brief's order, and the order
is a teaching sequence: one that works, one that works only under a condition, and one that does
not work at all.

### The Shoulder — the lesson, free

Sounded from 2000, 1300 on a north heading, the flight is **Tier 0 to all three marks**. The
Octave is 1,450 m away and dead astern at ×0.15, where Tier-1 contact reaches 1,405 m.

**The first sounding of the survey is inaudible to the Third by forty-five metres, and nobody in
the flight knows it.** That margin is authored and it is the mission's opening statement: this
worked because of where a bow was pointed, and the player was not told, and it was never close to
a matter of loudness. SIG 80 — the loudest number in the mission — went entirely unheard.

Turn the same pair around at the same spot and the Octave holds Tier 4.

### The Sixth — the lesson, conditional

The face runs 750 m north to south and the Fifth stands 300 m off its southern end, so the
standoff is a slider and the player will reach for it first. It does not work anywhere on the
face. Sounded dry from the northern end — 2800, 1500, the best point available — the Fifth sits
808 m away at 68° off the beam, a ×0.253 multiplier, and **Tier 4 reaches 819 m**. Full track, by
eleven metres, from the best spot on the face. Slide south and the angle closes faster than the
range opens, so it worsens the whole way down; slide north and the face runs out.

The answer is not geometry alone. It is **weather**. A resonance storm site sits over the seam's
southern half ([hazards.md](hazards.md) §5), and inside its plume PF falls to 0.70 × 0.35 =
**0.245**. The same sounding from the same spot on the same heading is then **Tier 1** — a
directionless smudge, inside §4's Tier-2 budget, and clear of Tier 2 by twenty-four metres.

The storm does not make the face free either. Sounded from the middle of it, at 2800, 1750, the
plume still leaves the Fifth at Tier 3. **The Sixth needs both answers at once** — the weather,
and the head of the face — which is why it is the second seam and not the first.

Two things make this the mission's hinge rather than a free pass:

- **The window is exact.** A storm's active phase is 30 s and a sounding is 30 s
  ([hazards.md](hazards.md) §5). They are the same length and that is not a coincidence in the
  fiction either — the Order times soundings to storms because that is what a survey in the
  Fields has always meant. A player who begins a second late finishes a second in the clear.
- **The storm bites.** 12 damage per second, and a Corvette has 420 hull. The **voice** stands in
  the plume for the full thirty seconds and comes out at roughly 60 — a hull that is not lost and
  is not whole. The **ear** does not have to be in the plume at all, because only the path from
  the beam to the mark needs degrading. A player who parks the pair as a blob burns two hulls
  where the mission costs one, and nothing tells them; the difference is the same lesson as the
  rest of the mission, which is that a pair is two positions.

### The Low Sixth — the lesson, refused

Sounded from 2000, 1700 on a south heading, the beam goes into the Octave at 1,050 m, dead
on-axis, ×1.000. That is **Tier 4** — full track, exact hull, health, facing.

The storm does not save it. At PF 0.245 an on-axis SIG 80 still classifies at 1,345 m, and the
Octave is at 1,050. **Tier 3 inside the storm**, which is the count, which is the hail.

There is no bearing that solves it, and no standoff either: slide 250 m either way along the
face and the Octave is still inside Tier 3 in the plume, because moving sideways barely changes a
range of a kilometre and the beam still points down the slope. The face opens north, which fixes
the beam pointing south, and the Third's deepest ear is what is standing south of it. The flight
can stand where it likes.

This is not a puzzle the player is failing to solve. **It is the mission's answer**, and §8 is
where the player is asked what to do about it.

## 7. What the Flight Can Hear

The mission is a listening mission played by the loudest faction, and the player's own ears are
worth stating because they are what makes the third seam a decision rather than a surprise.

A mark's idle hum is **SIG 35, omnidirectional**. Against a Corvette's HYD 50 through PF 0.70, a
mark resolves to Tier 1 at 2,447 m and Tier 3 at 1,380 m: **the flight can hear all three marks
from most of the map, and can classify them from the working ground.** The marks are not hidden
and were never meant to be. The Third does not conceal its ears; concealing them would be
discourteous.

Inside a storm this inverts twice over, and both halves are shipped mechanics rather than mission
authoring. PF 0.245 shortens everything — a mark falls to Tier 1 at 1,270 m. But Knight hulls
gain **+25 HYD** in a resonance storm ([hazards.md](hazards.md) §5, the faction interaction), so
a Corvette listens at 75 and recovers to Tier 1 at 1,636 m and Tier 3 at 923 m.

Read that pair together and the storm is the doctrine in one hazard: **inside it the flight hears
better than it did and is heard worse than it was.** It is also eating them at 12 a second. The
Knights' relationship to noise, handed over as weather, in the mission that teaches it.

Nothing else is in the water. No Drift — the Fields at 1,450–1,800 m sit above the Sounder's
working depth and outside every band in [bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §4's table, and no creature is
authored. This mission's opposition is three arrays that do not move, and it does not need
another.

## 8. The Objective

**Sound three seams and bring back a figure.**

Two of them can be sounded without being counted. The third cannot. That is not concealed from
the player and it is not discovered as a failure: at **13:00**, with the Shoulder and the Sixth
in hand, Kalliso puts the arithmetic on the channel herself (§12) — the figure is short without
the Low Sixth, the Low Sixth cannot be sounded quietly, and the flight is already standing 300 m
from the face where that is true.

The player then chooses, with both costs stated:

### Results

| Result | Condition | The Ninth's reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In tune** | The Shoulder and the Sixth sounded; no mark above Tier 2; the flight withdraws north of 900 m | "Two seams, entered. The third is standing and unmeasured, and the Order was not heard measuring it. The figure is short by the amount the Order chose not to spend. That is a figure." |
| **The whole count** | All three sounded; a mark holds Tier 3 and the Third hails | "Three seams, entered. The Third has asked what the Ninth is doing in its water, and the Ninth has answered, because the alternative was discourtesy. The figure is complete. So is the Third's." |
| **A short measure** | Fewer than two seams sounded, however the mission ends | "One seam, entered. The remainder is unmeasured. The Order does not guess at tonnage; it will be sounded again, by somebody, at a date the cycle does not offer." |

**All three are endings.** [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2's fourth rule says losing is content, and
this is the mission where the campaign first means it: the reading that gets the whole number is
the reading that costs the Order its quiet, and the reading that keeps the quiet hands Sull a
figure with a hole in it. Neither is the correct answer, and the campaign is seven missions of
finding out that there was not one. The player is not asked to replay it.

The third reading is not a punishment. It is the Order's actual condition — six hulls, one
window, and a number nobody has time to go back for.

### The failure, and the sounds that precede it

There is **one failure state and it is not a timer**: a mark holding **Tier 3 continuously for
sixty seconds**. Not an instant, not a peak — sixty unbroken seconds of a chapter-house's array
holding a count.

That is [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's sixty-second rule satisfied by the failure condition
itself rather than by a warning bolted to it. The fuse *is* the sixty seconds, it is the player's
own exposure readout, and it is stoppable at any point inside it by turning a hull.

What the player hears while it runs is the Fields themselves:

| When | What the player hears |
| --- | --- |
| 00:00 | **The ring.** Crystal country sounding faintly and continuously under everything, from the first tick to the last ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). Unresolved, unpitched, the room's own noise |
| On first Tier 3 | **The ring narrows.** The ambient chime begins pulling toward a pitch — the Third's array tuning down onto something it half-hears. The sound is the mark converging, and it starts the moment the count does |
| +40 s | **Unison.** The narrowing arrives on the flight's own hull tone. Twenty seconds of the Fields playing the player's note back at them |
| +60 s | **The hail.** Vrey's chapter, courteous, on an open channel. The survey ends where it stands |

The ring releases the moment the count breaks. A player who turns a hull hears the Fields go
back to being a haunted room, and that is the mechanic's whole feedback loop delivered without a
number.

**Length: twenty minutes.** Inside [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's 12–25, and the long half of
it: this is a survey, not a raid. Most of the twenty minutes is holding position correctly and
waiting for weather.

**Emission budget: 80. Exposure budget: Tier 2.** §4.

The ring is already in the water. **Nothing hears them** — at 2,500 m even a dead-on-axis cruise is under the Octave's threshold, so the opening position is silent by distance rather than by skill, and the player is given no reason to think about heading yet |

| 10:30 | The Sixth's figure. Better than the Shoulder's, and both together are not enough. Nobody says so yet |

A Corvette does 85 m/s and the map is 4,000 × 3,000 ([units.md](units.md)), so **transit is never
what costs this flight time** — the longest leg on the table is under a minute of travel. The gaps
in the schedule are station-keeping and weather. That is deliberate and it is the shape of the
lesson: a mission about facing must not be a mission about arriving, because a player who is
moving is a player whose heading is being chosen for them by the pathing.

The mission has one hazard, one clock and no chase. What it spends twenty minutes on is six
hulls, three bearings and a decision, which is the correct proportion for a mission whose whole
subject is where things are pointed.

## 10. What It Teaches

One system, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10: **directional SIG** — the loudness you carry is a
fact about a bearing, and heading is the lever. §4's four movements land in order across the beat
table: the meter is not the answer (00:40), heading is a separate choice (00:40 and the whole
transit), the beam does not stop at its target (03:00 and 05:30), and some geometry has no
solution (13:00).

What this mission deliberately does not teach:

- **Standing Wave, nodes and PF 2.0** — mission 2, whose whole subject is bending propagation on
  purpose. This mission *shows* the Fields' own PF being bent, by a storm, on the map's published
  interval, and never asks the player to operate it. Shown before operated, exactly as the ping
  was shown at Sorrowgate before it is handed over in mission 3.
- **Permanent loss** — mission 3, *Nineteen*, where every hull spent is spent for the rest of the
  campaign ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §7). Aptitude must make six feel finite **without spending
  any of it**, or the two missions teach the same lesson and the second one is a repeat. It does
  that by arithmetic instead of attrition: three seams need three pairs, three pairs need six
  hulls, and the flight is committed the moment it splits at 02:00 with nothing held back. The
  storm's 360 damage is the closest the mission comes, and it is deliberately a wound rather than
  a death — a hull at 60 of 420 that the mission never asks to fight is the Knights' condition
  rendered exactly: nothing lost, nothing whole, and no way to replace either.
- **Active sonar** — mission 3, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10, and the withholding is doing
  more work here than in any other campaign. A directional faction's ping is the one emission
  they own that is not aimed. Handing it over before this mission would mean handing the player a
  button that deletes the doctrine.
- **Depth** — everything sits between 1,450 and 1,800 m and the flight is rated for all of it.
  The Fields continue to 2,900 m, the First Chapter-House and its three mute technicians are down
  there, and mission 5 goes; this mission leaves the water below as water below.

### The title

Entry to the Order is by acoustic aptitude testing at age nine, and those who fail return home
([factions.md](factions.md)). It is the only faction in the Rift that cannot grow, and the test
is the reason: the Order does not choose its members, it *finds* them, and it finds too few.

The mission is named for that test twice over. Kalliso passed it at nine and it is the whole
reason she is in the water at all (§3). And the mission's own examination is the same
examination — the player is asked what they can hear, and where they are standing, and it is
graded, and the grade is a number that is either enough or is not. The Order has been running
this test on nine-year-olds for a century and it has never once returned enough.

The brief calls the mission an examination in as many words (§12), which is Sull being courteous
about the fact that the Order cannot afford to conduct a survey and a grading separately. It has
six hulls. It does both at once, and calls it the first.

## 11. The Map

`resonance-sixth-seam` · **The Sixth Seam** · one seat · 4,000 × 3,000 m · cell 250 m · base
floor 1,500 m.

Resonance Fields ground, sloping from the Ninth's road in the north to the Third's approaches in
the south. North is shallow and south is deep, as everywhere in the Rift
([world-map.md](world-map.md)); the mission is a descent that never becomes one, because the
Order's business stops at 1,800 m and the Third's house is below it.

One spawn, on the Ninth's Road: **2000, 250**. **No resources** — a survey, not a works order.
One hazard site: a **resonance storm at 2950, 1875, radius 700 m**, simulated
([hazards.md](hazards.md) §5). Its position is authored against the arithmetic rather than
sprinkled: it stands 404 m from both the Sixth's working standoff and the Fifth, so the whole
emitter-to-mark path lies inside the plume rather than crossing its edge — PF is integrated along
the path ([glossary.md](glossary.md)), and a plume the path only clips returns a blended factor
that §6's numbers do not describe. It reaches neither the Shoulder's standoff (1,110 m away) nor
the Low Sixth's (966 m), so no other seam gets weather. Visible from the first frame, per the
telegraphing principle ([maps.md](maps.md)).

Every rectangle lands on the 250 m cell grid and paints exactly the metres it reads
([maps.md](maps.md), "How a map is written"). The map is one biome throughout, which is unusual
and is the point: nothing on this map changes what sound does except the storm and the direction
a hull is pointed. Asset Recovery's argument was made in biome borders; this one is made in
bearings, and a second PF landscape would give the player somewhere else to look.

The three marks (§5) and the three seam faces (§6) are placed by the mission, not by the map.
**The Sixth Seam is a mission map and is not in the public catalogue** — one seat, no resources,
not balanced, resolved by mission id and nothing else ([maps.md](maps.md)).

## 12. The Briefing

Sull's brief, read to the flight at 00:00 from the Ninth Chapter-House. The Knights' register is
defined in [culture.md](culture.md) §3: precise, courteous, slightly archaic, and using musical
and mathematical metaphor literally rather than poetically.

> Voice Kalliso. The Order has three seams standing between this house and the Third, and no
> current figure for any of them. You will sound them and bring back what is there.
>
> The Shoulder first, then the Sixth, then the Low Sixth. Sound each in its order and do not
> strike two at once; a chord that nobody scored is not a chord.
>
> Six hulls are assigned. The passage is scored for six, and a piece is not improved by adding
> voices to it. Three faces, three pairs, a voice and an ear at each. You will find the
> arrangement sufficient because it is the arrangement.
>
> The Third keeps marks on its approaches, as a chapter-house does. They are the Order's own ears
> and you will treat them as such. You are not asked to deceive them and you could not; you are
> asked to be quiet in their direction, which is a different courtesy and the older one.
>
> Consider this an examination. You sat one at nine and were found in tune. Sit this one and
> bring me the number that is there, not the number I would prefer. I will know the difference,
> and so will you.
>
> In tune, Voice. Sull, Ninth.

### Objective readings, in play

Knight objective text is courteous, precise, and never imperative in the bare sense — the Order
asks, and the asking is not softer for it ([culture.md](culture.md) §3).

- *Sound the Shoulder. Thirty seconds, and mind where the note goes after.*
- *Sound the Sixth. The Fifth stands off its southern end and is listening. The weather is the
  interval you have, and the head of the face is where to spend it.*
- *The Low Sixth stands unmeasured. The Octave stands behind it. Both of those are true at once.*
- *Return to the Ninth's road. The survey closes at the figure.*

### The voices in the water

**Voice Ren Kalliso, on the flight's opening heading — 00:40**

> Six bows on the objective and every one of them speaking to the Third. Turn them. We are going
> south; we are not *saying* south.

**Kalliso, after the Shoulder — 04:00**

> Eighty, for thirty seconds, and the Third heard nothing at all. Remember that it was not
> quietness. It was a heading.

**Kalliso, at the Sixth's dry attempt — 05:30**

> Break it. That is the Fifth counting us, and it is very good at counting — it will count us
> from anywhere on that face. Take the head of it, and wait for the water to help.

**Kalliso, at 13:00 — the question**

> The two seams give the Choirmaster a figure and the figure is short. The Low Sixth would close
> it. The Low Sixth opens north and the Octave is standing directly behind it, so there is no
> heading that measures it quietly — I have looked, and there is not one.
>
> So it is a courtesy against a number. Sound it and the Third asks the Ninth what it is doing
> here, and the Ninth will have to answer, because we do not decline questions. Leave it and the
> Choirmaster gets an honest figure with a hole in it.
>
> I am not going to tell you which. She asked for the number that is there. Both of those are
> the number that is there.

**Chapter-Master Halden Vrey, on the hail — the *whole count* reading only**

> Ninth. You have six hulls in my water and you have been sounding the Low Sixth for a minute.
>
> I am not going to ask you to stop, and I am not going to ask you why. I am going to record that
> I heard it, and that I hailed, and that you replied — because you will reply, and that is the
> only part of this the Order still agrees on.
>
> In tune, Voice. Take them home.

Each line fails [culture.md](culture.md) §3 for the other three factions, which is that
document's own test (§6): Kalliso's first is an order given as a correction of *diction*, which
no other faction would reach for; her last refuses to recommend while stating both costs exactly,
where the Consortium would price one of them and the Commune could not close the question at all;
and Vrey's hail is a serious accusation delivered entirely as courtesy, which is a move only a
culture whose worst insult is *discourteous* can make.

## 13. Scaffold Status

What exists against this document and what does not, continuing the list
[mission-asset-recovery.md](mission-asset-recovery.md) §13 started and
[mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) and [mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md) §13
extended — the format finding out, one mission at a time, what it was missing.

**This is the first of the five whose central mechanic is unbuilt**, and the row is at the top
because the rest of the table is downstream of it.

| Requirement | Status |
| --- | --- |
| **Directional SIG — the term itself** | **Not built.** Specified for the first time by this issue's work, in [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8: a curve, floor ×0.15, half at ±45°, exponent derived from those two. SIG is scalar everywhere in the simulation — `echoLayer.ts` reads one number per emitter — and the term is a property of the *pair*, so it lands inside the candidate loop rather than hoisting out of it. The broadphase gets no discount: `maxAudibleRangeM` must bound on the loudest lobe. A strict cost to the 2 ms budget, never a saving |
| **A hull's facing** | **Not built, and the harder half.** The spec'd answer is an explicit facing order ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8) — a new order kind, the wire, the HUD and the AI commander. Today the server writes a literal `heading: 0` on every unit it sends, the client re-derives its own hulls' facing from position deltas, and the only bearing the server computes is inside a Tier-4 payload *after* detection resolves. Nothing upstream of the Echo pass has a facing to read. **This mission is not implementable until it does**, and that is a 60 Hz-path decision rather than a mission's |
| **The two budgets — emission and exposure** (§4) | **The format's finding from this mission.** Every previous mission stated one SIG number; a directional faction cannot, because loudness is a fact about a bearing. Exposure is already resolved and already on the wire ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §9), so the *readout* is built; what is not is a mission stating a bound on it and a predicate measuring it |
| **The failure predicate — a mark holding Tier 3 for sixty continuous seconds** | **Not built.** Closest shipped shape is Attendance's `attend` predicate — a tally over an observer's own resolution of an authored emitter. This is the same idea inverted: a *dwell* over a non-player array's resolution of the player, which means the runtime must ask what somebody other than the player resolved. That is legal on the server and has no wire consequence, but it is the first predicate to need it, and it must never reach the client |
| **The sounding — a directional emission at an authored SIG for an authored duration** | **Not built.** It is the lift ([mission-asset-recovery.md](mission-asset-recovery.md) §13) with a bearing: hold inside the authored region, aimed into the face, at 80 for 30 s, then a figure. A table row, not a language. It needs the facing model above and nothing else |
| **The pair — a voice and an ear, where the emitter is deaf for the duration** | **Not built.** A hull whose HYD is suppressed while it emits. [units.md](units.md)'s design note says HYD is a flat hull property and that anything modifying listening does so as an explicit HYD modifier, which is exactly the shape available: the sounding sets one for its own duration. Stated as a rule rather than assumed, because the note also says Silent Running must *not* touch HYD, and this is the first thing that legitimately does |
| The map, its six regions, one biome throughout | **Built** in shape — `resonance-sixth-seam`, one row of the literal per row of §11's table, in the mission registry beside Sorrowgate's and Face Six's. The mission definition is `second-chord-aptitude` |
| Resonance storms — full cycle, PF ×0.35, 12 damage/s, the Knights' +25 HYD | **Built** ([hazards.md](hazards.md) §5, three of four faction interactions). Every number §6 and §7 spend is the shipped one, unmodified. The 30 s active phase and the 30 s sounding are congruent by authorship, and if `STORM.ACTIVE_S` ever moves, this mission's hinge moves with it — the sounding duration is the dependent number, not the constant |
| The mission format — beats, predicates, registry, private rooms | **Built** (#190). `say`, `objective`, `hazard` and `resolve` cover most of §9's schedule; the two soundings and the failure dwell are the rows above |
| Fixed listening arrays as a non-player party | **Partly.** A mark is a structure with a HYD and a hum and no orders, which the roster can express ([units.md](units.md)); what is missing is a mission seating structures for a party that is neither the player nor fauna. Sorrowgate seats non-player *hulls* ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §5), so the standing exists and only the immobile case is untried |
| The Drift, absent | **Built** — `fauna: false`, as Attendance uses it. This is the second mission to author none |
| **The ring narrowing to unison** — the sixty-second telegraph as a pitch, not a cue | **Not started** ([audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md)). §8's telegraph is a *tuning*, which the mix has no vocabulary for yet: the Fields' ambient chime converging on the flight's own hull tone as a mark's resolution holds. The mission reads correctly as text with an exposure meter, and reads *right* only with the mix — the same status Attendance's dreams carry |
| In-mission character speech, heard | Text only, the standing status ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §13) |
| **Whether the flank floor is 0.15 or 0.10** | **Decided, and reversible.** 0.15 is authored against [audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md) §8's −20 dB read as an amplitude ratio (×0.1), placed slightly above it. Every margin in §6 is small on purpose — 45 m, 11 m, 24 m — so they are the right instrument for measuring whether the floor is correct, and the wrong thing to protect if it is not. Move the floor and re-derive these; do not tune the map to keep the margins |
