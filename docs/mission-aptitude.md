# The Second Chord 1 — Aptitude

> The first mission of the Knight campaign ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §7), specified. The fifth
> mission document the bible carries, to the pattern [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md)
> sets: everything here is authored — the forces, the water, the beats, the numbers and the text —
> and code transcribes this document. With it every campaign's first mission is a document of
> record.

**Setting:** the Third Chapter-House's outer formations, the shallow Resonance Fields on the east
slope, 214 PC ([world-map.md](world-map.md); [characters.md](characters.md)).

**Mission id:** `chord-aptitude` — namespaced by campaign, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §1.

**One thing this document does not own.** The doctrine it is built on — directional signature, the
quartered circle, ×1.00 / ×0.35 / ×0.10 — is spec'd in [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8 and
summarised in [factions.md](factions.md), because it is a faction-wide term and the next four
Knight missions each need it. Pinning it inside one mission's design notes would have left the
other six to restate it slightly differently. This document *consumes* those numbers and is the
first place they are worked against ground.

---

## 1. What a Tuning Is

Nine chapter-houses, each **built into a resonance formation, each a physical instrument tuned over
generations** ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). That sentence is already canon and this mission does
not invent it; what it does is take it literally. An instrument that is tuned over generations is
an instrument somebody tunes, on some particular afternoon, and this is the afternoon.

The **Third Chapter-House** stands at 1,450 m: the oldest voice of caution, Chapter-Master Halden
Vrey's seat, and the shallowest house the bible names — the one whose ordinary work still happens
in Mid-Water ([world-map.md](world-map.md); [characters.md](characters.md)). The house is not the
whole instrument. Six outer formations stand off it, and the lattice the chapter sings against is
the seven of them together. Once a year the outer six are **sounded** — a held tone played at each
formation and the crystal's reply read — and the drift between what came back and what should have
is what the chapter-wrights spend the following year correcting.

**The mission's whole job is that this is an examination, and that everybody in it knows so.**

Entry to the Order is by **acoustic aptitude testing at age nine**, and those who fail return home
([factions.md](factions.md); [timeline.md](timeline.md), 118 PC). The test is not a metaphor and it
is not off-screen: a child is put in front of a formation, a tone is played, and the child says what
came back. A tuning party does the identical thing with a hull instead of a pair of ears. Vrey has
run the children's testing at the Third for thirty years, and he runs this the same way, in the same
words, which is the mission's whole register in one production decision.

Three facts about this water decide the mission, and none of them is a fence.

**The Fields have no cover.** Crystal country is PF 0.70 and *scattered* — bearings lie, pings
return phantoms — but 0.70 is uniform, and there is no vent, no kelp, no ruin and no thermocline
crossing anywhere on this map ([environments.md](environments.md);
[world-map.md](world-map.md)). Every other faction's first mission hands the player somewhere to be
quiet. **This one hands the player a direction to be quiet in and nothing else.**

**The Order cannot be quiet.** Knight emissions are high and directional; the quiet is *the flank*,
and the flank is not a mode, it is an angle ([factions.md](factions.md);
[systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8). Silent Running exists, is available, and is the wrong tool
here for a reason §4 states in numbers rather than in doctrine.

**The Order cannot be replaced.** Perhaps thirty thousand souls, the least populous power by an
order of magnitude, entry closed to anybody who did not pass at nine
([factions.md](factions.md)). Six hulls is not a scenario restriction dressed as a story. It is
the number a chapter can put in the water.

## 2. Whose Hulls the Player Commands

**The player commands the Third's annual tuning party: six hulls under Voice Ren Kalliso, seconded
from the Resonance Fields command because a Voice is what the Order sends when it wants no
incident.**

Kalliso is thirty-one, the Order's best field officer and its most ordinary person — a soldier, not
a mystic, who joined at nine because she was good at hearing and stayed because she was good at
fighting ([characters.md](characters.md)). She is three years past the 211 PC crystal raids, in
which her chapter's entire junior cadre — nineteen Knights — was lost and will not be replaced. The
campaign spends that in mission 3 ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §7). **This mission does not spend it
and must not**, which is a constraint on the writing as much as on the rules: Kalliso does not
mention the nineteen, nobody mentions them to her, and the arithmetic of six is allowed to be felt
without being explained.

Chapter-Master Vrey is on the chapter channel for the whole mission, from the house. He does not
command the party — a Voice in the field outranks a Chapter-Master's opinion about the field, which
is a distinction the Order is precise about and nobody says aloud. He sets the exercise, he reads
the chord at the end, and in between he says four things.

**Engine bound, stated so nobody corrects it into a bug.** The party carries the Knight faction
value. One other player-faction party is seated — a Consortium survey, §5 — and it is a *party*,
with its own hulls and its own standing, placed by the mission rather than by the map
([maps.md](maps.md)). The Drift is not populated: no fauna are seeded and none is authored.

## 3. The Party

| Hull | Count | Stats — **cone / flank / wake** | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cruiser hull, Knight-rigged — *the Voice* | 1 | SIG **55 idle / 65 live** in the cone · 19 / 23 flank · 5.5 / 6.5 wake · HYD 65 · PR-2 ([units.md](units.md)) | Kalliso's hull and the party's ears. HYD 65 is what classifies the survey from 2,157 m, which is the margin the whole mission is spent out of (§4) |
| Corvette hull, Knight-rigged | 5 | SIG **28 cruise** in the cone · 9.8 flank · 2.8 wake · HYD 50 · +25 firing burst · PR-2 | The working hulls. Five of them and six formations, which is the arithmetic the party is short by exactly one of |

**These are generic hulls flying Knight colours, and the roster is the reason.**
[units.md](units.md) has no Knight entry — the faction's hulls have never been written — so this
mission fields the roster it has and applies the doctrine to it. The consequence is stated rather
than hidden: a real Knight hull's cone figure is expected to run about 2.2× a comparable hull's, so
that the compass average lands at parity ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8), and the party
above is therefore *quieter in the cone* than the faction should be. Nothing in the mission's
geometry depends on it being 28 rather than 60; every distance below is quoted from the model, and
a louder roster moves them all outward together.

### What the party does not carry

1. **No active sonar**, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's mission-3 rule — and for this faction
   the rule is almost redundant, because the button is doctrinally incoherent. §5 of
   [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) fixes the ping at SIG 95 **omnidirectional**, and
   §8 exempts it from the directional term for that reason. **The ping is the one emission the
   Order owns that has no bow.** A faction whose entire argument is that loudness has a direction
   would have to call it *discourteous*, which is a serious charge from a Knight
   ([culture.md](culture.md) §3). Withholding it until mission 3 costs this mission nothing it
   wanted.
2. **No base, no economy, nothing built.** The Knight economy is crystal-gated and the thinnest in
   the game ([factions.md](factions.md)); a tuning party produces nothing and spends nothing. The
   map carries no resource fields and the build pages are not offered — the pattern
   [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §3 set, with its reason attached: *a tuning is
   maintenance, and maintenance is not a works order*.
3. **No Standing Wave, no Sounding Spire.** Paired nodes and PF 2.0 corridors are mission 2's whole
   system ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §7), and a Spire's projected PR would be a second lesson in a
   mission that has one. The party's own soundings borrow the Spire's *loudness* — 80, its active
   figure ([units.md](units.md)) — and nothing else about it.
4. **Silent Running is present, and §4 prices it out rather than fencing it off.** The button is
   not removed, is never diegetically struck, and is the wrong tool by arithmetic the player can
   check.

**Nothing in this mission hunts the player.** The survey has an escort and the escort screens a
barge; neither goes looking. What can be lost is the tuning, and — only if the player insists on it
after the mission has already told them to leave — hulls.

## 4. Facing, as the Only Cover

The system this mission teaches, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2: one system, introduced in the
first three minutes and load-bearing by the last five. The system is **directional signature** —
the quartered circle of [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8 — and it lands in four movements,
each of which is a number rather than a doctrine.

**1. Facing is worth a factor of four in distance.** Against the survey's best ears — a Consortium
Cruiser at HYD 65, in PF 0.70 water — a Knight corvette at cruise is classified at:

| Where the listener stands | Effective SIG | Contact | Bearing | **Classification** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bow-on** — inside the cone | 28.0 | 2,508 m | 1,946 m | **1,414 m** |
| **Beam-on** — on the flank | 9.8 | 1,301 m | 1,010 m | **734 m** |
| **Stern-on** — in the wake | 2.8 | 595 m | 462 m | **335 m** |

Nothing about the hull changed. The player has one lever and it is worth 1,079 metres.

**2. The rule is two numbers, and a player can hold two numbers.** *Fourteen hundred on the bow,
seven-fifty on the beam.* Everything the mission asks is a consequence of those, including the one
manoeuvre it is really about: **you may go west, and you may not point west.** A hull that needs to
cross the map toward a listener does it in alternating legs, north-west then south-west, so the
listener spends the whole passage on one flank or the other and never in the cone. The Order's word
for two chords in succession is a **cadence**, and Vrey uses it for this because to a Knight it is
not a metaphor for the manoeuvre, it *is* the manoeuvre, one bearing resolving into the next.

**3. The sounding aims you, and that is the mission.** A sounding is taken from within 400 m of a
formation, bow on it, held for **twenty seconds at SIG 80** — the Sounding Spire's active figure
([units.md](units.md)), because a hull taking a sounding is doing the Spire's job by hand. Two of
its three sectors land on numbers the player already knows, exactly:

- **80 × 0.35 = 28.0.** A sounding's flank is a corvette at cruise.
- **80 × 0.10 = 8.0.** A sounding's wake is a hull running silent — the top of §6's 3–8 band.

Against the same HYD-65 listener, that reads 2,726 m to classification in the cone, 1,414 m on the
flank, and 646 m in the wake. **A sounding aimed at the survey is heard from most of the map; the
same sounding aimed away is heard from six hundred metres.** So the question a formation asks is
never *can I reach it* — every one of them is reachable — it is *what is behind it*, because
whatever is behind it is in the cone for twenty seconds.

**4. And the arithmetic that closes the argument.** A Knight hull running Silent Running emits 8,
which is classified **bow-on at 646 m**. A Knight hull at cruise, showing its wake, is classified at
**335 m**.

> **A cruising Knight pointed away is quieter than a silent Knight pointed at you — by three
> hundred and eleven metres, with its weapons live and at full speed.**

Silent Running costs the Order −45% speed and its guns to buy a worse number than turning around
buys for free. The button is not fenced, not struck, and not discussed; it is simply a bad trade
for one faction, and the mission is playtested against a player who tries it once, because every
stealth instinct the genre teaches points that way. **The Order does not run silent. It turns.**

Sorrowgate taught that hearing is worth everything; Asset Recovery aims loudness at fauna; Tend
spends a day being quiet; Attendance spends one not acting. Aptitude is the same detection maths a
fifth time, and its verb is *bearing* ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §2, rule 2).

### The SIG budget, aimed

**SIG budget: 28 — in the cone**, and the qualifier is the point. This is the fourth reading the
campaign has given the same rule and the first that could not be stated without a direction
attached: the prologue's twenty is a ceiling, Asset Recovery's sixty-five is a working level,
Attendance's eight is a description, and this one is **a figure that is meaningless until you say
where the listener is standing**. Twenty-eight is what a corvette at cruise puts down its own bow.
The same hull is emitting 2.8 astern in the same instant, and both are true.

**Which is why the meter this mission asks the player to watch is not the SIG meter.** A single
scalar cannot state a Knight's loudness, and [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §9 already ships
the number that can: **exposure** — the best tier anybody currently holds on your force, resolved
server-side and sent to you because it is a fact about your own hulls. It is on the wire today and
it is the instrument this mission is played on. §13 carries what the HUD still owes it.

It is playtested against a player who exceeds the budget, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10,
because most will — by pointing a sounding the short way, by crossing the picket bow-first, by
taking the Seam because it is the short way round. Exceeding it costs no hull. It is *heard*, and
what hears it is a survey that has been told twice this year that its instruments are faulty.

## 5. The Tolerance

The other party in the water, and the mission's ceiling — which is the first in the bible that is
not a number the player emits.

**A Consortium survey is coring the outer Fields under a Board writ.** Ninefold Vein is dying and
the concern has eleven years ([factions.md](factions.md)); Baen Tull's model needs field data, and
resonant crystal is the one commodity in the Rift the Consortium cannot make and cannot buy. The
survey's position, in its own register and therefore correctly: the outer Fields are unsurveyed,
and **a claim nobody has filed is not a claim**. The Order's title to this water is liturgical,
generational, and has never once been written down in a form the Consortium's filing system
recognises. Both parties are right inside their own frame, which is
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 1 doing its job before the campaign has properly started.

| Hull | Count | Stats | Standing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Survey barge — *coring* | 1 | SIG **55 sustained** · HYD 30 · PR-2 | Deaf and loud, on the [economy.md](economy.md) noise curve. It is the reason the party can hear the survey from four kilometres |
| Cruiser — the escort | 1 | SIG 55 idle / 65 live · **HYD 65** · PR-2 | The survey's ears, and the listener every distance in §4 is quoted against |
| Corvette — the picket | 2 | SIG 28 · HYD 50 · PR-2 | Two hulls walking two legs, §11 |

**The tolerance: thirty seconds, cumulative, at Classification or better, across the whole party.**

It is not invented for the mission. It is the Consortium's own documented reflex: a survey team on
the Mouth's rim has filed attendant organisms as **equipment fault, twice**
([timeline.md](timeline.md); [bestiary.md](bestiary.md)). An instrument that says something
implausible is an instrument, until it says it long enough. So:

- **Tier 1 and Tier 2 are free.** A smudge is weather. A bearing is a fish. The survey hearing
  *something* costs the party nothing, all mission, which is what keeps this from being a stealth
  mission with a binary fail and keeps it inside §4's target emotion — partial information a player
  can reason about.
- **Tier 3 is an entry.** Classification reports unit *type and count*
  ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §4). At that tier the survey is no longer hearing a noise; it
  is looking at two Knight hulls on a plot, and that is a sentence a writ obliges somebody to file.
- **Thirty seconds is the threshold of belief**, and it is cumulative rather than continuous
  because the Consortium's procedure is cumulative: the log is added up at the end of a shift, not
  watched.

**What exhausting it does, and what it does not.** At thirty seconds Surveyor Ade Bramm files a
contact rather than a fault, the escort moves to interpose between the survey and the last bearing
it held, and the Third recalls the party. From that moment the mission is an extraction: soundings
not taken are not taken, and the chord is read as it stands. **It is not a mission failure and the
epilogue does not treat it as one** — a partial outcome is an outcome
([glossary.md](glossary.md), *Mission Outcome*), and [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 4 says
three of the twenty-nine are winnable only as retreats. This is the miniature of that, in the first
mission of the campaign whose ending spends the Order.

**Nothing about it is hidden from the player.** The exposure readout carries the tier from the first
tick, and at **twenty seconds — ten short of the tolerance — the barge stops coring.** SIG 55 goes
to idle, and in this game a thing going quiet is the loudest event available. That is the warning,
it is the survey's own procedure rather than a rule bolted onto the close, and what follows it is a
Consortium column out of the Holding that is audible for four minutes before it arrives
([factions.md](factions.md), the Klaxon's own stated weakness). [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 asks
for sixty seconds. The survey gives ten, and then two hundred and forty.

## 6. The Six Voices

The Third's outer lattice is a **chord of seven**: the house and its six outer formations. The six
are named for the voices they carry, which is what a chapter calls them and not a decoration this
document added — the Order plays the interval a person's name resolves to at their committal
([culture.md](culture.md) §5), and it names ground the same way.

A formation is sounded from within **400 m**, bow on it, held **twenty seconds at SIG 80**. Resolve
that and the voice is **sounded** and the reply is entered against the chapter's lattice. Break it
early and nothing is entered; the drift is not half-measured, it is unmeasured.

| Voice | On the map | To the survey | What it asks of the party |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Descant** | 4,300, 1,500 | 2,750 m | Nothing. Face south, hold twenty seconds. The sounding, taught by having it, with 600 m of margin on the flank if the player faces it the obvious way |
| **Tenor** | 3,200, 600 | 2,130 m | North of the picket's top leg. Reachable, and **not on a westward bearing** — the first formation whose *route* is the problem rather than its aim |
| **Treble** | 3,600, 3,100 | 2,280 m | 400 m north of the Seam. Aiming it south puts the cone into PF 1.60 water, where a sounding's wake alone still classifies at 1,084 m. **The ground is a term too** |
| **Alto** | 2,300, 3,300 | 1,480 m | Inside the south picket's leg. A timing problem rather than a geometry one: the gate breathes on a two-minute cycle (§11) and this is the first voice that has to be taken in a window |
| **Bass** | 900, 1,100 | 1,140 m | Past everything, north-west. **The passage**, and the cadence that makes it: two bearings alternating, never one aimed |
| **The Drone** | 1,100, 3,000 | 1,120 m | Past everything, south-west, on the Seam's shoulder, with the survey standing between it and home. Route, aim and ground at once — and **the chord does not certify without it** |

Read the last row against the first: **a drone is the sustained bottom note the other voices are
tuned against**, so a lattice missing its Descant is a lattice with a thin top and a lattice missing
its Drone is not a lattice. That is why the Drone is the mission's keystone, and it is a fact about
chords rather than a rule the mission needed.

**The order is the player's.** Nothing scripts which voice is taken when, and a party that goes for
the Drone first is not punished for it — it is simply doing the hardest thing while it still has all
thirty seconds of tolerance in hand, which is a real strategy and the mission is playtested against
it. The table's order is the order of *increasing ask*, which is the order the beat table below
assumes a competent party will find, not one it enforces.

**Five hulls and six voices, and the party is short by exactly one.** A sounding is twenty seconds
of a stationary hull, and there are six of them across five kilometres of water, in sixteen
minutes, with a picket that closes the middle every two. The Cruiser can sound — it is a
hull with a bow — and it moves at 45 m/s against a corvette's 85, so using it costs more than it
saves except at whichever voice it is already nearest. That decision is the arithmetic of six,
made once, in the first two minutes, and it is the whole of what this mission takes from the
Knights' inability to grow. **Mission 3 spends hulls. This one only makes the player count them.**

## 7. What Is Heard

**The map sounds, continuously, and none of it is information.**

The Fields ring — crystal formations resonate under any pressure change, a storm, a passing hull, a
distant detonation, so the whole region sounds faintly and never quite settles
([world-map.md](world-map.md)). Knights read the chime the way the Directorate reads silence;
everyone else hears a haunted room. This is the first authored water in the bible whose ambient bed
is *pitched*, and the mix has to carry it without ever letting it read as a contact
([audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md)).

- **The Third, behind and above.** A chapter-house is a physical instrument built into a formation,
  so the house is audible as a tuned thing rather than as machinery: the one settlement in the game
  whose ambient signature is a chord.
- **The survey, coring.** Fifty-five SIG, sustained, from the middle of the map. The party's Cruiser
  holds it at contact from 3,824 m and at classification from 2,157 m, which means **the player
  classifies the survey long before the survey could classify them**, and that margin — 743 m
  bow-on, 1,822 m stern-on — is the entire working capital of the mission. It is also the Consortium
  being exactly what [factions.md](factions.md) says they are, used as a gift rather than as a
  weakness.
- **The picket, walking.** Two corvettes at 28, on two legs, on a two-minute cycle. Audible the
  whole time, which is the point: this mission never asks the player to guess where the listeners
  are. It asks what they are pointed at.
- **The soundings, six times.** Twenty seconds of held tone at 80, and the reply — the crystal
  answering in its own time, a beat behind, which is the thing the chapter is actually reading.
- **The Seam.** The southern strip where crystal country breaks toward the trenches: PF 1.60, and
  the trench's own signature — contacts arriving *twice*, the second arrival being the ground and
  not a second hull ([audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md)).

No fauna, no hazard, no third party, and nothing arrives that the player did not cause. The
discipline here is the opposite of Attendance's: that mission refused to put anything in the water,
and this one fills the water with sound and refuses to make any of it actionable except the two
things that are.

## 8. The Objective

**Sound the six, and bring the six home.**

Two counts, and they are not the same kind of thing, which is the mission's argument.

### Results

| Result | Condition | The reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In tune** | Six voices sounded, six hulls returned | Vrey: "In tune. The lattice is certified for the year, and the year is the only thing anybody in this chapter is certain of. You will do this again when you are old, and it will be someone else's first." |
| **A chord, and a rest where a voice was** | Four or five sounded including the Drone, six hulls returned | Vrey: "Five voices and a rest. A rest is written down, it is played, and it is not an absence — it is the part of a chord that is silent on purpose. I will not pretend this one was on purpose. The lattice is certified against five and the sixth is a year older than it was." |
| **Not certified** | Fewer than four sounded, or the Drone not sounded | Vrey: "The Drone was not taken. Everything above it was tuned against a note nobody read, so the chapter has six measurements and nothing to measure them from. This is not a failure of the party. It is a year, and the Order has more years than it has Knights." |
| **A hull not returned** | Any hull lost | Vrey: "The lattice will keep. — Say the name to the house yourself. It is not mine to enter." |

**The last row is the taboo, rendered as a refusal to speak rather than as a penalty.** The Knights'
prohibitions are rudeness to an opponent and any suggestion the Order can be replaced or expanded,
and both are about extinction ([culture.md](culture.md) §3). A reading that priced a lost hull
against a certified lattice would be the Consortium's register, not this one; a reading that
mourned it at length would be nobody's. **Vrey declines to enter it, and the mission ends short.**
That is the strongest thing the register can do and it costs the format nothing but an authored
line.

**"In tune" is the compliment**, it is the only one the Order gives, and it is two words. The
Directorate's highest praise is a whole sentence about sufficiency; the Knights' is an
observation about pitch. Neither is warmer than the other and both mean the same thing.

**The mission closes when the party is home, or at 16:00 — whichever comes first.** A party that
finishes early and turns for the house ends the mission early and is read on what it has, which is a
cadence and not a surrender: closing on your own terms is the one aesthetic judgement this faction
would make about a mission structure. Sixteen minutes is a conclusion, not a timer
([glossary.md](glossary.md), *Mission Outcome*).

### The failure, and the sounds that precede it

[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 asks that no mission fail on a timer and that every failure state be
audible for at least sixty seconds. Two states, and both clear it by a wide margin:

- **The tolerance.** Exposure is on the instrument from the first tick and climbs where the player
  can watch it. At twenty of the thirty seconds **the barge stops coring**, which is a 55-SIG source
  going silent in the middle of the map. What follows is a Consortium column that is audible for
  four minutes before it arrives — the faction's own documented weakness, used as the telegraph
  rather than worked around ([factions.md](factions.md)).
- **A hull lost.** Only reachable after the recall, only by a party that stays, and only in a fight
  the Order did not arrange — which is the sentence [systems-combat.md](systems-combat.md) §11 uses
  for the Knights and the sentence Vrey uses at 00:00. Combat here is elective, is never necessary,
  and is announced by a push that was audible before it started.

**Nothing else in this mission can be lost.** Nothing crushes — the whole map is Mid-Water and the
party is PR-2 ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md) §1). Nothing hunts. The survey does not want a
fight and would file one as an unbudgeted exposure.

## 9. Length, SIG Budget, and the Beats

**Length: sixteen minutes.** Inside [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's 12–25.

**SIG budget: 28, in the cone** — §4. **The tolerance: thirty seconds at Classification, cumulative,
across the party** — §5.

**This beat table is the world's clock, not the player's.** The soundings are the player's acts and
are taken in whatever order the party finds; what is scheduled below is the picket, the survey, and
the chapter. The bracketed rows are where a competent party tends to be, recorded so the mission can
be tuned against something, and nothing in the runtime enforces them.

## 10. What It Teaches

One system, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10: **directional signature** — where a hull is pointed
decides who hears it. It lands in order across the beat table: the sounding itself (Descant, taught
by having it free), the route (Tenor), the ground (Treble), the window (Alto), the passage and the
cadence (Bass), and all four at once on the voice the chord cannot do without (the Drone).

Underneath it, the campaign's real subject, which is the same trick [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2
rule 2 plays on every faction: **the Knights are told nothing about being few.** Six hulls, six
formations, sixteen minutes and a picket that closes the middle every two — the player counts hulls
against work for the entire mission and reaches the Order's central anxiety by arithmetic. Nobody
says *we cannot be replaced*. Kalliso, who has the best reason in the Order to say it, is the one
character in the mission who does not.

And a third thing, which is why the mission is called what it is. **The examination in the title is
not a metaphor for the mission; the mission is literally the examination**, administered by the man
who administers it, in the same words, to a party of adults. A player who works that out is holding
the Order's whole tragedy: an institution that closed its door at age nine, has been giving the same
test ever since, and is now down to thirty thousand people and one plan.

What this mission deliberately does not teach:

- **Standing Wave, paired nodes and PF 2.0 corridors** — mission 2. The doctrine's *other* half:
  Aptitude is about aiming what you already emit, and mission 2 is about building a place that
  emits ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §7).
- **Permanent loss** — mission 3, *Nineteen*, where every hull spent is spent for the rest of the
  campaign. Aptitude prices six hulls inside one afternoon; *Nineteen* prices them across seven
  missions. **Six is a small number to work with** and **six is a small number to have** are not
  the same lesson and this mission teaches only the first.
- **Depth, PR and projected access** — the Sounding Spire's PR+1 is the Knights' whole depth
  doctrine and no mission in §7 teaches it. This one cannot: the entire map is Mid-Water and PR-2
  covers it, so depth never comes up. Flagged in §13 as a campaign-level gap rather than papered
  over here.
- **Active sonar** — §3, and the campaign's oldest argument, which for this faction is also its
  shortest.

## 11. The Map

`outer-formations` · **The Third's Outer Formations** · one seat · 5,000 × 4,000 m · cell 250 m ·
base floor 1,700 m.

The shallow Resonance Fields on the east slope, below the thermocline and above the Abyssal line:
**every metre of this map is Mid-Water**, so PR-2 covers all of it, nothing crushes, and the depth
system is silent for the whole mission ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md) §1). The thermocline
duct ends at 1,300 m and the shallowest ground here is 1,450, so the layer's factor is 1 on every
pair and does not enter into the mission either ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §3). **Both are
deliberate.** One system per mission means the other two axes of the game have to be arranged to
say nothing, and the cleanest way to arrange that is ground on which they have nothing to say.

| Region | Rect (x, y, w, h) | Biome | Floor | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Fields | 0, 0, 5000, 4000 | Resonance Field | 1,700 | Crystal country. PF 0.70, scattered; painted first, and almost nothing is cut into it |
| The Third's Approach | 3750, 0, 1250, 1000 | Resonance Field | 1,450 | The chapter-house's own water. **The spawn**, and the region the party returns to |
| The Seam | 0, 3500, 5000, 500 | Abyssal Trench | 1,750 | Where crystal country breaks toward the trenches ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). PF 1.60 — the one strip of ground on this map that carries |

**Three regions, which is the shortest table in the bible, and the argument for it is §1.** Every
other mission map hands the player terrain to be quiet in: the prologue's occluded districts, Face
Six's vent line, the plateau's kelp, the Ninth's benches. This one hands them 0.70 water in every
direction and one strip that is worse. **The doctrine is the only cover on the map**, and a fourth
region would be an alternative to the thing the mission is teaching.

The formations are **authored points, not terrain** — the same choice the taps are
([mission-asset-recovery.md](mission-asset-recovery.md) §6). Crystal that rose 250 m off the floor
would lift a hull that crossed it, which is a real mechanic ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md)
§2) with nothing to say in a mission where every depth is safe; a marked position and a 400 m
sounding radius says everything the mission needs and no more.

**The parties, placed by the mission:**

| What | Where | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The party | spawn 4,500, 500 | Six hulls, at 1,450 m, in the Third's approach |
| Survey barge and escort Cruiser | 1,600, 2,000 | Stationary all mission. The barge cores; the Cruiser stands over it |
| Picket North | leg 2,500, 800 ↔ 2,500, 1,800 | 60 s each way |
| Picket South | leg 2,500, 2,200 ↔ 2,500, 3,200 | 60 s each way, in **opposite phase** |

**The gate breathes.** Two legs in opposite phase put the picket hulls 400 m apart at the centre
once every two minutes and 2,400 m apart once every two minutes, and the passage west is taken on
the second. The cycle is fixed, audible and never varies, so the mission is a rhythm problem and
not a reaction test — which is the correct shape for a faction that thinks in cadence and the wrong
shape for anybody else. It is the *only* moving thing in the mission and it moves on a metronome.

**Every rectangle lands on the 250 m cell grid and paints exactly the metres it reads**
([maps.md](maps.md), "How a map is written"). No resources, no hazard sites, no second spawn, and
`fauna` off. **The Third's Outer Formations is a mission map and is not in the public catalogue** —
one seat, no economy, not balanced, resolved by mission id and nothing else.

## 12. The Briefing

Spoken by Chapter-Master Halden Vrey from the Third, at the top of the exercise. The Knight register
is defined in [culture.md](culture.md) §3: precise, courteous, slightly archaic, musical and
mathematical metaphor used literally rather than poetically, and unfailingly polite in a way that
reads to everyone else as condescension.

**Chapter-Master Halden Vrey, setting the exercise — 00:00**

> Good. You have all done this before, at nine, with your ears, and the only thing that has changed
> is the size of the instrument.
>
> Six voices stand off the house. You will put a tone into each and read what comes back. Twenty
> seconds, bow to the formation, and hold it — a tone you interrupt is a tone you have not played.
> The chapter has one measurement a year and this is the year.
>
> A concern is coring in the middle of it. They are within their own procedure and I will not be
> asking them to leave, because we have never written down that this water is ours and they have
> written down that it is not. You will therefore be *courteous*, which today means unheard.
>
> You are loud in front. You are not loud on the beam and you are very nearly nothing behind. Two
> numbers: fourteen hundred metres on the bow, seven-fifty on the beam. You may go west. You may
> not point west — go in a cadence, one bearing and then the other, and let them have your flank
> both times.
>
> Six of you. Six voices. I would like both of those numbers to be the same at the end as they are
> now, and if I have to choose between them, I have chosen.

### Objective readings, in play

The Order states intervals and conditions, never tasks, and is courteous to the point of formality:

- *The lattice stands at six voices. None is entered.*
- *Descant is entered. The drift is a quarter-tone flat and the chapter will be told so.*
- *Two hulls are held. One is classified. Eleven seconds of thirty are entered.*
- *Twenty-two of thirty. The coring has stopped.*
- *The Drone is entered. The chord is whole.*

### The voices in the water

**Surveyor Ade Bramm, on the survey's open channel — 06:00**

> Log it as the second fault this year and grade the array for replacement at the next refit. It is
> not a hull. There is nothing out here to be a hull, and if there were, we would have costed the
> escort differently.

**Chapter-Master Halden Vrey, on the chapter channel — 08:00**

> The Choirmaster has written to the houses about a window. I am not going to discuss it with a
> party in the water and I would not discuss it with you in the house either, so take this as
> nothing: an instrument is tuned so that it will be in tune later, and the word doing the work in
> that sentence is *later*. Carry on.

**Voice Ren Kalliso, once, to nobody in particular — on the first sounding**

> That is the same tone. Twenty-two years on and it is the same tone. They did not even change it.

**Chapter-Master Halden Vrey, at the close — 16:00**

> The reading of the chord, per §8, and — in every ending but the last — one more sentence:
> "Enter it, and go and be dry."

Each line fails [culture.md](culture.md) §6's register test for the other three factions, which is
that document's own bar. Vrey's briefing concedes an opponent's procedure as *correct* and then
declines to act on it, which the Consortium would have costed and the Directorate could not have
said without claiming humility it does not claim toward people; his 08:00 line is a man refusing to
discuss a thing by describing exactly the thing, which the Commune would have opened for discussion
instead; Bramm's log entry disbelieves his own instrument in the vocabulary of a refit budget, which
no other register has the nouns for; and Kalliso's one line is a soldier noticing that an
institution has not changed a single note since she was nine and saying so with something that is not
quite affection and not quite an accusation — the Directorate would have called that sufficiency
and meant it, and the Knights are the only faction for whom it is a joke and a grief at once.

## 13. Scaffold Status

What exists against this document and what does not, continuing the list
[mission-asset-recovery.md](mission-asset-recovery.md) §13 started and
[mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §13 and [mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md) §13
extended. **This is the first of the five whose headline row is a system rather than a mission
mechanism**, and the honest summary is that this mission is further from buildable than the three
before it — though less so than when this section was written: the top two rows are built, and
what is left is four instruments and a map.

| Requirement | Status |
| --- | --- |
| **Directional signature — the term itself** | **Built** (#269). One multiplier on the emitter side of a pair that already carried three, folded in beside the thermocline factor because both are properties of the pair and neither can hoist out of the candidate loop. Cheaper than §8 promised: the sector is decided by a dot product against the hoisted bow and two compares, with no divide and no trigonometry in the inner loop. Nothing new crosses the wire. The trap the row warned about is now guarded by test — `echo-parity.test.ts` states §8's rule independently of the pass's own predicate, so the two cannot drift |
| **A hull's bow, when the hull is not moving** | **Built** (#269) — a `Heading` component, written by `movementSystem` and never cleared by it, carrying the *ordered* course rather than the travelled one so that terrain sliding a hull along a ridge cannot swing a cone nobody aimed. Added by `spawnUnit` and by nothing else, which is what makes "does this thing have a bow" the same question as "is this a hull" and gives §8's structure exclusion a home. It also fixes a smaller thing on the way past: a Tier-4 contact's reported facing was `atan2` of a zero velocity — due east, for every stopped hull on the map — and is now the bow |
| **The sounding — hold, at a SIG, facing a point** | **Half built, and the half that exists is the useful one.** `MissionLift`'s hold-and-cut is already "stand here for N ticks at SIG X" — `cutTicks` and `cutSig`, built for Face Six ([mission-asset-recovery.md](mission-asset-recovery.md) §6). **A sounding is that shape with a bearing added**, plus a 400 m radius instead of a region. The new part is the facing requirement and the fact that the hold *aims* the hull, which is the same component the row above asks for |
| **The tolerance — a tally over the player's own exposure** | **Not built, and the data is already there and already safe.** `ExposureReport` is computed every Echo tick per slot and carries the best tier anybody holds on that player's force; [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §9 argues at length that sending it leaks nothing, because it is resolved information about the player's own hulls. What is missing is a predicate that accumulates seconds while `exposure.tier ≥ Classification`. It is the mirror of Attendance's `attend`, which tallies the player's resolution *of* an emitter, and it should be built beside it |
| **Exposure as the primary HUD instrument** | **Not built.** [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §9 makes the player's own SIG meter a permanent HUD element and mentions exposure as carried on the snapshot; for a directional faction **the SIG meter is an incomplete statement** and exposure is the honest one. This mission is unplayable without it on screen, which makes it the first mission in the bible with a hard [ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) dependency rather than a soft one |
| **A beat fired by a condition rather than a tick** | **Not built.** Every beat in the format keys on a tick, which is right for authored transits and wrong for the barge falling silent at twenty seconds of an accumulating tally. The recall and the column are the same problem. The smallest honest shape is a beat with a predicate instead of an `atTick`, and it should be scoped as that rather than as a scripting language ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §9 — "a table, not a language") |
| A scripted party walking authored legs | **Built** — Tend's sweep (`MissionSweep`, windows) is exactly two hulls on two legs, and the picket is that with a longer window list ([mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §6) |
| The map, three regions, no resources | **Not built.** `outer-formations` does not exist. Nothing in §11 needs anything the map format lacks — three rectangles on the cell grid, one spawn, empty resource and hazard lists |
| A Knight hull in the roster | **Not written**, and this is a [units.md](units.md) gap rather than a code one. The faction has one structure and no hulls; §3 fields generic hulls and says so, and §8 of [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) records the 2.2× the roster should carry when somebody writes it |
| Resonance Field 0.70 as **scattered** — bearings that lie, pings that return phantoms | **Not built**, and this mission does not rely on it. PF is a scalar and 0.70 is applied as one; the "scattered" half of [environments.md](environments.md)'s entry — false contacts, wrong bearings — has never been modelled. It would make this map harder in a way the mission would have to be re-tuned against, so it is named here so nobody adds it and assumes the tuning survived |
| PR and depth, deliberately silent | **Built and unused** — the map is entirely Mid-Water and the party is PR-2, so `requiredPressureRating` returns 2 everywhere and the thermocline factor is 1 on every pair. Stated because a reader will check, and because the *campaign* gap is real: the Sounding Spire's PR+1 is the Knights' whole depth doctrine and no mission in [campaign.md](campaign.md) §7 teaches it |
| Permanent roster attrition | **Not built**, and not needed here — mission 3's row, and §10 argues why the two must stay apart |
| In-mission character speech, heard | Text only, the standing status ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §13) |
| The Fields' chime as a pitched ambient bed, and a chapter-house that sounds like a chord | Not started ([audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md)). The first authored water in the bible whose ambient bed is *tuned*, which is a new problem for the mix rather than a bigger version of an old one |
