Title screen review

The Port, Breathing

Five title screens for Echoes of the Abyss, all of them moving. Every colour, face and glow recipe below is already in the style guide. What is new is a heartbeat.

The shell is the port (docs/ui-ux.md §14). It is the only screen a player meets before the water, and right now it is the one part of this game that does not move at all. Each concept below is a live screen, not a picture of one. Switch motion off in the bar to see what each is left with, and hold a seat to see the resume entry every concept has to find room for.

Shipping today

For comparison

Rebuilt from App.css at the same scale as the concepts, so the comparison is like for like rather than a screenshot at another size.

Dead frame
The column is 560 px on a 1440 px screen. Most of the frame is unused black rather than atmosphere, and the violet rise the CSS already paints from the bottom edge never becomes visible against it.
No argument
Seven identical cards. Campaign and Credits carry the same weight, so the screen declines to say what to do first.
No pulse
Nothing moves, so the reduced-motion setting has nothing to reduce. The thesis of the whole game is that the water is alive and listening.
Half a plate
The plate VI card anatomy is only half spent: no registration ticks, no cyan header rule, no scanline texture, and a 4 px radius where the style guide asks for survey marks.

Sounding

Least change

The mark is an emitter, so let it emit. Every four seconds a front leaves the throat and falls down the list, and each entry brightens as it passes. Nothing on this screen is lit by a hover rule any more. It is lit by the ping.

Breathes
One front every 4.0 s, which is twenty ticks of the Echo Layer's 200 ms grid. The front crosses an entry in 80 ms and the entry decays over 480 ms. The throat flashes on emit.
Licensed by
style-neon-noir.md, motion and FX timing: sonar cadence is the heartbeat, and HUD pulses quantise to the 5 Hz grid. The front leaves violet and arrives cyan, handing over at the foot of the masthead, so the Mouth keeps its reserved ink and the interface answers in its own.
Stilled
No travel. Entries hold at the middle brightness the front averages to, and the rule under the masthead goes solid.
Costs
It is the current layout with a light on it. Shippable this week, and it does not fix the dead frame.

Thermocline

Depth as layout

The screen gets a depth axis. The lockup sits at the surface, the entries descend, and each is dimmer and wider-tracked than the one above it. A ribbon down the left edge carries the real bands, and the water drifts.

Breathes
Strata drift over 34 s. Marine snow rises past a camera that is descending. The pressure needle sways over 6.4 s.
Licensed by
systems-depth.md §1 for the bands and the 1,200 m thermocline. naming.md kept the pressure gauge as a motif for loading and settings chrome after setting it aside as the mark, which is the only reason a needle is allowed here.
Stilled
Strata and needle park. The snow is removed rather than frozen, because frozen snow reads as dust on the screen.
Costs
The tallest build of the five. The ribbon is a second thing to read before the entries, and in portrait it is the first thing that has to go.

Listening Room

The port is a station

A hydrophone runs down the left third of the frame on an empty channel, showing a noise floor and no contacts. The entries lose their boxes and become rules, because the plate VI card is the in-match voice and this screen is not the instrument.

Breathes
One waterfall row every 200 ms, the Echo Layer's own tick made literal. The floor swells over about twelve seconds, and the three standing ridges in it drift with it. Those ridges are the port's own machinery: a real hydrophone floor has stationary lines in it, and an even field of hiss reads as television snow rather than as water.
Licensed by
ui-ux.md §14, "the port has a sound". The waterfall says in the visual channel what the menu bed already says in the mix. Both of its axes are named, bearing across the head and elapsed time down the gutter, because an unlabelled spectrogram is wallpaper.
Stilled
The waterfall holds its last frame and stays on screen, because a still spectrogram is still a reading.
Costs
An instrument that shows nothing real can read as a lie. So the trace is cyan, the ink that tells, and it is labelled as an empty channel; the hover mark is magenta, the ink that asks. That is your hand on the console, not a contact. It also spends a third of the frame on something nobody can act on, which is the price of the atmosphere.

Silent Running

The riskiest

The menu sits at the noise floor. Move the pointer and the screen brightens and the signature readout climbs. Go still and both sink back. The game's first rule gets taught before the game starts: everything that makes you strong makes you loud.

Breathes
A 10 s luminance cycle at ±7%. Moving raises the signature and about two seconds of stillness lets it fall back. Each row also answers on its own: --near is 1 under the cursor and 0 a third of a frame away, falling off with the square of the distance, because that is what sound does.
Licensed by
systems-echo.md §6, by way of SILENT_RUNNING in the shared constants: the column's three marks are its SIG_MIN of 3, its SIG_MAX of 8, and the 43 that breaking silence costs on top. The silent band is a twelfth of that scale, and the column says so. The lockup is naming.md's silent-running variant, monochrome and unglowed, which that document calls on-doctrine, and it is the one thing here that never dims. The four survey marks at the frame's corners are the plate VI card's registration ticks spent at frame scale, which is the quietest way to make four objects on a dark field read as one plate.
Stilled
The ten-second cycle stops and the panel holds at its readable level. The pointer response and the readout stay, because those answer you rather than run on their own, and the number is what carried the information anyway.
Costs
A menu that dims when you stop touching it is a usability argument you have to win. Every label and every note sits at a readable floor and only brightens as you approach, so nothing is behind a hover, and the row keeps its height at every level so the list never reflows under your hand. It still wants a phone test, where there is no pointer to be near with.

The Mouth

Key art

The mark at frame scale. Five arcs across the whole screen, the throat at the bottom edge, and one door per band. The bands brighten downward, so the list descends into the light and the thing you most want is the deepest.

Breathes
A brightening travels down the five arcs on a 4 s cycle, one arc every 320 ms. The throat halo dilates on the same four seconds, which is 0.25 Hz.
Licensed by
naming.md, the mark and its rules. Brightness increases with depth and never the other way, which is what forces the list to run deepest first.
Stilled
The arcs hold their static brightness ramp and the throat halo holds at full. The ramp is the mark, so it never goes away.
Costs
Reading order runs against importance, which is why Settings and Credits leave the bands for the corner: five doors into the water, five bands. Each label is cut out of the stroke it sits on by a mask whose ends fade, not by a patch of background, so the arc dies into the word rather than stopping square against it and the label is set into the mark instead of floating near it. The fade is a fixed length, so even the narrowest band keeps a stub of arc either side of the longest label it can be asked to hold. Note lines still appear on hover only, so the screen says less at rest than the others, and with no seat held the shallowest band carries nothing. And the throat stays the throat: nothing in this picture is you, which is why a held seat takes the deepest band and not the light.

What it costs to ship one

The docs are canonical in this repository, so a concept becomes real by amending a document first and then transcribing it. Sounding and The Mouth argue with naming.md. Thermocline and Listening Room argue with ui-ux.md §14. Silent Running argues with both, and adds a row to the reduced-motion table in style-neon-noir.md.

In code, all five are TitleScreen.tsx and the menu block of App.css. Three need no new component at all. Thermocline and Listening Room add one canvas each, which is the only place any of this costs a frame.

Every concept keeps what the shell tests hold: seven real buttons with their accessible names intact, the resume entry autofocused while a seat is held, visible keyboard focus, and a reduced-motion path. No concept introduces a colour that is not already a token, which is what gate 4 of graphics-standards.md asks before a screenshot is even worth taking.
