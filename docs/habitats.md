# Habitats — Inside the Rift's Cities

> [world-map.md](world-map.md) says where the Rift's people live, to the metre. This document
> says what it is like to be there: what a berth is, what the light is, what the air costs, what
> a day sounds like, and what each of the four cultures calls beautiful — because the Rift has
> four incompatible answers to that, too. Mission text, environment art and the campaign's
> interiors are written out of this document; the gazetteer's numbers bound it.

Nothing here moves a SPEC number. Where this document names a thing the bible had not — a hall,
a custom, a face at a hatch — it is new canon, reconciled to what already stands: the depths are
the gazetteer's, the palettes are [factions.md](factions.md)'s, the sound figures are
[economy.md](economy.md)'s and [units.md](units.md)'s, and every interior the mission documents
have already walked through is described here exactly as they left it.

---

## 1. What Every Habitat Shares

Four cultures, one water. Before the habitats diverge, five facts hold in all of them.

**Everything is a retrofit.** Deep habitation before the Collapse was research stations,
vent-field industry, and a handful of colonies, and none of it was built to be lived in for
good ([timeline.md](timeline.md)). Two centuries on, every inhabited structure in the Rift is
something temporary that was never allowed to stop: a pressure hull with a second hull grown or
welded or cut around it, a gallery that was a working before it was a street. The one place
built to be permanent fell in ([world-map.md](world-map.md), *The Drowned City*). A Rift
architect's first question is never *what shall we build* — it is *what is already here that
will hold*.

**A berth is the unit of housing, and a berth is a debt.** The word came from the Consortium's
debt-berth contracts and now means *obligation* everywhere ([culture.md](culture.md) §2), so
even the Commune, which owns nothing, and the Directorate, which owes nothing, house their
people in a word that means owing. A habitat's size is quoted in berths, never in rooms.

**Dry is the luxury, and every habitat is organised around who gets to be dry.** Water is free.
A room where paper survives, a bed that is never cold, a wall without a film of condensate — that
is wealth, in all four cultures, and the layout of every city below is a diagram of who has it.

**The air is a utility with an owner.** The thermal grid, strung down the west wall since 71 PC,
carries vent heat and power to every habitat that pays for draw, and *whoever runs the grid runs
the air* ([economy.md](economy.md); [timeline.md](timeline.md)). Three of the four powers pay.
The habitats that do not — the plateaus, which grow their own, and Sufficiency, which eats — are
the two the Consortium cannot switch off, and both of them know it.

**Every habitat has a hush.** Silence is owed when someone else is listening
([culture.md](culture.md) §1), so every inhabited place keeps one room, gallery or terrace where
the courtesy is absolute: the plateaus' watch-edge, the Holding's array floor, the galleries, a
chapter-house's chord, the court's chamber. In a habitat, the hush is where the important thing
happens, and a stranger can find it by walking toward the quiet.

## 2. The Plateaus — a garden under a pale sky

*Shelf band, 200–400 m, the Rift's north shoulder · Pelagia Commune · PF 0.55*

The oldest inhabited ground in the Rift, and the only place a person would call beautiful without
being corrected ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). Kelp columns forty metres tall sway in the tidal
draw; the farm rows between them are marked in bioluminescent trail, `#8FE36B` on `#0B241E`; and
overhead at midday the Lid glows — the poison, lit from above by a sun nobody has stood under,
reading from below as a pale ceiling ([mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §1). It is the closest
thing to a sky anyone in the Rift has, and it is 150 m of sour water. The Commune knows both
things and has decided to find it lovely anyway.

**Where people live.** A plateau berth is grown, the way a hull is: a pod of chitin composite
hung in the kelp holdfast, pigment-lit, warm from the algae reactor it shares with its row
([factions.md](factions.md)). Nothing on a plateau is painted, and colour is alive — a berth dims
when its reactor is sick, exactly as a Commune hull dims when it is wounded. Rows of berths follow
farm rows, so a plateau's housing plan is its planting plan, and a person is named for the ground
they tend: half of Marr Plateau is called Marr ([culture.md](culture.md) §4).

**The day.** A tide of work — three tenders lifting, two scouts listening down the drop
([mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md)). A working plateau harvests at 18 SIG
([economy.md](economy.md)) and the kelp eats most of that, so a tender under load is audible from
the next row and not from the next plateau. The loudest thing on a good day is the turn-of-tide
bell, and a plateau that hears a hull at cruise has already stopped work to listen, because a
hull at cruise is somebody else's.

**The hush.** The southern edge, where the terrace falls away toward the Mid-Water lanes and the
bare slope carries. The watch sits there. Children learn silence at the drop before they learn to
swim ([culture.md](culture.md) §1), and the Commune's one formal etiquette is that nobody speaks
to the watch while the watch is at the edge.

**What is dry.** Almost nothing. A plateau berth is damp by construction — it is a growing thing
in the water it grows in — and paper does not survive a season. This is why the Commune keeps no
files and classifies nothing ([timeline.md](timeline.md), 191 PC): its records are kept in
people, and the word *unfiled* means safe ([mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §1). The one dry
room on a plateau is the seed-store, and the Bloomwright has the only key to it that exists.

**What it costs.** The plateaus are the shallowest inhabited water in the Rift, and shallow is the
insult. The Commune's ground is the closest to the Lid, the cheapest by every other culture's
reckoning, and the only ground the Drift's predators cannot climb to
([mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §1) — safe and poor for the same reason. And the terraces are
adjacent to Sector Kell: in 197 PC the flood front crossed from the Holding's poorest sector onto
the Commune's fields, and the boats were not big enough ([timeline.md](timeline.md)). Kell
Plateau is replanted and the kelp grows and the name has not changed.

**What the Commune calls beautiful.** Anything that grows without being told to. A row that
has closed over its trail. The midday glow. Real food, offered.

**A face.** Ottilie Marr, tender crew, b. 290 m, twenty-three. She has stood under the Lid's glow
every midday of her life and has never asked what is above it, because the question is an old
person's ([culture.md](culture.md) §1). She can name every hull on the drop lane by its screw. Her
mother was a Kell.

### The Turning — where a plateau votes

*The room [campaign.md](campaign.md) §5 puts Seeding 3 in: a plateau voting while it is attacked.*

A bloom-collective has no chamber, and the reason is acoustic before it is political. Kelp eats
the top of the spectrum at PF 0.55 and a voice does not cross two rows
([audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md) §9), so the plateaus never built the room a vote would
need — there is nowhere on a terrace where a thousand people can hear one person. What they built
instead is a decision that moves.

**Where people are.** A convocation is *walked*. It opens at one row and passes along the
holdfast lines, every row turning it and carrying it to the next, until the walk comes back to
the row it started at with nothing new in it. That closing circuit is the count: not a tally of
hands but a question that has stopped changing. A plateau that is **still turning** is one where
the walk returned altered, and it goes round again ([culture.md](culture.md) §3). The rows are
the room, and a person turns it on the ground they are named for.

**Convening.** The turn-of-tide bell, rung off-tide. It is the loudest deliberate act on a
plateau and very nearly the only one, and every neighbour hears it: a plateau that convenes has
announced to the water that it has stopped working. The Tidespeaker may ring it — Marr can
convene, persuade and refuse, and cannot order ([characters.md](characters.md)) — and so may any
row that can find a second row willing to ring with it. **Emergency convocation** is the same
walk with every row's bell rung at once, so that the circuit runs in one tide instead of several.
It exists, and it has never been rung.

**The hush, broken on purpose.** The count is read at the southern edge, in the watch's own
water, because the bare slope is the only ground on a plateau that carries and a result has to
reach everybody at once. Nobody speaks to the watch while the watch is at the edge (above); a
convocation is the single exception the Commune has agreed to, and it is the one time the
plateaus break their own courtesy knowing they are doing it. It is also, exactly, the moment the
vote becomes audible to anything listening up the drop.

**What is dry.** Nothing, and nothing is written. A convocation leaves no record but the people
who walked it, which is why a Commune decision cannot be produced afterwards, cannot be entered
at Sorrowgate, and cannot be checked against what anyone now says it was. *Unfiled* means safe;
it also means the second Seeding will never be shown to have been authorised by anybody.

**As a map.** Kelp Forest rows and a bare southern slope, as [mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md)
left them: the walk is a route along the holdfast lines, the bell is a loud event at a fixed
tick, and the reading at the edge is the mission's own SIG spike. The Commune's hush is courtesy
rather than an order (§8), so a mission prices it as courtesy — the convocation breaks it and the
player pays for it in what hears them. The circuit's length is the mission's clock, and it takes
exactly as long as it takes; a plateau under attack cannot vote faster. Which terrace holds it was
the mission document's to choose ([world-map.md](world-map.md) names four): the room needs a full
row-set to walk, a watch-edge that carries, and a bell.
[mission-convocation.md](mission-convocation.md) §11 chose Marr Plateau and reuses *Tend*'s map
literal unchanged, so [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 5 has two missions on one map — and,
since #379, a carry between them.

## 3. Holding One — a city that is a ledger

*Mid-Water, 350–1,300 m, the west wall · Bathyarch Consortium · loud*

The Consortium's capital is a vertical city built down the wall face, berths from 350 m to
1,300 m, universally called *the Holding*. Asset 001, the first entry in the registry, and the
registry is the point: nothing in the Holding is unnumbered ([world-map.md](world-map.md)).
Every bulkhead, hatch, pump and berth carries a stencilled asset number in `#F2B233` hazard amber
on `#8C8378` iron grey, and a citizen gives directions in them the way other cultures use names.

**Where people live.** By grade, literally. Junior berths at the top, Board country at the
bottom, and a promotion is measured in metres of descent. The city is a stack of berth-sectors,
each a converted pressure vessel strung to the wall and to the grid, each riveted, patched and
over-engineered in the manner of everything the concern builds — a sector is *repaired*, never
replaced, and older plate shows through newer everywhere ([factions.md](factions.md)). The
Holding has no plan. It has an order of accession.

**The day.** The Holding hums, permanently — vents, pumps, freight screws, the shift klaxons that
the doctrine is named for — and the Consortium stopped apologising for it a century ago. A
citizen navigates by pump signature: a grid-walker knows which sector they are in by the note of
the circulation, the way a Commune farmer reads kelp lanes ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). The
hum is not noise. It is the sound of the air being on, and a sector that goes quiet is a sector
that has been sealed.

**The hush.** The array floor, below Board country, where the registry's open arrays stand — the
listening instruments the concern keeps for the Board's own ears
([mission-item-nine.md](mission-item-nine.md)). The workplace regulation the Consortium made of
silence ([culture.md](culture.md) §1) is absolute there, and a grid-walker sent down to the
arrays walks the last forty metres with their tools wrapped.

**What is dry.** Depth. The deepest berths are the driest, because the concern spends its
condensate control where its standing is, and a Board member's vault is the driest room in the
Rift. At the top, in the cheap sectors, the walls run. *Married dry* is a Consortium phrase first
([culture.md](culture.md) §1), and in the Holding it has a floor number.

**Three places the city is built around.**

- **Sector Kell**, at 350 m — the top of the city, the cheap berths, the debt-berth families,
  and the sector Varr-Kest flooded in 197 PC to save the rest: 1,900 dead, still on the registry,
  sealed and unlit ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). The concern does not strike assets. Sectors
  either side of it have been re-pressurised twice since, and their walls still run.
- **The Underworks**, beneath everything — the Surface Age vent works the city was roofed onto,
  galleries and castings poured before year 0, which the engineers call the root: roofed water,
  admitted only between 1,900 and 2,100 m, reached through one open shaft and priced in crush for
  every working hull the city owns ([mission-tolerance.md](mission-tolerance.md) §1;
  [world-map.md](world-map.md) §3). The Holding is heated from its own root,
  and the root is two centuries older than the concern that depends on it.
- **The Underway**, at the very bottom — Asset 002, half a Surface Age transit hall the first
  concessionaires roofed their first vault into, and the room the Ninth Board has met in since
  there was a Board, because in its drowned acoustics everyone hears everyone, imperfectly, and
  nobody hears more than anyone else ([mission-item-nine.md](mission-item-nine.md) §1).

**The workings.** The Holding is where the Consortium sleeps; the vent fields are where it lives.
Ninefold Vein's faces work below the layer at 900–1,400 m, its rail head stands above it at
Fivewell, and a crew moves between the two by barge — three watches to a face, seventy-one
berths across the three barges that carry them, transferred *at grade* if the face is producing
and reverted to pool rate if it has closed ([mission-shift-change.md](mission-shift-change.md) §1). A face has a muster hall, a
registry office and a refuge chamber, and the refuge chamber is the only room on the Vein with a
door that closes from the inside ([mission-asset-recovery.md](mission-asset-recovery.md)).

**What it costs.** Roughly 40% of the Rift lives in Consortium debt-berth
([factions.md](factions.md)), and a debt-berth berth is exactly what it sounds like: housing,
against future income, forever. The Board knows the system is indenture and has modelled the
alternative, and the alternative kills more people. That is the Holding's whole moral shape, and
it is legible from outside as a lit wall with its poorest floors at the top.

**What the Consortium calls beautiful.** *Orderly.* A number made on schedule. A wall of lit
sector numbers running two kilometres down the rock, every one of them accounted for. The
Consortium does not have a word for beauty and does not need one; *well-costed* does the work.

**A face.** Pell Ansgar, grid-walker, b. 410 m, thirty-eight, second-generation debt-berth. He
maintains the circulation between 400 and 700 m and can tell the tide from the pump note without
a clock. His berth is two floors below where he was born, which is the only promotion he expects,
and he considers it a good one.

## 4. Sorrowgate — the city nobody lives in

*The drowned city, mid-Rift, 340 m to 2,400 m · neutral ground · PF 0.80, occluded*

The Surface Age's one attempt at a permanent deep colony: a civic city with a transit line, a
passenger terminus and a hydrophone array larger than anything built since, half-finished at
year 0, fallen in some year nobody kept, and nameless — the Rift calls the whole ruin Sorrowgate
after the gate its dead went through ([world-map.md](world-map.md);
[mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §1). It is the only place in the Rift built by
people who expected to stay, and it is the only place nobody does.

**What stands.** Broken domes, collapsed tunnels and coral-clad colonnades from the Concourse
down to the Commit: the Coral Ruins biome is the Surface Age's drowned works wherever they
stand, and this is chief among them ([environments.md](environments.md)). Its architecture is
the one style in the Rift that was designed rather than retrofitted — arches, halls, a terminus
with a vault — and every faction's builders have walked it and none of them have copied it,
because it fell.

- **The Upper Concourse**, 340 m — the passenger terminus, above the layer, the city's front
  door and the prologue's way out.
- **The court**, 1,500 m — a collapsed transit dome under the thermocline, where nothing said
  carries upward and nothing above carries down: an arch, a chamber, a service lock, and the
  water underneath. Arbiter Halloran has kept the Rift's only neutral record here since 165 PC.
- **The Commit**, to 2,400 m — the basin the transit line carried the city's dead out over. All
  four cultures still commit their dead downward, and this is the ground they inherited it from
  ([culture.md](culture.md) §5).

**Who is here.** The court, and the court does not count itself as living anywhere: Halloran, a
clerk, the crews of two tenders and four escort hulls that the Commune gave and the Consortium
has never let anyone forget ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §2). No faction
salvages the city and no faction settles it, by the same unspoken arrangement that leaves Halvard
closed — a place that fell is a place you do not move into.

**The hush.** The chamber, under a silence order that is not taken on trust: hardpoints struck in
front of all four parties, the count read aloud rather than transmitted
([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §3). The court's whole procedure is two centuries
of people not disturbing something in the basin below, formalised and then forgotten as a reason.

**What is dry.** The record. The court's one luxury is a dry room for it, and the record is the
court's one asset — the register that speaks in the perfect tense exists because paper survives
in exactly one chamber of the drowned city ([culture.md](culture.md) §3).

**What it costs.** Neutrality is a fiction all four powers maintain because the alternative is
having nowhere to swap prisoners ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §2). The court
has no navy, no draw of its own, and no standing except that everyone afterwards agrees what was
said. And it sits inside a colossus's reach, because the layer that makes a court possible is the
same layer a Sounder lives under.

**What the record calls beautiful.** Nothing. The court gives one compliment, *you were quiet*,
and it is the only one it has ([culture.md](culture.md) §3).

## 5. The Chapter-Houses — living inside an instrument

*The Resonance Fields, 1,400–2,900 m, the east slope · Hadron Knights · PF 0.70, scattered*

Nine houses, each built *into* a natural resonance formation, each a physical instrument tuned
over generations, numbered First through Ninth in order of founding — and the Order founded
deep, so the First stands nearest the Mouth, which is the Order's whole doctrine in one
architectural fact; the number is an age and not a depth, and the Third at 1,450 m stands above
the Ninth at 1,900 ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). Perhaps thirty thousand souls
between the nine, the least populous power by an order of magnitude, and entry only at nine years
old, by ear ([factions.md](factions.md)).

**Where people live.** In the instrument. A chapter-house is a formation of crystal with cells,
halls and a chord cut into it, and every wall is a face of something that rings: `#C9A6FF`
crystal glow through `#E6E9F2` alloy fittings, the light cold violet and the walls faintly
sounding under any pressure change — a storm, a passing hull, a door. The house is not the whole
instrument; outer formations stand off it, and the lattice the chapter sings against is all of
them together, sounded once a year and corrected for the year after
([mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §1). A Knight's berth is a cell in a chord. It is
dry, because crystal work requires it, and it is never silent, because crystal does not permit
it.

**The day.** Tuning, teaching, craft. The Order's ordinary work is the maintenance of nine
instruments and of the ears that play them, and a chapter-house day is organised around
intervals rather than tides — the house sounds its own hours. Children who passed at nine learn
crystal-craft, resonance mathematics and the liturgy of the Answering; those who did not have
gone home ([factions.md](factions.md)). Family names are retained across generations because the
families are finite ([culture.md](culture.md) §4), so a house is a handful of surnames, and a
Vrey who is not the Chapter-Master is one of eleven at the Third.

**The hush.** The chord itself — the hall at the formation's heart where the house is played. A
Knight's silence is a formal concept, not an absence ([culture.md](culture.md) §3), and the chord
is where it is kept: the one room in the house where nothing is struck and nothing is spoken,
so that the crystal can be heard settling.

**Three houses that are not like the others.**

- **The Third**, 1,450 m — the shallowest, the oldest voice of caution, Chapter-Master Vrey's
  seat, and the house whose ordinary work still happens in Mid-Water. Six outer formations, a
  tuning party of six hulls once a year, and *"Enter it, and go and be dry"* as the whole
  hospitality the Order offers ([mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md)).
- **The Ninth**, 1,900 m — the youngest and now the first: Choirmaster Sull's house and the
  Order's centre since her elevation, with thirty-six years of sealed transcripts in it that
  she has shown nobody ([characters.md](characters.md)).
- **The First**, 2,900 m — the deepest, where the Fields break toward the trenches, where the
  First Chord was raised in 178 PC and aimed down the trench axis into the Mouth. The Chord
  still stands; so do its three surviving technicians, who have not spoken since the reply and
  have not stopped writing. The First is half shrine and half hospice, and the Order does not
  discuss it ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). Its three rooms are described below.

**What it costs.** The Order cannot grow. Every berth in a chapter-house was cut for a family
that is still in it, and nineteen berths at Kalliso's house have been empty since 211 PC and
will not be filled ([characters.md](characters.md)). A chapter-house is the one habitat in the
Rift with more rooms than people, and the Knights are the one culture that finds that
unbearable rather than luxurious.

**What the Order calls beautiful.** *In tune.* A reply that arrives when it should. A lattice
whose drift, after a year, is nothing.

**A face.** Chapter-wright Anselm Vrey, b. 1,460 m, fifty-four, one of the Third's eleven Vreys
and no relation he can trace to the Chapter-Master closer than four generations. He has tuned the
Third's fourth outer formation for thirty years and has never left the Fields. He sat the
children's testing panel for twelve years and can still name every child who did not pass.

### The Conclave — nine chords, and the count is the order they answer in

*The room [campaign.md](campaign.md) §7 puts Chord 4 in: the vote Vrey has the standing to call.
The Directorate means another act by the same word — a crossing attended, not a chord struck —
at §6, The Upper Terraces; [glossary.md](glossary.md)'s Conclave entry carries both.*

**It is not a gathering.** Nine houses cannot convene. The Order cannot replace a hull or a
Knight ([factions.md](factions.md)), the houses are instruments and do not travel, and a chapter
that crossed the Fields to sit in a room would be spending the one thing it has none of. A
conclave is *sounded*. Each chapter-master strikes their own house's chord where they stand and
the lattice carries it: the outer formations a house tunes and corrects once a year are the
carriage, which makes the annual sounding ([mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md)) the thing
that keeps the Fields able to hold a conclave at all, and not only a piece of housekeeping.

**The room is the chord, in nine places at once.** The chord is the house's hush — the one hall
where nothing is struck and nothing is spoken (above) — and a conclave is the single exception to
that rule. To call one, a chapter-master breaks their own house's silence deliberately. It is
done perhaps twice in a generation, and it is understood as a cost paid before an argument is
made rather than as a procedure.

**The count.** A call is a struck chord at an appointed interval. The houses answer in turn, and
the count is the sequence: which of the nine answered, and how far into the interval each waited.
A house that lets its interval pass has abstained, and everybody hears the abstention, because
the Fields were listening at that moment for exactly that. A Knight's silence is a formal thing
and not an absence ([culture.md](culture.md) §3); a conclave is where that distinction has
consequences.

**What standing is, architecturally.** Maintenance. A chapter-master may call a conclave if their
house's lattice is in tune this year — if the outer formations will carry a struck chord to the
other eight as a call rather than as drift. Standing is therefore something a house can lose by
neglect and cannot buy, and it is held by the wrights: the Third has it because Anselm Vrey and
his like have kept the formations corrected for thirty years (above). Chapter-Master Vrey has the
standing and does not use it ([characters.md](characters.md)) — and not calling is not nothing
here. He is heard not calling, at every interval where the Third could have been.

**As a map.** Cut structure inside a Resonance Field, per §8: the chord authored as Coral Ruins
for its shadows, roofed water above it, the outer formations standing off across scattered PF
0.70 water where bearings lie. A defensive mission in a chapter-house is holding a room whose
loss is not a building — a house whose formations are broken has no voice in the next count, and
that is what an attacker on this ground is actually taking.

### The First — the shrine, the hospice and the sealed room

*The room [campaign.md](campaign.md) §7 puts Chord 5 in: twelve minutes at 2,900 m, and no combat.*

Three rooms in one house, at the Fields' southern edge where crystal country breaks toward the
trenches ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). The Order does not discuss the First, so what follows is
the building's account of itself and not a Knight's.

**The Chord.** Still standing, still aimed down the trench axis, and — the fact the Order does
not say out loud — still *tuned*. The house's wrights keep it playable, as they have every season
since 178 PC, which means the First Chord is not a monument and not a ruin but an instrument in
working order that nobody is permitted to sound. Chapter-Master Vrey built its lattice with his
own hands and now wants it shut down ([characters.md](characters.md)); it was corrected this
season regardless. A shrine in the Order's terms is a thing kept in tune, and that is the whole
of the First's discomfort.

**The hospice.** Not a place to die. In the Order's terms a hospice is where somebody is kept in
tune who can no longer sound, so the three live in cells opening off the chord, attended by
apprentices who share their work, keep their hours and transcribe. The care is craft: the same
courtesy a house pays an instrument that has stopped holding its pitch, paid to three people who
have not spoken since 178 PC and have not stopped writing ([timeline.md](timeline.md)).

**What is dry, and what that is for.** Crystal work requires dry, so the First is dry, and paper
survives in it. That makes the deepest house in the Order the one building in the Rift where a
hospice is also an archive, and it is the reason the writing exists at all rather than the reason
it is kept.

**The sealed room.** Thirty-six years of writing in three hands: more paper than is held anywhere
else in the Rift, sealed a case at a season by the house's own wrights, dated, and stacked in a
cut dry room off the hospice. The Choirmaster reads there alone ([factions.md](factions.md)), and
Ivane Sull was one of the apprentices in the room in 178 PC, so she has been going back to a
place she was already in ([characters.md](characters.md)). What the three write is described here
by its volume, its cases and its room, and it is not read out — not in this document, not in a
mission, and not in narration ([culture.md](culture.md) §6).

**As a map.** A quiet mission on this ground is authored with no hostile party in it: cut
structure as Coral Ruins for the cells and the sealed room, roofed water over the approach, and
the Chord as the one standing structure. The tension is that everything here is in working order
— an aimed instrument, a dry archive, three attended people — and the mission's whole job is to
let the player stand in a house where nothing is broken and nothing is safe.

## 6. Sufficiency — the city that announces itself by subtraction

*Abyssal, 2,750–3,400 m, the head of the Ninth Trench · Abyssal Directorate · PF 1.60 on the axis*

Terraced galleries cut into the slope at the head of the Ninth, housing the Undermarshalcy, the
Cantorate and the largest population in the deep. The name is the Directorate's highest word of
praise turned into an address, and the city means it: no debt, no wage, universal berth, every
citizen assigned, modified and belonging ([world-map.md](world-map.md);
[factions.md](factions.md)).

**Where people live.** In rock. A Sufficiency berth is a cell cut into the trench wall, one of a
terrace of identical cells, lit by red biolights in ordered rows — `#C2465E` on `#0A0710` — and
warm. The city buys no draw: it is heated and lit the way its hulls are grown, from Biomass, the
deep's own metabolism ([economy.md](economy.md); [bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §5), which is why a
Directorate city is dim, warm, regimented and never cold, and why the Consortium cannot switch
it off. A citizen can walk out of an airlock at 3,000 m and simply live
([factions.md](factions.md)), so the city's doors are open in a way no other habitat's are: the
galleries stand open on the trench, and the water is the corridor.

**The day.** Assignment. A cohort is assigned at birth, modified for a band and belongs to it,
and a day in Sufficiency is the band's work done in the band's water — and then attendance. A
Directorate city is quieter than the water around it: silence is owed, and here it is policy
and written down, so the city's whole sound is the ambient hush deepening and then red light in
rows ([world-map.md](world-map.md); [culture.md](culture.md) §5). Directorate citizens are
measurably healthier and report higher satisfaction than anyone in the Rift, and none of them
chose any of it ([factions.md](factions.md)). The city is not miserable. Writing it miserable
collapses the argument ([culture.md](culture.md) §6).

**The hush.** The attending galleries, the city's southern face, open on the Ninth's axis toward
the Mouth: stalls in rows, the Cantorate's dome standing over them, and a shift of cohorts
asleep and dreaming while four hulls hold the water outside
([mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md)). Attendance is compulsory, unpaid, and the best
part of everyone's month. The transcripts — 6,000 pages since 88 PC — are kept in the galleries
and published nowhere.

**What is dry.** Everything, equally, and none of it very. Universal berth means universal
damp, shared without grade: the Undermarshal's cell on the upper terrace runs exactly as much as
a cohort's on the lower rows, and this is the one fact about Sufficiency that Consortium
visitors find genuinely shocking.

**Two places that are not the city.**

- **The banding ground**, the upper Ninth at 1,500–2,400 m — the cohort halls cut into the north
  wall where a year lives during its intake, the shift on which it finds out whether the band
  it was made for is the band it can hold ([mission-intake.md](mission-intake.md) §1). The city
  is below; the year is above it, at the top of the water it will spend its life in.
- **The shallow band**, the First Trench at 1,800 m — the Directorate's shallowest holding,
  where the 8% of each intake who cannot hold their band are reassigned to labour
  ([factions.md](factions.md); [mission-exposure.md](mission-exposure.md)). Nobody dies. It is
  a posting, and it is the one posting in the Directorate that resembles the debt-berth they
  despise, and Korrin visits.

**What it costs.** Consent. Adze does not remember being asked ([characters.md](characters.md)).
A Sufficiency berth is free, warm, permanent, and was assigned before its occupant could speak,
and the game does not decide whether that is a life taken or a life given.

**What the Directorate calls beautiful.** *Sufficient.* A cohort that holds its band. A watch
that files its transcript without interpreting it. The return, heard, every cycle, and never
explained.

**A face.** Mara Tessen, 4th Trench Cohort, b. 2,900 m, thirty-one, assigned to the freight
galleries where the Fourth's shortcut meets the deep. She has attended one cycle in three every
month of her adult life and describes it as the best part. She has met a Consortium factor once,
in the Fourth, and told him she was assigned and he was sold, and has not decided which of them
had the better arrangement ([culture.md](culture.md) §3).

### The Upper Terraces — where not moving is the act

*The room [campaign.md](campaign.md) §6 puts Attending 6 in: fighting with half an army.*

**Where people are.** Two terraces at the top of the city, facing each other across the head of
the Ninth: the Undermarshalcy cut into the north wall, the Cantorate into the south, standing
directly over the attending galleries so that the Cantorate always sits above the hush it keeps.
The top of Sufficiency is its shallowest water, and shallow is the insult
([culture.md](culture.md) §3) — command sits nearest the way out, which every citizen reads as
obviously correct and no visitor does. Ossary was born at 3,100 m and now sits three hundred
metres above the water he came from ([characters.md](characters.md)); he could ask to be seated
lower and has not, because the Cantorate is seated where the Undermarshalcy can be answered.

**What a conclave is.** Attendance. The Directorate does not debate and does not count hands: a
question is put by the Undermarshalcy at a cycle, and it is answered by who crosses the open
water between the terraces to attend it. An assignment is sufficient when a cantor has attended
the calling of it — so the Cantorate's authority is not a veto, it is a body in a place, and the
whole of it can be exercised by remaining where it already is. The Order means another act by the
same word — a chord struck across nine houses and answered in turn, at §5, *The Conclave* — and
[glossary.md](glossary.md)'s *Conclave* entry carries both senses under their factions.

That is why the First Cantor does not move against the Undermarshal. He does not have to. Nothing
is refused, nothing is said that anyone could quote, no cantor crosses, and the cohorts whose
calling went unattended stay in their cells — not disobedient, not withheld, simply not assigned.
It is a civil war conducted in liturgical language by two people who are both right, and its
whole physical vocabulary is a distance and who is standing on which side of it.

**The hush.** The attending galleries beneath them, and the crossing itself. During a conclave
the terraces are quieter than the trench, so a cohort under way between them is the only sound
there is, and every arrival and every non-arrival is heard by the whole upper city. That is what
makes not moving legible as an act rather than as a delay.

**What is dry.** Nothing, equally, and not very — the Undermarshal's cell on the upper terrace
runs exactly as much as a cohort's on the lower rows (above). There is no state room in
Sufficiency and no bench grander than another bench. Rank is a depth of birth and a band, and the
wall says neither.

**As a map.** Two cut-structure regions authored as Coral Ruins (§8) on the north and south
walls, with open Abyssal Trench water between them and PF 1.60 down the axis, so a crossing is
heard the length of the trench. The galleries' written silence order — SIG 25, the debt recorded
([mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md) §3) — reaches the terraces above them. A mission
that fights with half an army authors the missing half as **never assigned** rather than as
casualties or as a reinforcement timer: the units the player does not have are a theological
outcome, and the room is where that is visible.

## 7. Halvard — the place that is not a habitat

*1,600 m, the west wall · nobody's*

A pre-Collapse habitat that imploded in 14 PC, the Rift's founding grave. The salvage that
recovered its dead chartered the Consortium; the wreck was left as the salvors closed it; by
unspoken agreement across all four powers Halvard is not entered, not mined, and not built within
a kilometre of ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). Crews cut engines passing it.

It is listed here because it is the rule the other six habitats are built against. A place that
fell is not resettled — not Halvard, not the drowned city, not Sector Kell — and every inhabited
structure in the Rift is a place that has not fallen *yet*. Underwriter Tull's father was a
Halvard salvor ([characters.md](characters.md)). The Rift's architecture is the salvors' habit,
two centuries on: hold what holds, and close what does not.

## 8. A Habitat as a Map

A mission set inside a city is authored in the same grammar as one set in open water
([maps.md](maps.md)), and three of the bible's mission maps already are. The rules they settled:

| Rule | Where it was settled |
| --- | --- |
| **Cut structure is Coral Ruins.** A city's halls and galleries are authored as the Coral Ruins biome — PF 0.80, hard acoustic shadows behind every standing wall — for a structure that is not ruined. The biome is the acoustics of *built space*, and a ruin is only the case where nobody maintains it | [mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md), *Sufficiency's Lower Rows*; [mission-intake.md](mission-intake.md), *The Cohort Halls* |
| **Roofed water is a ceiling.** A gallery, a transit tunnel or an overhang is water with rock above it, authored as a ceiling deeper than 0 m, so a hull can only be there by being *under* something ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md) §1). A city's interior is mostly this | [maps.md](maps.md) |
| **A habitat's hush is a silence order.** Where a city keeps its quiet, a mission holds the player to a SIG ceiling and writes the debt down — SIG 25 at the galleries, struck hardpoints at the court. The Directorate's is written; the court's is enforced; the others are courtesy, and a mission on a plateau or in the Holding should price them as such | [mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md) §3; [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §3 |
| **A city has a sound before it has a skyline.** The Holding is heard four minutes before it is reached; Sufficiency is heard as the hush deepening; a plateau is the turn-of-tide bell; a chapter-house is a chord. The ambient bed is the establishing shot | [audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md); [world-map.md](world-map.md) |
| **Neutral ground is ruined ground.** The places factions share are the places none of them built. A habitat is never neutral; a mission that needs all four parties in one room needs Sorrowgate | [world-map.md](world-map.md) §6 |
| **A dome that comes down is one event at one tick.** A mission beat may repaint a region's biome and collapse its geometry in the same breath — specified, built, and spent by two shipped missions: `attending-conclave` brings a dome down into Abyssal Trench, and `seeding-deep-furrow` turns a sown furrow to Kelp Forest, the one repaint in the bible that makes ground better. It is the only way a habitat is allowed to change during play | [environments.md](environments.md), *Coral Ruins*; [mission-conclave-attending.md](mission-conclave-attending.md) §13; [mission-deep-furrow.md](mission-deep-furrow.md) §13 |

## 10. Writing and Art Rules for Interiors

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Establish a habitat by its sound first — the hum, the hush, the bell, the chord — and its light second | Establish it by a vista. Nobody in the Rift has a window worth describing, and nobody looks ([culture.md](culture.md) §6) |
| Light interiors from the faction's own sources: pigment on the plateaus, hazard amber in the Holding, biolight rows in Sufficiency, crystal glow in a chapter-house — the four palettes in [factions.md](factions.md), no new hexes | Light a habitat from above. The only light from above in the Rift is the Lid's glow on the plateaus, and it is the poison |
| Show wealth as dryness: a room where paper survives, a wall that does not run | Show wealth as size or ornament. The Rift has neither to spare, and the one culture with spare rooms finds them unbearable (§5) |
| Let every habitat be a retrofit — plate over plate, cells cut into a working, a hull roofed into a vault | Design a habitat from a clean plan. The only planned city in the Rift fell in (§4) |
| Write the Directorate's cells as warm and the Commune's berths as damp, and let each culture find its own arrangement obviously correct | Write Sufficiency as a barracks or a plateau as a slum. Both are the outsider's reading, and every faction is right in its own register ([culture.md](culture.md) §6) |
| Quote a habitat's size in berths and its status in metres | Quote either in rooms, floors or population. A Rift citizen would not know what you meant |
| Keep the hush. Every interior has one room where the courtesy is absolute, and a scene set there is quiet or it is a shove | Stage a shouting match in a gallery, a chord, or the court. The register does not permit it, and neither does the water |

**The habitat test:** describe the room without naming the faction. If a reader cannot hear
whose it is, the room is not yet written.
