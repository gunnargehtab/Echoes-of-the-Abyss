# The Second Seeding 3 — Convocation

> The third mission of the Commune campaign ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §5), specified. The
> fifteenth mission document the bible carries, to the pattern
> [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) sets: everything here is authored — the
> forces, the water, the beats, the numbers and the text — and code transcribes this document.

**Setting:** Marr Plateau, the Rift's north shoulder, 214 PC, six tides after *Tend*
([world-map.md](world-map.md); [characters.md](characters.md)).

**Mission id:** `seeding-convocation` — namespaced by campaign after `seeding-tend` and
`seeding-thin-water`, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §1.

**This is the first mission fought on ground another mission has already been fought on.** It
is deliberately the same plateau and deliberately the same map literal, `marr-plateau`, and
§11 says why at length. [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 5 — "Drift Health carries between
missions on the same map… A player who fights loudly through the Kelp Labyrinth returns later
to a quieter, deader, more legible version of it, and nobody tells them why" — has been a
design rule with no mission behind it since the epic opened, because until now no two missions
shared ground. This pair is the first that could carry it, and the carrying is now built
against exactly this pair (§13).

## 1. What the Rows Are

**A bloom-collective has no chamber, and the reason is acoustic before it is political.**

Kelp eats the top of the spectrum at PF 0.55, and a voice does not cross two rows
([habitats.md](habitats.md) §2; [audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md) §9). So the plateaus
never built the room a vote would need — there is nowhere on a terrace where a thousand people
can hear one person, and two centuries of trying would not have made one. What they built
instead is a decision that moves.

**A convocation is walked.** It opens at one row and passes along the holdfast lines, every row
turning it and carrying it to the next, until the walk comes back to the row it started at with
nothing new in it. That closing circuit is the count: not a tally of hands but a question that
has stopped changing. A plateau that is **still turning** is one where the walk returned
altered, and it goes round again ([culture.md](culture.md) §3, *turning*). The rows are the
room, and a person turns it on the ground they are named for.

Three consequences follow, and the whole mission is made of them:

**A convocation cannot be hurried, because hurrying it is what it is for.** The circuit's length
is a physical fact about a terrace — seven rows, four kilometres of holdfast line, and a voice
that carries two rows. The campaign row's beat says the vote "takes exactly as long as it
takes", and that sentence is not a mood. It is the pace of the one institution in the Rift that
has no way to make a decision faster than people can walk one to each other.

**A convocation is loud, twice, and both times on purpose.** Convening it is the turn-of-tide
bell rung off-tide — the loudest deliberate act on a plateau and very nearly the only one, and
every neighbour hears it. A plateau that convenes has announced to the water that it has stopped
working. And the count is read at the southern edge, in the watch's own water, because the bare
slope is the only ground on a plateau that carries and a result has to reach everybody at once.
Nobody speaks to the watch while the watch is at the edge ([habitats.md](habitats.md) §2); a
convocation is the single exception the Commune has agreed to, and it is the one time the
plateaus break their own courtesy knowing they are doing it.

**A convocation leaves no record but the people who walked it.** Nothing is written, because
nothing on a plateau stays dry ([habitats.md](habitats.md) §2). A Commune decision cannot be
produced afterwards, cannot be entered at Sorrowgate, and cannot be checked against what anybody
now says it was. *Unfiled* means safe ([mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §1). It also means the
second Seeding will never be shown to have been authorised by anybody — which is the exact
property the concern has come here to enter in a ledger, and §6 is what that looks like from the
other side.

The question being turned today is the one that arrived at the end of *Tend*, carried plateau to
plateau at 15:00: **the plateaus are asked to turn a second seeding.** Anholt asks it. The count
will wait for every garden, and the water will not.

## 2. Whose Hulls the Player Commands

**The player commands Marr Plateau with its work stopped: the rows, the watch, and — for the
first time in this campaign — Warden Juno Teel's element, which has guns.**

Three things changed between *Tend* and this tide, and the force list is all three:

**The work has stopped, so the tenders are not tenders today.** A plateau that convenes stops
working, and everyone within twenty kilometres knows it has. The four tenders on the water are
not carrying share; they are carrying the question, drifting the holdfast lines at idle so a row
can turn around them. Their 8 SIG idle is the mission's working figure, and 18 is what the
player pays for moving one in a hurry.

**Teel is here, and Teel is armed.** *Thin Water* was the campaign's stated loss and Teel walked
out of it having got six of ten home; she has spent the tides since arguing that the Commune's
pacifism is a subsidy paid in other people's children ([characters.md](characters.md)), and
nobody has out-argued her. Three Commune Corvettes stand on Marr Plateau today because the
Warden put them there and no vote was taken about it, which is a joke the mission does not make
out loud and Marr notices anyway (§12).

**The button exists now.** [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 withholds active sonar until mission 3
of each campaign, and this is mission 3. It is not locked, it is not hidden, and §4 prices it.

**Engine bound, stated so nobody corrects it into a bug.** The player's force, the rows and the
Holdfast carry the Commune faction value; the assertion party carries the Consortium's. Two
factions, one mission, no lobby — a mission seats its own parties
([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §2 established the pattern).

### What the force does not carry

1. **No production, no construction.** `construction` is locked, with the reason in register:
   *nothing is built during a turning*. The Holdfast is on the map as the plateau's home and the
   mission's stake, not as an economy — the day's work is stopped, and a plateau that convened
   and then started a foundry would be a plateau that had not convened.
2. **No throttle**, for the third Commune mission running and for
   [mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §3's reason: their answer to "how loud should the work be"
   is a number they bred into the equipment. Today the work is not happening at all.
3. **No new hull.** Teel's element is the Corvette of [units.md](units.md) with the Commune's
   faction values, exactly as *Thin Water* fielded it. Nothing here asks for a roster entry.

**Silent Running is present, and this is the mission that finally prices it against the clock.**
`SILENT_RUNNING.PELAGIA_SPEED_MULTIPLIER` is 0.8 against everybody else's 0.55: the Commune pay
−20% for silence where the rest of the Rift pays −45%. In *Tend* that discount was a gift. Here
it is a tax, because the mission's currency is arriving somewhere before a row stalls, and a
watch scout that goes silent to cross the terrace unheard crosses it at 96 m/s instead of 120.
The plateau's cheapest advantage is the first thing the vote makes expensive.

## 4. The Walk

The system this mission teaches, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2: one system, load-bearing by
the last five minutes. The system is **Convocation — the walk, and the bell that collapses it**,
and it lands in four movements.

### 1. A row turns when the plateau is on it and the water is quiet enough

Seven rows, walked in the order §11 lists. A row **turns** when a live Commune hull has held
inside its 400 m for sixty seconds *and the row's water has stayed under the ceiling for all of
it*. When a row turns, the walk advances to the next; the seventh hands the count to the edge.

The ceiling is the point. A voice does not cross two rows, so a row that cannot hear itself
cannot turn — and the loudest thing likely to be standing on a Commune row is a Commune
Corvette.

**SIG budget: 26**, a ceiling, and unlike every Commune budget before it this one is *measured
at the row that is turning* rather than over the force. It is chosen to sit two above a moving
tender's 18 and two below a Corvette's 28, which means:

- a tender may hold a row, moving or still;
- the watch may hold a row at any speed;
- **Teel's element may not be on the row it is defending.** A Corvette at 28 breaks the ceiling
  by itself, silent or not — `SILENT_RUNNING.SIG_MAX` is 8, but a Corvette running silent is a
  Corvette that has stopped covering anything.

So the guns hold the row *next door*, and the mission is a positioning problem in which the
thing that protects a row is the thing that cannot stand on it. That is the Commune's entire
argument rendered as a rule the player's hands find in ninety seconds, and it is why §2 rule 1
does not need an evil Consortium to generate pressure.

### 2. Still turning

A row that cannot turn does not fail. It **stalls**, and the walk waits. Ninety seconds of stall
and the walk **returns altered**: every row already turned rings once, the circuit restarts at
the first row, and everything walked so far is walked again. Nothing is lost but the tide, and
the tide is the only thing there is.

Two things stall a row:

- **A hull that is not the plateau's, inside the row's 400 m.** An assertion hull standing on a
  row is the whole of the concern's method (§6). It does not have to fire. It has to be there.
- **The row's water over 26.** Which the player can do to themselves, and most will, once.

**The restart is the mission's telegraph and it is entirely made of bells.** Every row already
turned rings when the walk returns altered — five rows in, that is five bells across the
terrace, and the player learns what the sound means the first time it costs them six minutes.
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 asks that a failure be audible sixty seconds out; this is a
mission whose reversals are audible *the moment they happen* and whose one true failure state
(§8) is a Cruiser crossing a garden.

### 3. Mass mobility, which is what four tenders and seven rows means

The force cannot cover the circuit. Four tenders, two scouts and three Corvettes against seven
rows and 6,579 m of holdfast line means the plateau is always short somewhere, and the walk
always arrives at a row before the hull that has to be on it does. The mission is therefore a
continuous act of moving a small force around a large terrace ahead of a question — which is the
second thing [campaign.md](campaign.md) §5 row 3 says it teaches, and it is taught by scarcity
rather than by a tutorial.

The concern reads this arithmetic correctly and plays it: two Corvettes that never fight, moving
row to row, are worth more to the assertion than two Corvettes that win an engagement (§6).

### 4. Emergency convocation — the bell, and the line Marr has never crossed

**Marr's commander ability, and the reason this mission exists.**
[characters.md](characters.md) specifies it as *"All Commune units within 2,000 m gain +25%
speed and immunity to Silent Running penalties for 15 s. Usable once per match"*, and gives her
the conviction *"A power you'd use in an emergency is a power you already have"* and, under
**Hasn't crossed**, *"She has never invoked emergency convocation. It exists. It has never been
used."*

Diegetically it is one act: **every row's bell rung at once**, so the circuit runs in one tide
instead of several ([habitats.md](habitats.md) §2). Mechanically it is that act's two halves,
and the document specifies both because the character entry specifies one and the campaign row
promises the other:

- **The remaining rows turn together.** Every row still unwalked completes in one sixty-second
  turn rather than in sequence, provided each has a hull and each is under the ceiling. The
  circuit stops being a queue.
- **Fifteen seconds of a quiet army moving at speed**, for every Commune hull within 2,000 m of
  the Holdfast. This is where the arithmetic the roster has been quietly holding finally
  matters: the Commune's silent multiplier is 0.8, so immunity plus the bonus reads **0.8 → 1.0
  → 1.25**. Fifteen seconds of a hull that moves half again as fast as it was moving **and is
  still silent**. That is a different ability from fifteen seconds of a fast one, and it is the
  one the Commune's own numbers describe: the faction that pays least for silence is the faction
  for whom "immunity to Silent Running penalties" is nearly worthless *unless* the immunity is
  read as removing the multiplier rather than restoring it.

  **Which penalties immunity covers, stated so the literal does not have to guess.** The speed
  multiplier only. `SILENT_RUNNING` carries a SIG floor as well — `SIG_MIN` 3, `SIG_MAX` 8 — and
  the floor stays. A convocation makes the plateau fast; it does not make it inaudible, and an
  ability that did both would be the one mechanic in this game that is not an argument about
  sound.

**The price is that everyone hears it.** Every bell at once is authored at **SIG 70** — the
figure construction broadcasts at, and the loudest number this map has ever carried. Through PF
0.55 that reads Tier 1 to a Corvette's HYD 50 at **3,246 m** and Tier 3 at **1,831 m**: from
anywhere on the terrace, to everything on the drop, at a resolution that names the hull. The
plateau does not merely become audible. It becomes *identified*, and it identifies itself.

So the ability is a decision with a real invoice, taken once, by a woman who has spent thirty
years making the absence of that decision look like a strength — and taken by the player, on her
behalf, in a mission that is engineered so that most players will need it and some will get
home without it. §9 puts it in the last five minutes because that is where
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 says the mission's system must be load-bearing, and because a
line that is crossed early is not a line.

### The other button, and what it is doing here

Active sonar arrives in this mission because [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 says it arrives in
mission 3, and it is **available and not the mission's system**, because the same section's
first rule says one system per mission and this mission's system is the walk. Both rules are
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10; only one reading satisfies both; the document takes it rather
than inventing a third lock.

What the document does do is price it, because a button handed over without a price is a button
the campaign spent a mission withholding for nothing. `ACTIVE_SONAR.SELF_REVEAL_RADIUS_M` is
2,400 m at Tier 4, from an emitter at SIG 95. On this map, on this tide, a ping does three
things at once: it resolves 900 m of a terrace whose contacts the player can already hear at
leisure; it identifies the pinger to the entire drop; and it breaks a hush that the mission's
own plot is about breaking *on purpose, by vote*. There is nothing here the ping is the answer
to. That is the mission's argument about it, and the player is free to disagree by pressing it.

Whether the campaign should go further — whether the count read at the edge and the ping should
be the *same act*, so that the Commune's one deliberate loudness and the game's loudest button
are one mechanic rather than two that happen to be in the same mission — is a claim about this
mission's argument rather than about its scope, and §13 carries it as an open question rather
than settling it here.

## 6. The Assertion

**Two Consortium corvettes come over the lip at 03:30 and stand on the rows. A Cruiser joins
them at 09:00. Nothing in the concern's force fires on a row, a berth or a tender, and the
reason is not mercy.**

The concern's model, in 214 PC, is that a short war is cheaper than a slow collapse
([timeline.md](timeline.md)). Ninefold is in terminal decline and Item 9 is being read by a
fourth Board member; the plateaus are the one part of the Rift whose yield is not falling. A
garden entered in a ledger acquires a value someone else can cite, and *Tend* is where the
entry was made: **"A ledger is patient. We will be meeting that entry again."**
([mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §8). This is the meeting.

**The instrument is procedural and its procedure is the mission.** An assertion of interest must
be read at the ground it concerns, in the hearing of whoever holds it, and the ground held under
assertion until a decision is produced. The Commune keeps no files, classifies nothing, and
produces no decisions that can be produced. So the concern's method is not an attack. It is to
stand hulls on rows and wait, and to log — accurately, and with no malice whatsoever — that no
decision was produced.

Against a culture whose decisions are made by walking, standing on the ground *is* the attack,
and neither side can say so in the other's vocabulary. That is the campaign rule doing real
work: nobody here is wrong, and the Commune cannot explain why it is losing without conceding
the frame in which a plateau's assent is a document.

**Why they do not shoot the gardens, and why that is worse.** An asset the concern intends to
enter in a ledger is worth nothing burnt. The restraint is economic rather than moral, which is
the coldest sentence in this document and the truest thing in it. What the assertion *will* fire
on is a hull that obstructs it — Teel's element, if it engages, and a tender caught interposing
— because obstruction of a served assertion is the one thing the writ makes lawful. *Thin Water*
established the shape ([mission-thin-water.md](mission-thin-water.md) §6): the concern's guns
answer a question the Commune asked.

**What each hull is worth to the walk, measured rather than asserted.** Through Kelp Forest at
PF 0.55, against the watch's HYD 70:

| Hull | SIG | Tier 1 | Tier 3 (Classification) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Corvette | 28 | 2,259 m | 1,274 m |
| Cruiser, idle | 55 | 3,445 m | 1,943 m |
| Cruiser, systems live | 65 | 3,824 m | 2,157 m |

The map is 4,000 × 2,500 m. **A Consortium Cruiser on this terrace is audible from every row on
it**, and the mission never pretends otherwise: the player always knows where the assertion is,
and knowing is not the same as being able to be there. This is the inversion *Tend* promised and
this mission spends — a force this quiet hears everything, and hearing everything turns out to
buy exactly nothing when the problem is that a question has to be carried four kilometres by
hand.

## 7. What Is Heard

The plateau, with the work missing from it.

- **The absence of the day.** *Tend*'s soundscape minus the tenders under load: the same rows,
  the same Lid overhead, and the 18-SIG working hum that this player spent a whole mission
  learning simply not there. A plateau that has convened is a plateau that has stopped, and the
  campaign lets the player hear the stop before anybody explains it.
- **The bell, off-tide, once** — 00:00. The loudest deliberate act on a plateau, and every
  neighbour hears it. Anholt Plateau and Teel's landing both go quiet in the minute after,
  listening.
- **The pack at the foot of the drop**, working the lane traffic's leavings at 900 m, audible
  all mission and physically unable to climb ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §4). The same pack, in
  the same water, on the same map, six tides on: the sound of safety, which this mission is
  about to demonstrate has nothing to do with the thing that is actually happening.
- **The rows turning** — a low, plural, moving sound at 12 SIG, which reads Tier 1 to a
  Corvette's HYD 50 at 1,078 m: the concern can hear the question travelling, and cannot tell
  from the sound which way the plateau is turning it.
- **The assertion, read into the water** — 03:30, in the clear, in Consortium register (§12).
- **The Cruiser over the lip** — 09:00, and after that continuously, at 55 idle and 65 with
  systems live. The loudest thing that has ever been on this map.
- **The bells, when the walk returns altered** — §4. The mission's reversal, as a sound, every
  time.
- **Every bell at once**, if the player rings it — 15:30 or thereabouts, at SIG 70. Once. It has
  never been rung before, and after this mission it has.
- **The count, read at the watch's edge** — 17:00, at SIG 30 on the drop's trench paint (PF
  1.6), which is Tier 1 to a Cruiser's HYD 65 at **4,389 m**: off the eastern edge of the map
  and down the lanes. The vote becoming audible to anything listening up the drop is not a
  mistake in the design. It is the design, and [habitats.md](habitats.md) §2 wrote it down
  before this mission existed.

No Sounder, no hazard event, no new species. The mission's discipline is the same as *Tend*'s
and it is harder to keep here: refusing to add a threat to a design whose threat is that a
decision takes as long as it takes.

## 8. The Objective

**Close the count, and keep the Holdfast.**

Seven rows turned in order and the count read at the edge before the tide turns, on a terrace
the assertion is standing on. Everything in §4 is how; this section is what the mission does
with the result.

**The one failure state, and the sixty seconds that precede it.** If a hull of the assertion
stands inside the Holdfast for sixty continuous seconds, the plateau is **held**, and the
mission is lost. That is a real failure with a real telegraph, and the telegraph is the loudest
thing on the map: the Cruiser turns off the row line and stands toward the Holdfast at 13:00
with its systems live, at 65 SIG, resolving to the watch at Tier 1 from 3,824 m and — because the figure that matters is what
the *deaf* hull hears — to a tender's HYD 30 at Tier 1 from 2,252 m. From anywhere on the
terrace, to everything on it, for a full minute before it arrives.
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's rule is satisfied by the mission's own antagonist being
physically incapable of approaching quietly, which is the version of that rule this game was
built to be able to write.

**Everything else is an outcome, not a failure.** A count that does not close is a plateau that
is still turning, which is a thing the Commune has been for two hundred years and does not
regard as a defeat. The concern regards it as a result, and the two readings are both in the
epilogue because neither is wrong.

### Results

| Result | Condition | Marr's reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The count closed** | Seven rows turned and the count read at the edge before the tide, Holdfast held | "It came back with nothing new in it. That's a count. Whatever we've just decided, we decided it — and there's no paper anywhere that will ever say so, which is the same protection it has always been and the first time I've heard it sound thin." |
| **Still turning** | The tide turned with the walk unclosed, Holdfast held | "We're not refusing. We're still turning it. We've said that sentence to four generations of people who wanted an answer today, and it has never once been a lie. It has also never once been said with somebody standing on the fourth row." |

**The count's content is not an outcome, and the mission never asks the player to vote.** What
the plateau decides about the second seeding is not in this mission's gift — Anholt has the
votes and [campaign.md](campaign.md) §5 row 6 is where that lands. The mission is about whether
a decision can be *reached at all* by a culture that reaches decisions this way, while somebody
who reaches them a different way is standing in the room. That is why the outcome ladder grades
the walk and not the answer, and why the closed count's reading is the least comfortable of the
three.

## 9. Length, SIG Budget, and the Beats

**Length: nineteen minutes.** Inside [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's 12–25. The figure is
chosen against §4's arithmetic rather than picked: an unbroken circuit is seven rows × sixty
seconds = seven minutes of turning, so a plateau that never stalls closes at about 08:00, a
plateau that returns altered once closes at about 15:00, and a plateau that returns altered
twice does not close inside the tide unless the bell is rung. The mission is tuned so that most
players lose one circuit.

**SIG budget: 26**, a ceiling measured at the turning row — §4.

## 10. What It Teaches

One system, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10: **Convocation** — the walk, its ceiling, its
restart and its bell, in the four movements of §4, landing in order across the beat table: the
first row (01:00), the ceiling discovered by breaking it (03:30–05:00), the first *still
turning* (05:00), the shortage of hulls against seven rows all mission, and the bell (15:30),
which is where the system is load-bearing.

**Mass mobility is the same lesson from the other side**, which is why
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §5 row 3 lists both and this document treats them as one: a force
too small for its ground, a question that moves, and fifteen seconds once in which the plateau
can be everywhere it needs to be. The ability is not a speed buff bolted to a defence mission.
It is the only moment in the mission when the geometry stops being the enemy.

What this mission deliberately does not teach:

- **Seeding and terraforming** — mission 4, *Deep Furrow*, where the thing this plateau has just
  turned becomes a piece of the Abyssal that people can live in.
- **The Spore Veil as an instrument** — mission 5, *In Writing*.
- **Fighting your own faction's momentum** — mission 6, *Radicals*. This mission is careful to
  keep the count's *content* out of the player's hands (§8) precisely so that mission 6 is the
  first time the campaign asks them to feel something about what Anholt won.
- **Active sonar as a system.** It arrives here and is priced here (§4) and is nobody's answer
  to anything on this map. The campaign hands it over in the mission where the Commune breaks
  its own hush by vote, and lets the player notice the two facts sitting next to each other.

## 11. The Map

`marr-plateau` · **Marr Plateau** · one seat · 4,000 × 2,500 m · cell 250 m · base floor 320 m.

**The same map literal as [mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §11, unchanged.** Seven regions,
the same rectangles, the same trench paint on the drop, the same Holdfast spawn at 2,625, 375.
This is the document's most consequential scoping decision and it is made deliberately:

- **[habitats.md](habitats.md) §9 says the room is "the rows as the room"**, and *the rows* is
  *Tend*'s own word for Marr Plateau's farm terraces. Marr is the plateau Marr is named for and
  the Tidespeaker of; a convocation on a terrace the player has never worked would be a vote in
  a room with no memory in it.
- **[campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 5 finally has a pair under it.** Drift Health carries
  between missions on the same map, and until this mission no two missions shared a map, so the
  rule has been a design intention with nothing to intend. A player who worked this plateau
  quietly in *Tend* arrives here to the water they left; a player who did not, does not. That
  carrying is **built** (#379), and it was built against this pair — the point of choosing this
  map was that the rule would have a concrete first case to be built against, which is what
  every other unbuilt rule in this bible got before it shipped.
- **The alternative costs a map and buys nothing.** A second, unvisited plateau would need its
  own literal, its own gazetteer entry and its own establishing minutes, in exchange for ground
  the player has no memory of — which is the opposite of what "the rows as the room" is worth.

What this mission adds to the map literal is **markers, not geometry**: seven row markers and
the edge where the count is read. No new region, no new biome, no `ground` beat.

| Row | Marker (x, y) | Region | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1,625, 500 | The Gardens | The east end, where the walk opens. The bloom nodes *Tend* harvested, standing untended for a tide |
| 2 | 750, 875 | The Gardens | The west end, and the first row the assertion stands on |
| 3 | 500, 1,125 | The West Lane | The lane's head. The Tetherjelly clusters the player re-seated in *Tend* are here, and the −0.10 PF they bought is the one row that is quiet on its own |
| 4 | 1,125, 1,625 | The West Lane | The lane's foot, closest to the drop, and the row the concern holds longest |
| 5 | 1,875, 1,250 | The Terrace | Mid-terrace, between the lane and the Holdfast. Open, and there is nothing here to hide behind |
| 6 | 2,625, 625 | The Holdfast | Home's own row. If this row cannot turn, the Holdfast is contested |
| 7 | 3,375, 1,000 | The Terrace | The east rows, toward Teel's landing. The furthest from everything |
| — | **2,000, 1,875** | The Drop | **The watch's edge**, where the count is read. Trench paint, PF 1.6, and the reason §7's last figure is 4,389 m |

Row radius 400 m; hold sixty seconds; ceiling 26 SIG. The circuit — rows 1 through 7 and back
to the first — is **6,579 m** of holdfast line against a force of nine hulls, four of which
move at 40 m/s.

**Marr Plateau is a mission map and is not in the public catalogue.** One seat, not balanced,
resolved by mission id and nothing else ([maps.md](maps.md)) — and now resolved by two mission
ids, which is the first time that has been true.

## 12. The Briefing

Spoken by Tidespeaker Ysolde Marr, off-tide, after the bell. The Commune's register is defined
in [culture.md](culture.md) §3, and its refusal of the imperative mood is under more strain here
than in any Commune mission so far, because this is a briefing given while an instrument is
coming up the drop. [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 says the difficulty is the point, and this
document agrees in writing: a plateau that could give an order today would be a plateau that had
already lost the argument the campaign is making.

> We rang it off-tide. You'll have heard. Everyone will have heard — Anholt's people, Teel's,
> and the lane. That's not a slip. A plateau that convenes says so, out loud, and stops working
> while it does.
>
> Anholt asks us to turn a second seeding. We're turning it. It opens on the east gardens and
> it goes row to row, and it comes back when it comes back with nothing new in it. We can't make
> that faster. We've never been able to, and most tides that's been the best thing about us.
>
> The concern is coming up the drop this morning to read something at us. We think they mean to
> stand on the rows while they do it. They won't fire on a garden — a garden's worth nothing to
> them burnt, and we'd rather you heard that from us than worked it out.
>
> A row turns when there's somebody on it and it's quiet enough to hear itself. Juno's brought
> hulls. We didn't vote on that either. They're loud, so they can't be where the question is —
> the question has to be able to hear itself, and a corvette at twenty-eight is two rows of
> nothing.
>
> There's a bell for all of this. Every row at once. It exists. Nobody has rung it, and I've had
> thirty years to. We'd rather come home without ringing it. We're saying *rather*.

### Already seen: a filed plateau

[campaign.md](campaign.md) §1's rule again, on the same scene and from the other end of the
plateau. A player who came here from a *Tend* the sweep filed reads the briefing below instead,
and **the mission is byte-for-byte the mission** — the walk, the ceiling, the rows, the heavy
over the lip and the bell are untouched.

The changed paragraph is the third. The assertion read into the water at 03:30 says *this survey
year*, and a filed *Tend* is where that survey year's figures came from: the sweep took them off
these rows while the gardens were working. So Marr arrives knowing not just that the concern is
coming but that what they read will be *accurate*, and that its accuracy is the plateau's own
doing.

> The concern is coming up the drop this morning to read something at us, and we know roughly
> what. They have had our numbers since the sweep came up the lane and heard the gardens working,
> so when they say *this survey year* they will mean ours, and the figures will be right. That is
> the part worth sitting with before they get here. It isn't a claim they are inventing on the
> way up. We think they mean to stand on the rows while they read it. They won't fire on a garden
> — a garden's worth nothing to them burnt, and we'd rather you heard that from us than worked it
> out.

**Where the rule stops, stated because §13 asked for the other side of it.** An earlier draft of
this document's scaffold row wanted the assertion at 03:30 to change as well — Anholt's figures
in the concern's mouth, read differently to a plateau that had been surveyed. §1 refuses that,
and the refusal is the rule rather than a limitation of the build: a beat spoken inside the
mission is the mission, and a variant that moved it would make two players' *Convocations*
different scenarios wearing one name. The knowledge still reaches the player — it reaches them
before the drop, from Ysolde, where a plateau's knowledge belongs — and the assertion is heard by
both players identically, which is what makes it land differently for one of them.

### Objective readings, in play

The Commune cannot command, so its objectives arrive as statements of what the tide holds:

- *The walk's on the second row. It wants somebody standing there and it wants it quiet.*
- *That's still turning. It'll come round again — we've lost the walk, not the question.*
- *They're on the fourth. They're not shooting. They're just there, and that's the whole of it.*
- *The heavy's off the row line. It's standing toward the Holdfast.*
- *The count's ready to read. It goes to the edge, and the edge carries — everything on the drop
  is about to know what we sound like when we agree.*
- *There's the bell. All of them. Well.*

### The voices on the water

**The assertion, read into the water — 03:30**

> Asset assessment, north shoulder terraces, this survey year. An interest is asserted in the
> ground and yield of this terrace pending production of a decision by the holding party. The
> concern will hold the ground concerned while the assertion stands. Nothing is being taken.
> Nothing will be fired upon. This reading is the notice and the notice is complete.

**The watch, calling the heavy — 09:00**

> That's the big one over the lip. You'll not need us to tell you where it is. You'll not need
> us to tell you where it is at any point today.

**Warden Juno Teel — 15:00**

> I'm not going to argue with you, Ysolde. I've stopped. I'll just say the thing and then I'll
> go and stand where you'd rather I didn't.
>
> You can refuse to fight. You cannot refuse to be fought. They've been on our rows for eleven
> minutes and nobody has broken a rule, and we are losing. Ring it or don't. But stop telling
> the young ones that not having a way to do this is the same as having decided not to.

**Tidespeaker Ysolde Marr, if the bell is rung — ~15:30**

> All of them. Now, please.
>
> — and then, quietly, to nobody: "It was always going to be a tide like this one. I used to
> think that meant I'd know."

**Tidespeaker Ysolde Marr, at the tide's turn — 19:00**

> The reading of the count, per §8, and then one sentence she should not say aloud and does:
> "We built a way of deciding things that can't be rushed, because nothing that mattered had
> ever needed to be. I'd like the record to show we were right for two hundred years. There
> isn't a record."

Each line fails [culture.md](culture.md) §3 for the other three factions, which is that
document's own test (§6): the assertion is a threat with no threatening word in it, and its
"nothing is being taken" is sincere, which no other register could manage; the watch's call
refuses a bearing on the grounds that a bearing would be redundant, which the Consortium would
log and the Knights would tune; Teel's line is the imperative mood arriving in a Commune mouth
and being *apologised for in advance*, which is the register bending exactly as far as it goes
and no further; and Marr's last sentence is the Commune's proudest fact and its epitaph in the
same breath, spoken by the person who will be charged for both.

### One question this document does not settle

**Whether the count and the ping should be the same act.**

[habitats.md](habitats.md) §9 calls this mission "the first time the Commune's one hush is
broken on purpose"; [habitats.md](habitats.md) §2 puts the count at the watch's edge because the
bare slope is the only ground that carries; and [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 hands the player
active sonar in this exact mission. A reading follows, and it is a good one: a convocation is a
calling-together, active sonar is the loudest speech in the game, and Marr's whole
characterisation is a woman who will not raise her voice — so the ping and the vote could be one
mechanic rather than two that happen to share a mission, and the count could be *read as a ping*
with everything that implies.

§4 takes the other reading: the ping arrives because [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 says it
arrives, is not this mission's system because the same section says one system per mission, and
is priced as a button the mission gives the player no reason to press. Both campaign rules are
satisfied by that and only that, which is why it is what the mission is specified as.

But fusing them would be a claim about what this mission *argues*, not a scoping detail, and it
would change §7's last figure, §8's ladder and the shape of the ability in the row above. It is
a decision for whoever owns the Commune campaign's argument, and it is recorded here rather than
made here — the same way [mission-standing-wave.md](mission-standing-wave.md) §13 recorded the
corridor's PF against `MAX_PROPAGATION_FACTOR` for a person to settle, which #372 then did.

**It is still open, and the build did not need it.** #373 wrote the literal to §4's reading —
the ping arrives because §10 says it arrives, is not locked, and is priced by there being
nothing on this terrace it answers — and the count is an ordinary `extract` of a watch hull into
the edge, which is a different mechanism from the button in every respect. Fusing them later
costs one objective and one figure in §7, and nothing in the walk or the ability. The question
is a design call and stays one.
