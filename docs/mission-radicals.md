# The Second Seeding 6 — Radicals

> The sixth mission of the Commune campaign ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §5), built — one of
> the fourteen documents that complete the bible's campaign, written to the pattern
> [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) sets and the Commune documents continue:
> everything here is authored — the forces, the water, the beats, the numbers and the text —
> and the code that ships transcribes this document (§13).

**Setting:** Sorrowgate, the drowned city, mid-Rift, 214 PC, two tides after *In Writing* — the
arch down, the lock cut back, the court's chamber fallen since the spring, and the basin below
it awake since the same morning ([world-map.md](world-map.md) §3;
[mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §9; [characters.md](characters.md)).

**Mission id:** `seeding-radicals` — namespaced by campaign after `seeding-in-writing`, per
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §1.

**Premise, for the entry that offers it:** *The plateaus turned a second seeding. Nobody turned
the rim, and nobody turned this week. Anholt's people are going anyway, through the drowned
city, and Marr Plateau's guns are asked to go with them.*

**This is the first mission in which the player's own hulls are ordered by somebody else, and
the first the Commune plays on ground it did not tend.** Every Commune mission so far has put
the plateau's whole force under the player's hand and asked nothing of it but a number agreed
in daylight. Here four of the nine hulls the player commands are given their orders by the
Bloomwright, on her clock, from the barge she is aboard — a leg a minute, stood again at the
half, with a dive wherever the ground drops — and the player may countermand every one of them
for thirty seconds at a time. §4 says what that costs and §13 records that the format could do
it all along.

## 1. What the Commit Is

**The basin the city committed its dead into — 2,400 m of Abyssal Trench under the court's
fallen chamber, the ground all four cultures inherited the descent from, and the one road from
the north shoulder to the far south that runs through a place nobody built.**

Sorrowgate is the Surface Age's one attempt at a permanent deep colony, fallen in some year
nobody kept, and the Rift calls the whole ruin after the gate its dead went through
([world-map.md](world-map.md) §3; [habitats.md](habitats.md) §4). The prologue spent it: an
exchange, a ping, a colossus, an arch. This mission arrives many tides later and finds the
city as the prologue left it, because nothing here is maintained and nobody moves into a
place that fell. The plateaus are not moving in. They are moving *through*, south, toward
the rim, with the Kell seed and the barge that carries the programme, on the tide Anholt said
she would go, before any plateau had turned the rim or the week.

Three facts about that water decide the whole mission, and none of them is a fence.

**The arch is down and the lock is the way in.** At 10:40 of the prologue the span across the
chamber's northern row went solid and the service lock was cut back through it
([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §9), and the map literal cannot carry that: it
is a mission fact, so this mission fires the same two ground beats at 00:00 on the same regions
with the same values, and the city is as the spring left it (§11). The consequence is the
route: from the Descent into the chamber there is one door, 500 m wide, roofed at 1,300 m, and
a column that wants the basin goes under that roof at the pace a column goes. Fourteen came
out through it in the spring, or fewer. The court read the count and the plateaus never had it.

**The basin is awake, and it cannot be quiet.** A Sounder lives under the layer here
([habitats.md](habitats.md) §4), quiet for forty-nine years and answered in the spring. It idles
at 45 through the basin's 1.6, which is Track to a barge's HYD 65 out to 2,378 m and Contact
to 5,655 — from the moment the column is under the layer it has the colossus at a name and
then at a track, for the whole crossing, exactly as the prologue's flight did
([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) §13). The information is perfect. What the
column cannot do with it is the mission.

**The route is a stack of dives, each into a hunter's band and out the other side.** 330 m at
the Concourse; 850 in the Descent, which is Draymaw water (500–1,300 m); 1,475 through the
lock and the Gate, which is Hollow water (1,250–2,150); 2,300 across the basin, which is the
colossus's (1,300–2,700) — and each depth is ordered by somebody else, at forty-five metres a
second, at a SIG floor of 72 ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md) §2). Every dive is loud,
every dive lands inside the reach of something, and every one ends fifty metres or more under
the floor of the thing it passed: 1,475 is 175 m below anything a Draymaw will chase, and
2,300 is 150 below anything a Hollow will. The lane is drawn so that the column at the
Bloomwright's own figures is under the notice of everything in the water by four or five
points, and everything the escort owns is over it.

Nobody in the column says any of this. It is the ground the mission stands on.

## 2. Whose Hulls the Player Commands

**The player commands the seed column and the guns that go with it: four deep-rated hulls
with thirty-three aboard, ordered south by the Bloomwright; three of Warden Teel's corvettes,
live; and two ordinary scouts at the Concourse that cannot follow.**

The programme has six deep-rated hulls and has never had a seventh: two scouts refit in 204 PC
for the proof, and four seed hulls — the barge and three tenders — refit for the seeding.
Radicals fields the four. The two proof scouts are not here: they left for the rim ahead of
the column and are already on it, the charting pair [mission-prospect.md](mission-prospect.md)
§5 seats there, which fixed before this document existed that the plateaus arrived first and
mentioned it to nobody. The watch the player has today is the plateau's ordinary pair, PR-1,
and the Shelf is the whole of their water.

**Nobody commands the column, and the column is ordered anyway.** Bloomwright Sefa Anholt is
aboard the barge. She commands nothing the player commands; she orders the column, and her
orders are the beat table — every leg from 01:00 to 11:30 is a `move` beat addressed to the
four seed hulls, on whatever slot owns them, which is the player's (§4, §13). Tidespeaker
Ysolde Marr speaks the briefing from home, on the plateau channel, to the escort at the Upper
Concourse, and it is the only Commune briefing that names the thing it is not doing. Warden
Juno Teel is on the escort with three corvettes, live, and not struck for the first time since
the furrow ([mission-in-writing.md](mission-in-writing.md) §3); she was at this exchange in the
spring with three scouts and she knows the lock. The watch calls what it hears, from above the
layer. The column's crews are thirty-three people by household, and the epilogue reads them.

**Engine bound, stated so nobody corrects it into a bug.** One party and a court slot. The
column, the escort and the watch carry the Commune faction value on the player's slot; the
court's slot is reserved and empty, as every literal reserves it — and empty in the fiction
too, for the first time: the chamber fell in the spring and the record went up to the
Concourse with whoever went up, and this document does not say who. No second navy is in the
water. The Drift is not a party: three shoals, a pack, two Hollows and the prologue's colossus
are authored creatures on the Drift's own slot, and `fauna` is off. The map literal is
`sorrowgate`, the prologue's, unchanged (§11).

### What the column does not carry

1. **Mines, locked.** The reason in register: *not grown — a mine is a thing that waits, and
   the column does not.* The Commune's grown mines are its ambush kit
   ([systems-combat.md](systems-combat.md) §11), and an ambush is a thing laid by people who
   will still be there.
2. **Depth charges, locked** — *not grown.*
3. **Construction, locked** — *nothing is built in a place that fell.* No Bastion, no Foundry,
   no economy; the column earns nothing and rearms nowhere, so six torpedoes at the Concourse
   are six torpedoes at the far water.
4. **No throttle**, for the sixth Commune mission running and for
   [mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §3's reason. Nothing is harvested today.

### What it does carry, for the first time

Everything loud the roster gives a corvette, and the button. **Weapons live**: three guns at
550 m, 50 a cycle at 1.8 s, +25 on the discharge and +40 for two seconds if the hull was
silent. **Torpedoes**: two aboard each, SIG 60 for twenty seconds of run at 160 m/s, 3,200 m,
and — because a torpedo inherits its launcher's rating and implodes below it
([systems-combat.md](systems-combat.md) §5) — a corvette's torpedo runs at 1,790 m over the
basin and dies at 1,800. **Noisemakers**: 70 for eight seconds, twenty of cooldown, released
60 m astern. **Active sonar**: available, unlocked, priced in §4 as the one loud thing on the
panel that, alone, wakes nothing.

**Silent Running is present, and this mission divides it between two clocks.** Silence on
the column is the player's to set and a move order leaves it standing: a silent barge under
way is 7.6 at 36 m/s and a silent tender 4.5 at 32, and the Bloomwright's half-minute
re-orders do not touch it (`applyMove` writes an order and nothing else). What breaks it is
depth. `match.ts` clears Silent Running only for an order **deeper** than the hull
(`applyDepth`, "diving is not something you do quietly"), and the descent floors SIG at 72
for as long as it lasts — so the column is loud exactly three times on the Bloomwright's
clock, for 11.6, 12.8 and 18.3 seconds, and loud for one more dive every time the player
brings it up and the half-minute puts it back down. On the escort it is the ordinary trade:
a corvette running silent is one that has stopped covering anything, and one that breaks
silence to fire pays 40 on top of the 25.

## 4. Momentum

The system this mission teaches, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2: one system, introduced in
the first three minutes, load-bearing by the last five. The system is **momentum — the column
that does not wait, and the noise that covers it** — and it lands in four movements.

### 1. The order stands again

At 01:00 the four seed hulls are released and the beat table moves them to 2500, 700 at
330 m. At 01:30 the same order lands again. At 02:00 the next leg lands with a depth, and at
02:30 the same leg lands a second time. Eleven legs, each ordered at the minute and again at
the half, from 01:00 to 11:30 — twenty-two orders to each of four hulls — and every one is a
`move` beat, which the runtime applies to whatever slot owns the tag (`runtime.ts`, `fire`,
`sink.applyMove(Owner.slot[eid], …)`), unqueued, replacing the hull's whole plan. Nothing in
the format forbids a player-party tag, and no shipped literal has ever named one. This
document spends that as its whole system (§13).

The consequence is the mechanic and not a hole: **the Bloomwright's order is an ordinary
order, and the player may countermand it until the next one lands.** A player who wants the
column stopped, or elsewhere, or higher, gives that order and has it for thirty seconds; at
the half the table gives Anholt's again. Holding the column is therefore a thing done against
her, for half a minute at a time, by hand, and the price is arithmetic: every second the
column is held is a second later it stands on the row the basin crosses, and the basin's
clock (§6) does not stretch to meet it — three holds of twenty-five seconds put the column on
the lane at 12:25, which is the second the basin is on it (§9).

### 2. The column can be made quiet, and cannot be made slow — and every climb is paid for in a dive

The re-order costs nothing while the player agrees with it. `applyDepth` sets the target and
clears silence only when the target is deeper than the hull: a column already at 850 m is
re-ordered to 850 m at the half and keeps whatever silence the player set on it. A column the
player has brought up — to get its ears above a Hollow's band, to keep a corvette in step, to
be careful — is dived again at the half, at 72, for the difference. So the three dives on the
Bloomwright's clock are the column's loud minutes and the player's are on top of them: every
hold that climbs is one more descent at seventy-two, into the water that was just left.

The column cannot be slowed at all. Silence costs the Commune a fifth of its speed and the
tenders are the slow hulls at 40 m/s; the longest leg is 750 m, nineteen seconds; the lane is
4,571 m from the seat to the far water and the table gives it ten minutes. A column that
takes every order arrives at each waypoint inside twenty seconds and idles there, the barge
at 55 and the tenders at 18, for the forty that remain — which is the stretch a player can
make silent, and the stretch in which the Bloomwright's momentum is the sound of a big hull
waiting to be told again.

### 3. The basin attacks the loudest thing it hears, and re-chooses every half-second

The Drift's rule is the bestiary's first sentence about it: fauna target the loudest thing,
not the nearest ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §2). The engine's reading of that rule decides
this mission, so it is stated here in the engine's own terms (`fauna.ts`, `listen`,
`advanceStage`, `act`):

- A creature listens every **0.5 s** (`DRIFT.SENSE_INTERVAL_S`) and takes as its target the
  one thing it perceives loudest — by detection ratio, through the path's PF and the
  thermocline, measured horizontally — whether or not that thing is over its Interest. A
  Committed colossus with a quiet corvette and a live barge in its ears goes for the barge.
- The ping's ×3 (`ACTIVE_SONAR.FAUNA_AGGRO_MULTIPLIER`) applies only while the pinger's
  `ActivePing.remainingS` is above zero — **three seconds** (`REVEAL_DURATION_S`).
- From Ambient, a creature becomes Interested after **four continuous seconds** at or over
  Interest (`INTEREST_DWELL_S`; the counter resets to zero the tick the sound drops under).
  Interested, it is loud — a Sounder calls at 100 — and Committed the moment anything is
  Commit-loud, or after twenty seconds whatever happens (`COMMIT_AFTER_INTERESTED_S`).
- Committed, it stays so until thirty seconds have passed under Interest, or its target dies,
  or — a Sounder — it has passed through what it was going for; then Cooling, forty-five
  seconds at 100, drifting home, and **anything Commit-loud re-commits it at once**, with no
  dwell.
- A fresh battle mark within 800 m adds **15 × intensity** to whatever it hears best
  (`wreckBonus`) — a gun discharge lays 0.09 of intensity at its target, merged, so five
  seconds of three guns is a full mark, decaying with a ninety-second constant.

Three things follow and the whole of §6 is built on them. **A three-second ping is three
seconds of a four-second dwell**: alone, from Ambient, it wakes nothing on this map, and the
fourth second has to be something else — the pinger's own 28 at its own range, a noisemaker,
a shot. **A cooling colossus needs no fourth second**: for forty-five seconds after it lets
go of a target, one ping from inside 1,479 m brings it straight back. **And a fight lifts
everything nearby by fifteen** — which on this map means a fight at the western doorway,
550 m from where the colossus lies, and a barge on the lane that was 50.7 in its ears and is
now 65.7 (§6).

### 4. Be louder than the column, somewhere else

So the Commune's answer, for the first time in the campaign, is noise on purpose. Every
figure below is a corvette's tool through the basin's 1.6 against the colossus's HYD 90, with
the barge beside it for scale; the ratio at 100 m is the number the decoy's arithmetic is done
in, because two sounds compare as their ratios at their own distances and the ratio falls as
the 1.6th power of range.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The barge, systems live, under way | 65 | as long as it moves | 1,273 | 713 m | 587 m |
| The barge, idling | 55 | as long as it waits | 1,078 | 642 m | 529 m |
| The barge, silent | 7.6 | as long as nobody dives it | 149 | 186 m | 153 m |
| A dive, any hull | 72 | 11.6–18.3 s per dive | 1,411 | 760 m | 626 m |

| A corvette breaking silence | 68 | 2 s | 1,332 | 733 m | 604 m |
| A corvette firing from silence | 93 | 2 s | 1,822 | 891 m | 734 m |
| A noisemaker | 70 | 8 s | 1,371 | 746 m | 615 m |
| A torpedo, running away | 60 | 20 s | 1,175 | 678 m | 558 m |
| A ping, tripled | 95 × 3 | 3 s | 5,584 | 1,795 m | 1,479 m |

Read across: a cruising corvette out-shouts a barge with its systems live only from inside
**0.59** of the barge's distance, out-shouts a silent barge from anywhere inside **2.26** of
it, and a noisemaker out-shouts a live barge at equal distance by eight per cent. Read down:
nothing the column does on the lane is over the colossus's Interest — the lane is drawn at
750 m (§6) — and everything in the lower half of the table is, from a corvette that is closer
than the barge. The decoy is **ping-plus-position**: a ping is the only sound that reaches the
colossus from beyond 900 m, it lasts three seconds, and on the fourth the colossus is
listening for the loudest thing it can hear at its own distance, which is the corvette if the
corvette has stayed, and the barge if it has not.

And the corvette is safe. The colossus bites no hull at all — `act` returns before damage for
any non-structure it is a Sounder — and grinds by transit only hulls of 95 m and up, within a
body plus a radius in three dimensions (`transit`, `TRANSIT_MIN_HULL_M`). An 80 m corvette can
call the largest thing in the Rift onto itself because it is too small to be worth its
notice, and a 130 m barge cannot be inside 102.5 m of its line at its depth without losing
1,500 points of hull in the seven seconds it takes to pass. That is Drenn's mistake, made
deliberately, by the faction that never pings: the prologue's underwriter called the basin
onto a Cruiser; the plateau calls it onto a corvette, from beside a Cruiser, and the whole
question is which of the two it finds louder when the three seconds are over.

### The SIG budget

**SIG budget: 65** — the barge with its systems live: the momentum's own figure, and a ceiling
the player is meant to exceed on purpose. *Tend*'s twenty and *Convocation*'s twenty-six were
ceilings kept; *Thin Water*'s thirty was a ceiling broken by firing; *Deep Furrow*'s
forty-five was the number the day was for; *In Writing*'s eight was a posture. This one is the
loudest hull the plateaus own, moving, and the mission is playtested per
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 against the player who exceeds it — by breaking silence to
fire at the doorway, by dropping a noisemaker in the Descent, by pinging the basin to find out
what is in it and then doing something on the fourth second.

## 6. The Basin

### The colossus, placed and not driven

`the-colossus` is a Sounder placed at 00:00 at 1750, 3650 at 2,000 m — over the Commit's
2,400 m floor, inside its 1,300–2,700 m band — with `driveTo` at its own spawn and

which hands it to its own trigger model on the first pass. It is the Drift's from tick zero.
HYD 90, Interest 55, Commit 75, idle at 45, calling at 100, 30 m/s across the floor and
12 m/s up or down it (`DRIFT.VERTICAL_SPEED_MPS`), 9,000 hull that six torpedoes would not
half spend.

## 10. What It Teaches

One system, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10: **momentum** — the order that stands again,
the column that can be quieted and not slowed and pays for every climb in a dive, the basin
that attacks the loudest thing and re-chooses every half-second, and the plateau's answer,
which is to be louder than its own column somewhere else — in the four movements of §4,
landing in order across the beat table: the first order (01:00), the first dive taken by the
table (02:00) and the rule learned by ordering the column up and hearing it dive again
(02:30), the lane beside the colossus (10:00) where four points of margin are the whole
crossing, and the basin rising (12:00), which is where the system is load-bearing — a column
held against the Bloomwright is a column on the lane when the lane is crossed.

**Underneath it, the campaign's subject continued.** *Tend* taught that quiet is a livelihood;
*Thin Water* that the water can stop protecting it; *Convocation* that the arrangement cannot
decide anything faster than people can walk; *Deep Furrow* that a garden can be safe by depth;
*In Writing* that a plateau can grow a silence other people are deaf inside. Radicals turns
the same people against their own faction's momentum and finds that the momentum is right
about the water: the column that goes gets through, and the escort's job is not to protect it
but to not be the thing that wakes the basin on it. The Commune's oldest skill is doing
nothing loudly enough to be heard doing it. The Bloomwright's is doing something. The mission
hands the player both and asks which they reach for with three guns on the panel.

### One question this document does not settle

**Whether the player should be able to countermand the column at all.** §4 spends the fact
that a `move` beat is an ordinary order and the player may overwrite it for thirty seconds at
a time, and builds the mission's price on it: every hold is a hold against the Bloomwright,
paid in time, and every climb is paid in a dive. The other reading is stricter and more
Commune — the column is not the player's to touch, the beat table is the whole of its will,
and the escort's only verbs are noise and position — and it would need one row the format has
never carried: a player-party hull whose orders the player cannot give, which is `releaseTick`
made permanent, and which no document has yet asked for. That mission is *In Writing*'s
grammar with the guns live, and it is a good mission; it is not this one, because a column
the player cannot hold is a column the player cannot fight, and the campaign row says
*fighting*. It is recorded here rather than made here, the way
[mission-convocation.md](mission-convocation.md) §13 records the count against the ping.

**What the build adds to the question, without settling it.** The permissive reading is now
playable, and its extreme is measured rather than imagined: the test plays a column countermanded
on every Echo pass — all four hulls ordered back to the seat, which is the only cadence that beats
a table re-ordering them twice a minute — and the mission closes at 15:00 as Lost with
*the-escorts* and *the-households* both Met. Thirty-three people alive, nobody in the basin, and a
count that still reads short. That is the outcome the stricter reading would delete, and it argues
both ways at once: a player can refuse the crossing outright and lose nobody, which is either the
whole of the escort's dilemma or a hole in it. The question is the same question; it now has a
number against it.
