# The Pelagion Rift — Geography & Gazetteer

> The canonical map of the setting: the Rift's shape, its vertical order, and every named place in the bible, each with its depth, its allegiance, and its sound. Drawn as [Plate VII](concept-art/plate-07-rift-chart.png) in the Pressure Cartography language ([art-direction.md](art-direction.md)); this document owns the facts, the plate transcribes them. Where the plate errs — it draws Kell Plateau east of Teel, away from Sector Kell's fence (§4), and Fivewell deeper than the Ninefold Vein — §3, §4 and §7.2 win. [world.md](world.md) says what broke and why humanity is down here; this document says where *here* is. Skirmish maps are cut from this ground ([maps.md](maps.md)), the campaign is placed on it (§5), and §7 gives it one metric frame for the continuous 3D world.

A rule before the geography: in a game where nobody looks and everybody listens, **a place is its sound**. Every entry below carries an acoustic identity, because that — not its skyline — is how a Rift inhabitant knows they have arrived somewhere.

---

## 1. The Shape of the Rift

The Pelagion Rift is a branched tectonic scar roughly **ninety kilometres end to end** and never more than forty wide — one deep valley system holding, as far as anyone can demonstrate, the whole of surviving civilisation. Whether anyone else survived in some other refuge is a question the Rift has stopped funding ([world.md](world.md)).

The Rift runs **north–shallow to south–deep**, and that single gradient organises everything:

- **North**, the Rift rises to meet the Shelf: kelp plateaus in the first clear water under the Lid. The Commune's country.
- The **west wall** carries the vent fields and the Consortium's vertical city, joined by the thermal grid.
- **Mid-Rift**, where the Shelf breaks toward deep water, stands the drowned city — the Surface Age's one permanent colony, now the Coral Ruins and the neutral ground.
- The **east slope** below the thermocline is crystal country: the Resonance Fields and the Knights' nine chapter-houses.
- **South**, the floor steps down through the trench country — nine trenches, counted downward — into the Directorate's bands.
- At the far southern end, below everything, is **the Mouth**.

Depth is status, so the map is also a social diagram: the Rift's whole hierarchy is legible as latitude. An insult in the north ("shallow") is a compass direction everywhere else.

## 2. The Vertical Order

The water column, from the poison down. Band boundaries are the ruleset and identical on every map ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md)); everything else here is the world.

| Depth | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| 0–150 m | **The Lid** — the sour surface layer, dead since the Collapse ([world.md](world.md)). A mechanic since the three-layer revision — a hull up there runs the sour timer ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md) §2) — and no map invites anyone into it |
| 0–400 m | **Shelf band.** The first clear metres under the Lid down to 400 m. Kelp plateaus, Commune gardens, the cheapest berths |
| 400–1,800 m | **Mid-Water band.** The contested middle: the Holding, the vent fields, the drowned city, the shallower chapter-houses |
| 1,200 m | **The thermocline** — not a place but a divide: sound crossing it is cut to a whisper, and the column above and below are, acoustically, different worlds ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §3) |
| 1,800 m and below | **Abyssal band.** The trench country, the deep chapter-houses, crush attrition for the unrated, and the Directorate at home |
| 4,410 m | **The Mouth.** The floor of the setting, in every sense |

## 3. Gazetteer

Places are grouped by region, north to south. Depths of birth quoted for characters throughout the bible ([characters.md](characters.md)) are addresses on this map.

### The Plateaus — the Rift's north shoulder · Pelagia Commune

Kelp forest terraces in the Shelf band, 200–400 m, stepped down the northern rise like fields on a hillside. The oldest continuously inhabited ground in the Rift, and the only place in the setting a person would call beautiful without being corrected: kelp columns forty metres tall swaying in the tidal draw, bioluminescent trails marking the lanes between farm rows, and the pale ceiling of the Lid glowing faintly overhead at midday — the closest thing to a sky anyone down here has stood under.

**Sound:** the quietest inhabited water in the setting. A working plateau harvests at 18 SIG ([economy.md](economy.md)), kelp absorption eats what little that carries (PF 0.55), and the Commune regards ambient hush as a shared possession — a loud neighbour is a social problem before it is an acoustic one.

- **Marr, Anholt, and Teel plateaus** — three of the great garden terraces. Commune custom names a person for the plateau they tend ([culture.md](culture.md) §4), so the bible's Commune figures are walking map references: Tidespeaker Ysolde **Marr** (b. 320 m), Bloomwright Sefa **Anholt** (b. 210 m), Warden Juno **Teel** (b. 380 m).
- **Kell Plateau** — the terrace that shared a name and a fate with the Consortium sector beside it. The 197 PC flood front crossed it; 4,000 farmers got out, 200 did not ([timeline.md](timeline.md)). Replanted since, and the kelp grows, but the Commune has never renamed it and never will: a plateau's name is its people's, and Kell's people include the two hundred.
- **The Enclosure** — the densest kelp stand in the Rift, half farm and half maze, where the Commune held the 199 PC standoff without firing — and where its refusal to fight was paid for in its own dead, Tidespeaker Marr's partner among them ([timeline.md](timeline.md)). The **Kelp Labyrinth** archetype is modelled on ground like this, not cut from it: its floors lie far below any plateau ([maps.md](maps.md)).
- **The Kell Shoulder** — a four-kilometre crossing in the Shelf band between Kell Plateau and Marr, under two kilometres of it bare scoured rock between the last kelp of each: a rise too shallow for the trench country's cold and too swept for kelp to take, the shortest way between the two plateaus, the only ground in the Commune's own country that the Commune's numbers do not describe, and the one Shelf ground in the Rift with no plateau on it. The rise stands at 340 m; the Consortium's grid spur crosses it east to west under a posted closure at 420 m, a vent under-run masks the corridor's middle at 620 m, and at the southern edge the Kell slope falls away to 900 m and keeps falling ([mission-thin-water.md](mission-thin-water.md) §1; [mission-shallow.md](mission-shallow.md) §1, §11). **Sound:** Open Water, PF 1.00. Eighteen carries eighteen here, which is why a harvest column caught on the shoulder dies in the open, and why a Directorate cohort can climb it without standing in a garden.
- **The Furrow** — the one piece of Abyssal water under the plateaus, and the one nobody was born to: a cleft where the scar branches beneath the north shoulder under Anholt's terrace, dropping from the Mid-Water lanes — the Foot, where the plateau's lane comes down, at 900 m — through the thermocline to a floor at 2,200 m, and on to a sill at 2,600 m where the cleft opens to the deep — the observer's station, off the end of the garden ([mission-deep-furrow.md](mission-deep-furrow.md) §11). It is the ground Bloomwright Sefa Anholt seeded in 204 PC to prove that habitable depth can be manufactured ([timeline.md](timeline.md)): one furrow of trench floor, ten years grown, a green terrace on bare rock lit from its own pigment, one row wide; the campaign sows the second furrow beside it ([mission-deep-furrow.md](mission-deep-furrow.md) §1, §11; [mission-in-writing.md](mission-in-writing.md)). **Sound:** the layer's. Home cannot hear the furrow and the furrow cannot hear home; the seeded floor absorbs like kelp (PF 0.55) and the bare cleft beside it carries like a trench (1.60), so the quietest garden in the Rift stands in the loudest water under the plateaus.

### The West Wall — the industrial shore · Bathyarch Consortium

The Rift's western face from the Shelf break down to Halvard at 1,600 m — and, under the Holding's overhang, to the Underworks' floor at 2,100 m — veined with geothermal fields and strung with the **thermal grid** — the pipe-and-cable spine, built 71 PC, that carries vent heat and power to every habitat that pays for draw ([economy.md](economy.md)). Whoever runs the grid runs the air, and the grid runs along this wall.

**Sound:** the loudest region in the setting, permanently. The wall hums — vents, pumps, freight screws, shift klaxons — and the Consortium long ago stopped apologising for it. Grid workers navigate home by pump signature the way the Commune reads kelp lanes.

- **Holding One** — the Consortium's capital: a vertical city built down the wall face, berths from 350 m to 1,300 m, universally called *the Holding*. Asset number 001, the first entry in the registry, and the registry is the point: nothing in the Holding is unnumbered. Depth is grade, literally — junior berths at the top, Board country at the bottom, and a promotion is measured in metres of descent. Executor Varr-Kest (b. 1,180 m) was born four floors above where she now sits. Board country ends in the **Underway**, Asset 002, half a Surface Age transit hall the first concessionaires roofed their vault into, where the Ninth Board has met since there was a Board ([mission-item-nine.md](mission-item-nine.md) §1). And the city has a root below its berths: the **Underworks**, the Surface Age vent works the Holding was roofed onto — galleries and castings poured before year 0, roofed water only between 1,900 and 2,100 m, reached through one open shaft and priced in crush for any working hull in the city ([mission-tolerance.md](mission-tolerance.md) §1; [habitats.md](habitats.md) §3). The registry calls it the Underworks; the engineers call it the root; the Holding is heated from it, and it is two centuries older than the concern that depends on it.
- **Sector Kell** — the Holding's shallowest berth-sector, at 350 m: the cheap berths, the debt-berth families, the top of the city. In 197 PC a containment failure threatened the habitat entire, and Varr-Kest signed the order to flood Sector Kell to save the rest. 1,900 dead, the habitat saved, the flood front across the neighbouring plateau ([timeline.md](timeline.md)). The sector is sealed, unlit, and still on the registry — the Consortium does not strike assets, even the ones it killed.
- **Sector Vayle** — the berth-sector at 1,050 m whose bulkhead frame stands where a cascade from the Underworks reaches the city: eleven hundred berths, and the section the Ledger's fifth mission weighs against the root ([mission-tolerance.md](mission-tolerance.md) §1; [habitats.md](habitats.md) §9).
- **Ninefold Vein** — the founding field, producing since before year 0, on the corporate seal, and dying. Nine vents on a fault line at 900–1,400 m; workings run by Foreman Corwin Osk (b. 940 m), second-generation debt-berth. Terminal decline since 209 PC, eleven years to insolvency, and the reason the Consortium is at the Rim ([factions.md](factions.md)).
- **Fivewell** — the youngest producing field, shallower and smaller, the grid's second pillar and nobody's romance: Fivewell was drilled by the book after Ninefold taught the book. The **Ventfront Divide** archetype is cut from ground like this ([maps.md](maps.md)).
- **Halvard** — a pre-Collapse habitat at 1,600 m that imploded in 14 PC, the Rift's founding grave. The salvage that recovered its dead chartered the Consortium five years later, and the wreck has been left exactly as the salvors closed it: by unspoken agreement across all four powers, Halvard is not entered, not mined, and not built within a kilometre of. **Sound:** none. Crews cut engines passing it, which in the Rift is what reverence is.

### The Drowned City — mid-Rift · neutral ground

Where the Shelf breaks toward deep water stands the Surface Age's one attempt at a permanent deep colony: a civic city with a transit line, a passenger terminus, and a hydrophone array larger than anything any faction has built since ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md)). It was half-finished at year 0, it took the descent's whole weight in the overload decades, and it fell in some year nobody kept. **Its name did not survive it.** The Rift calls the whole ruin Sorrowgate, after the gate its dead went through. The Coral Ruins biome is the Surface Age's drowned works wherever they stand — failed colonies, dead stations, the transit line's outlying stops — and this city is chief among them: broken domes, collapsed tunnels, coral-clad colonnades ([environments.md](environments.md)).

**Sound:** occluded and untrustworthy. PF 0.80 with hard acoustic shadows behind every standing wall — the one region where a listener can be wrong about what is fifty metres away, which is precisely why the neutral ground is here and why the campaign keeps returning to it ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §10).

- **Sorrowgate** — the Arbitration Court: a collapsed transit dome at 1,500 m, under the thermocline, where Arbiter Mosk Halloran has kept the Rift's only neutral record since 165 PC. All four powers use it; all four deny using it. Specified in full, to the metre, in [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md).
- **The Upper Concourse** — the old passenger terminus at 340 m, above the layer: the drowned city's front door, and the prologue's way out.
- **The Commit** — the basin below the city, dropping to 2,400 m, where the transit line once carried the city's dead out over deep water. All four cultures still commit their dead downward, and this is the ground they inherited the practice from ([culture.md](culture.md) §5).
- The **Sunken Metropolis** archetype is cut from the city's districts ([maps.md](maps.md)).

### The Resonance Fields — the east slope · Hadron Knights

Crystal country: fields of natural resonant crystal seaming the eastern slope from 1,400 m down to 2,900 m, below the thermocline almost entirely. The crystal formations ring under any pressure change — a storm, a passing hull, a distant detonation — so the whole region sounds faintly and continuously, like a struck instrument that never quite settles. Bearings lie here (PF 0.70, scattered), pings return phantoms, and the Knights hold it less as territory than as a musical instrument they live inside.

**Sound:** the ring. Knights can read the Fields' ambient chime the way the Directorate reads silence; everyone else hears a haunted room.

- **The nine chapter-houses** — each built *into* a resonance formation, each a physical instrument tuned over generations, numbered First through Ninth in order of founding. The Order founded deep — the First house, the oldest, stands nearest the Mouth, which is the Order's whole doctrine in one architectural fact — and the houses since were cut where the crystal was, so the number is an age and not a depth. The **Third Chapter-House** (1,450 m) is the oldest voice of caution — Chapter-Master Halden Vrey's seat. The **Ninth** (1,900 m), four hundred and fifty metres below it, is the youngest and now the first: Choirmaster Ivane Sull's house, and the Order's centre since her elevation ([characters.md](characters.md)).
- **The First Chapter-House** — the deepest, at 2,900 m, at the Fields' south-west corner, where the scar ends and crystal country breaks toward the trenches. The **First Chord** was raised here in 178 PC and aimed down the trench axis the Ninth carries into the Mouth (§4) — the one alignment in the Rift where PF 1.6 water carries a transmission that far ([environments.md](environments.md)). The Chord still stands. So do its three surviving technicians, who have not spoken since the reply and have not stopped writing ([timeline.md](timeline.md)); the First is now half shrine and half hospice, and the Order does not discuss it.
- **The Seam** — the Fields' southern boundary, running east from the First along the margin. Not a cliff: a strip of trench-painted floor along the margin at 1,750–1,780 m, PF 1.60, the one line of ground on the Order's side of the water that carries, and the edge every Knight map on the southern margin ends at ([mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §11; [mission-standing-wave.md](mission-standing-wave.md) §1).
- **The Fifth** — a canyon in crystal that rings at a fifth, and has been called that for a hundred and forty years by people who meant it literally — the road and its name are older than the Order, which was founded in 118 PC and inherited both ([timeline.md](timeline.md)): three kilometres of crystal-walled defile, a kilometre wall to wall at the narrows, running north from the Seam up into the Third's country — rim 1,450 m, floor 1,700 m, its South Mouth opening onto the Seam at 1,780. The only covered road between trench country and the northern slope; everyone has always used it, and the Order has never written down that it is theirs ([mission-standing-wave.md](mission-standing-wave.md) §1, §11). **Sound:** covered in the Order's sense — PF 0.70, scattered — with a trench at its bottom end, so anything loud in the Fifth is heard down the axis by whatever is further down than either.
- **The Rest** — a trench head on the Fields' southern margin where crystal country meets trench country, the ground the 211 PC crystal raids were fought over ([timeline.md](timeline.md); §4 below): the Head at 1,600 m, the shallowest ground; the Order's bench at 1,750 m, the last fifty metres of a PR-2 rating; the trench floor at 2,150 m, four hundred metres under the bench; the Deep End at 2,400 m, where the trench falls east along the margin and then turns south toward Directorate country, as every trench falls. Nineteen Knights went down here in 211 PC and lie on that floor, out of reach of a PR-2 hull. Until 211 the trench was named for the interval it rang at; the Order renamed it for a rest — the part of a chord that is silent on purpose — and does not discuss the renaming. Those below have a number for the trench, in a record the Order is not shown, and this document does not give it ([mission-nineteen.md](mission-nineteen.md) §1, §11).
- The **Crystal Convergence** archetype is cut from the Fields' central formations ([maps.md](maps.md)).

### The Trench Country — the southern deep · Abyssal Directorate

South of the thermocline's reach the floor stops sloping and starts *stepping*: nine trenches, counted downward — the First at 1,800 m to the **Ninth**, whose axis runs below 3,400 m toward the Mouth's approach. **A trench is numbered by where it ends, not where it begins.** Every one of the nine is a canyon that is entered high and falls south, and the count is the order of their floors: the First's is the shallowest floor of the nine — 1,800 m under a worked rim at 1,750, falling to 2,400 at its deep end — and the Ninth's is the deepest, though the Ninth's *head* at 1,500 m stands above the First's rim, and the Fourth's freight pipe at 1,700 does too. The head of a trench is where it is entered; the number is where it goes (#421). Trench walls channel sound impossibly far along each axis (PF 1.60): the trenches are corridors in every sense, no secrets down their length, only distances. The Directorate's cohorts are named for their trench — Cohort-Prime Adze (b. 3,400 m) commands the **9th Trench Cohort**, the deepest standing formation in the Rift.

**Sound:** discipline. Directorate settlements are quieter than the water around them — silence is owed, and here it is also policy. A Directorate city announces itself by subtraction: the ambient hush deepens, and then there are red lights in ordered rows.

- **The Ninth, head to Mouth** — the profile every Directorate ground is a point on, written once here so the missions can stop disagreeing about it (#421). The trench is entered from under the duct at the **banding ground**, 1,500–2,400 m, a stair of cohort halls down its north wall; the stair ends at the head of the trench proper, where **Sufficiency** is cut into the slope at 2,750–3,400 m; the **attending galleries** open off the city's southern face onto the axis at **3,000 m** — the deepest water any hull in the Rift is ordered into, and the ruleset's ceiling ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md) §1; [mission-conclave-attending.md](mission-conclave-attending.md) §11). Below the galleries the axis is 3,400 m and deepens south to **4,100 m** at the sill, three kilometres on, where it leaves the galleries' map — water nothing of the Rift's stands in, and the water the return comes up out of ([mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md) §6, §11). Then the trench *rises*. Over its last eight kilometres the floor climbs a thousand metres to meet the Mouth's northern edge at the Lip, **3,100 m**, and the walls open out into the one trench that has no far wall (§4 below; [mission-first-arrival.md](mission-first-arrival.md) §1). So the Ninth is deeper in its middle than at either end: a column walking it to the Rim goes down out of the city and up onto the edge, which is why the Directorate arrives at the Mouth *along* it, at 3,000 m, on the only side anybody was ever born on. Eleven kilometres, galleries to Lip. **Sound:** one corridor at PF 1.60 the whole length, and the rise at the end hides nothing — a trench carries over a sill as it carries over a floor.
- **Sufficiency** — the Directorate's seat: terraced galleries cut into the slope at the head of the Ninth Trench, 2,750–3,400 m, housing the Undermarshalcy, the Cantorate, and the largest population in the deep. The name is the Directorate's highest word of praise turned into an address, and the city means it: no debt, no wage, universal berth, and every citizen assigned, modified, and belonging ([factions.md](factions.md)). Undermarshal Setha Korrin (b. 2,780 m) was born on its upper terrace and has never seen the Shelf ([characters.md](characters.md)).
- **The attending galleries** — Sufficiency's southern face, opening onto the Ninth's axis at 3,000 m, where the Choir's attendance shifts are held: a shift spent listening down the trench toward the Mouth and recording, without interpretation, what you dreamt ([culture.md](culture.md) §5). The dream transcripts — 6,000 pages since 88 PC — are collected here and published nowhere.
- **The banding ground** — the upper Ninth at 1,500–2,400 m, above the city: the cohort halls cut into the north wall where an intake year lives during its banding, at the top of the water it will spend its life in ([habitats.md](habitats.md) §6; [mission-intake.md](mission-intake.md) §1). **Sound:** cut structure in a trench — Coral Ruins galleries opening onto PF 1.60 water — and a year finding its band is the loudest thing Sufficiency permits above itself.
- **The shallow band** — the First Trench at 1,800 m: the posting for the roughly eight per cent of each intake who cannot hold their band, a rendering row cut into the north wall under a worked rim, and the stalls of the reassigned ([glossary.md](glossary.md), *The Shallow Band*; [habitats.md](habitats.md) §6; [mission-trench-awakening.md](mission-trench-awakening.md) §1, §11). **Sound:** maintenance. The band is heard as work that does not stop, and nobody in it is loud.
- **The Fourth Trench** — the freight shortcut between the west wall and the deep: a pipe five hundred metres wide with a floor at 1,700 m, its walls solid rock, that both the Consortium and the Directorate claim and neither patrols alone. The **Deep Yard** — the concern's berths and its failing plant, at 1,650 m — sits at the shortcut's southern mouth ([mission-baffle.md](mission-baffle.md) §11). The pipe is the shortcut and not the trench: the Fourth proper is the Fan and the Foot below it (next entry), and a trench is counted by its floor, so the Fourth's number comes from the Foot's 2,400 m and not from a pipe that runs shallower than the First. The **Abyssal Rift Corridor** archetype is cut from this ground ([maps.md](maps.md)).
- **The Fourth's foot** — where the shortcut stops being a corridor and becomes the deep. South of the Deep Yard the pipe opens into **the Fan** (2,000 m) and falls to **the Foot** (2,400 m), the last bench, where the Cantorate's listening dome stands; the **4th Trench Cohort's freight galleries** are cut into the fan's east wall at 2,900 m — Mara Tessen's water, and the beginning of the deep the Ninth is the far end of ([mission-the-dome.md](mission-the-dome.md) §1, §11; [habitats.md](habitats.md) §6).

### The Mouth — the far south · nobody's

At the southern terminus of everything, at **4,410 m**, an eleven-kilometre depression that returns sonar pings before they should arrive, on a cycle that shortened from 43 hours to 39 in 213 PC ([timeline.md](timeline.md)). The bible's standing rule applies to this document as to every other: the Mouth is described, measured, and never explained ([culture.md](culture.md) §6).

- **The Rim** — the depression's edge, carrying the richest resonant-crystal deposits ever surveyed and, since 214 PC, attendant organisms a Consortium survey team has now filed, three times, as equipment fault ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md); [timeline.md](timeline.md)) — and a Directorate watch, two submersibles that have stood on the lip for as long as anyone has had ears there, attending what they have always attended ([mission-first-arrival.md](mission-first-arrival.md) §1; [mission-prospect.md](mission-prospect.md) §1). The Rim is where all four campaigns converge, in the same week, for four incompatible reasons ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §8) — the eleven kilometres every ending is fought over. Every mission on it is played on the same ground, the Mouth's northern edge, which steps down in four bands ([mission-prospect.md](mission-prospect.md) §11):
  - **The Staging**, at 1,500 m — the approach and the way home, below the layer and above the commitment. The return line.
  - **The Slopes**, to 2,200 m — the descent's ground: two thousand metres of arriving.
  - **The Terraces**, at 2,600 m — crystal country at the rim, the six charted faces, a Resonance Field whose ring never settles and whose bearings lie a little.
  - **The Lip**, at **3,100 m** — the depression's edge itself, which carries like a trench because it is the beginning of one that has no far wall. The attendants are here.

  The Lip is the edge; 4,410 m is the floor, and nothing of the Rift's stands on it. A mission that says the Rim is *4,410 m below everything* means the depression it stands over, not the ground it stands on.

**Sound:** the return. Every hydrophone in the trench country hears the Mouth's cycle, has for two centuries, and the Rift's deepest culture is built around not saying what it is.

## 4. Distances That Matter

The Rift is small enough that its wars are neighbourhood disputes, and the campaign leans on that.

- The plateaus and Sector Kell were **adjacent** — a flood front crossed from one to the other in 197 PC. The Commune's food and the Consortium's poorest berths have always shared a fence.
- The First Chapter-House and the trench country **meet** — Knight crystal territory ends where Directorate water begins, which is why the 211 PC crystal raids cost the Order its junior cadre ([timeline.md](timeline.md)) and why the Second Chord's crystal shortfall points at the Rim.
- Sorrowgate sits within a long day's transit of **everyone**, which is the entire reason it works: a court no delegation can claim was hard to reach.
- The Ninth Trench's axis **aims at the Mouth**, and sound runs both ways along a trench. The Directorate did not choose to hear the Mouth. They settled where hearing it was unavoidable, and then became the people that made them.
- Sufficiency's attending galleries stand **eleven kilometres** north of the Lip, the Rim's edge, down the Ninth's axis — the distance the calling, the column and the account all travel in the rim week ([mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md) §1; [mission-conclave-attending.md](mission-conclave-attending.md) §1).

The campaign board draws it: every slot stands on Plate VII at its ground, in its campaign's ink, with the place, the depth and whose water read beside it ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §14, "The chart") — one row per map in `packages/frontend/src/menu/riftChart.ts`, held to the catalogue by test.

**What the campaign does not stand on.** Three of the words the world docs build on never reach a mission, and a writer should know it before a line assumes the player has heard them. **The Enclosure**, promised for the Commune campaign in an earlier draft of this table, is in no mission document: the Commune's seven are fought on Marr, the shoulder, the Furrow, the drowned city and the Rim, and the Enclosure remains the 199 PC standoff's ground and the ground the Kelp Labyrinth is modelled on ([maps.md](maps.md)). **The Deep Choir** is named in none of the seven Directorate missions, **the Answering** in none of the Order's, and **Anwen Sull** in none at all ([glossary.md](glossary.md); [characters.md](characters.md)). They are docs-only, which is not a fault — a faction does not say its own name for itself out loud — but it is a fact about what the player has been told in play. Two of the four do reach the player between missions: the record ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §14, *The record*) enters Anwen Sull's dismissal in 88 PC and the Enclosure in 199 PC on its pages, so a briefing may assume a reader who has finished the prologue has read those two names once; the Deep Choir and the Answering it does not say, and nothing does.

## 6. For Level Design

- **Maps are cut from regions, not invented beside them.** Each skirmish archetype in [maps.md](maps.md) is cut from, or modelled on, a region of §3 (the Enclosure, Fivewell, the Fourth Trench, the Fields, the drowned city), so a map's biome mix is a fact about the world before it is a balance decision.
- **Authoring rules live in [maps.md](maps.md)** — 250 m cell grid, rectangles, ellipses and polygons painted in order, floors and ceilings per region. This document contributes the *where* and the names; it deliberately owns no cell-level numbers. The one exception is §7's atlas frame, which places a whole map rather than a cell.
- **The north–south gradient is free asymmetry.** A map set on the west wall should be loud (vents, grid hum, high ambient SIG tolerance); one cut from the plateaus should be quiet enough that any noise is an event. Placing a map on this geography is choosing its acoustic politics.
- **Neutral ground is ruined ground.** Sorrowgate establishes the pattern: the places factions share are the places none of them built. Coral Ruins is the campaign's home biome for exactly this reason ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §10).

## 7. The World in Three Dimensions — SPEC

The canonical account of the continuous world (#1449, contract #1455). Plate VII places the campaign in illustration coordinates — a 2,000 × 2,750 plate whose vertical axis is latitude and depth at once — and every mission map owns its own grid ([maps.md](maps.md)). Neither is a world a camera can travel. This section gives the Rift one metric frame, places Sorrowgate in it first, and says what a mission's border looks like from inside. [free-camera.md](free-camera.md), [art-direction.md](art-direction.md), [ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §14, [maps.md](maps.md) and the pilot brief ([visual-reboot.md](visual-reboot.md)) point here rather than restating it. Every number below is presentation, TUNABLE unless it is a fact this document already gives, and none of it is simulation tuning.

### 7.1 Continuous to the eye, simulated only where the mission is

The world is **continuous visual geography, not a persistent simulation**. The whole Rift is drawn at overview detail; one mission's ground, and the water around it, at mission detail. Only the active mission is simulated, by the server, on its own map exactly as it is today. Nothing outside the mission rectangle moves, sounds, is detected or can be ordered into: the atlas is public ground, identical for every player, and carries no unit, structure, creature or state. Live ground — the arch after it falls — reaches the client only through the active session's own map data, never by editing the atlas. The room says where that ground stands: a room whose mission is placed names its region on the `terrain` message, at join, reconnection and rematch alike, and the session's ground is pasted into the region only when the room names the one the view was opened for ([#1460](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1460)). The client never pastes a room's ground into a region the room did not name: a room that names none, or another, draws no surround, and the overview keeps the detail rectangle's hole with nothing in it round the mission. Only a client and a server of different builds could disagree, since the room's mission is the one the client asked for, and no skirmish map is placed until the client opens from the room's region (§7.9). A session with no mission, a `?map=` skirmish boot, is placed by its map id when that skirmish map has a region, and keeps today's presentation when it has none; a mission map is never placed by its id, only by its mission ([#1529](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1529)). The room asks `worldRegionForSession` in `packages/shared/src/world.ts`. The client's world view still opens by mission alone; a skirmish client cannot ask by map, since on a join its map id is a placeholder and the room is already on its own water, so it will open from the region the room names.

**Sorrowgate is the pilot.** Its gameplay, map literal, assets and lines do not change ([mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md); [visual-reboot.md](visual-reboot.md) §2). The pilot is selected by mission identity, as the surface profile is, so every path that runs the prologue draws it in the world: the surround, the fade and the focus clamp of §7.3–§7.4. Only a launch from the campaign board travels there (§7.6). The title screen's Tutorial door opens its briefing as it does today, and the world appears at the home frame when the room is joined; a `?mission=` link and Resume enter the same way, and each leaves the way it does today. Every other mission keeps its current launch and presentation. So does [Radicals](mission-radicals.md), which plays on Sorrowgate's map under another mission's identity, and so does a `?map=` boot on Sorrowgate's map, which is not the prologue.

The Underworks' carries the Upper Berths, the Face and the overhang a kilometre west and east, the Berths a row north and the overhang a row south. Board Country's detail covers all but the Underworks' last two rows, and the Underworks' covers Board Country's map and the Upper Workings' top. Each draws them coarsely, as their own ground and surrounds do. The baseline stands the props its map's ground stands, wherever it is drawn: the placements the session stands on the room's first ground, each on the baseline's own relief, and none in the surroundings. Live ground is pasted over this baseline by the session and never written into it (§7.1). When the session lets the world view go, its ground goes with it, and the region is drawn from the baseline again, arch standing, until the next room sends its own. On a return to the board it goes once the baseline is built (§7.6 step 4). Before #1457 the pilot drew an apron of the map's own base ground, the owner's call on #1461. A ground delta at the patch's edge moves the surround's ground within a cell of it, in place.
- **Seam:** the detail rectangle's edge is a line of the overview grid; a surround that would end between two 1,000 m lines grows outward to the next one. Sorrowgate's needs no rounding, its edges falling at 15, 25, 38 and 47 km. Detail owns everything inside it and the overview everything outside, so no surface overlaps another, and the seam's vertices take the overview's heights so the two meet without a crack. Across the detail's last 500 m its heights and colour ease into the overview's, so there is no ledge or line at the seam either.
- **Two details drawn together:** §7.5 keeps two regions resident, and each surround is authored alone, so where two detail rectangles share cells they disagree. Each 250 m cell goes **by place** ([#1528](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1528), the owner's call): to the region whose map rectangle lies nearest the cell's centre, by §7.4's *d*. A mission patch stands at *d* = 0, so it beats any surround. Two patches tie only where §7.2 lets them overlap, which is where their cells agree. A tie goes to the session's region, or on the board to the region whose id sorts first. The other region's mesh leaves the cell undrawn, and its props do not stand there: a prop follows its ground, so one cell never stands two maps' props. Like the fade, the owner is a property of the place, never of travel order. Where two regions' cells meet, each keeps its own heights, so the two need not meet without a step along that cell edge.

### 7.4 The mission border

Inside a mission the border is **a fade into darkness, not a wall**. Three rules, one rectangle — the mission map's extent — shared by all of them:

1. **The focus is clamped; the eye is not.** The camera's focus stays inside the mission rectangle for every verb that moves it: drag, keys and edge scroll, the scope, zoom about the cursor, centring, `Home` and fit, and any scripted move ([free-camera.md](free-camera.md) §4). The orbiting eye may swing outside it, keeping its 25 m clearance over whatever public ground lies beneath. On the campaign overview the focus is clamped to the atlas frame instead.
2. **Only the exterior fades.** A scenery or water fragment at plan distance *d* outside the rectangle — zero inside, Euclidean from the nearest edge outside, so corners round off — is drawn at *f*(*d*) = 1 − smoothstep(0, *W*, *d*), with ***W* = 2,000 m** and smoothstep the cubic 3*t*² − 2*t*³. Inside, *f* is exactly 1 and nothing changes. At *W* the exterior meets the backdrop's darkness, which is why the detail surround is 500 m wider than the fade. The factor is a property of the place, never of where the eye stands. The HUD, the player's own hulls, resolved contacts, survey cues and every acoustic reading are never faded.
3. **The exterior takes no orders.** A click that lands outside the rectangle is refused on the client; the server's movement bounds stay what they are, and stay the authority.

The exterior's darkness is the backdrop's own colour along each fragment's ray, graded from the backdrop's anchor, so the ground becomes exactly the water behind it. Exterior marine snow takes the same factor. Nothing out there is heard, so while anyone in the mission listens the exterior drains to the acoustic veil's floor ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §4.5) by the same factor: as clear as the mission's own ground at the edge, wholly unheard by *W*, where it is the backdrop anyway. The floor is a display-space multiplier, applied after the output transform. With nobody listening the exterior is as clear as the mission's own field.

The pilot keeps its rim line and drops its skirt. The rim is chart ink, never dimmed ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §4.5), and it is what marks the line rule 3 refuses clicks beyond, where the fade has barely begun: *f* is still 0.96 one cell out. The skirt was the wall the world ended at — fogged like the seabed, never drawn as an instrument line — and the surround's public ground replaces it, so no chart ink is dimmed. A non-pilot mission keeps both. The fade ramps in over **0.8 s** once the mission's first state arrives and out over the same on the way back, instantly under reduced motion.

### 7.5 Budgets

The whole frame — overview, detail, water, props, the player's force, every pass — stays inside [graphics-standards.md](graphics-standards.md) gate 6: **≤ 150 draw calls and ≤ 400 k triangles**, pixel ratio at most 1.5. That is the only cap: the world's layers take no reservation of their own, and the tutorial's ground and force spend from the same 400 k ([visual-reboot.md](visual-reboot.md) §6). Draw calls and triangles are counted for the whole frame rather than shared out. A mission-density mesh over the whole Rift would cost about 1.84 million terrain triangles, which is why overview and detail are two resolutions rather than one. At most **two detail regions** are resident — the one arriving and the one left — with at most **two chunk requests in flight**; a cancelled or failed request releases what it held. Every cache is bounded per page, as the model templates are. A region's detail is one chunk, its mesh, skin and props. It is drawn while the overview is drawn, the region is in view, and one of its overview cells projects wide enough to read as a block; a little narrower lets it go again, so a camera at the edge of that does not swap it back and forth. A session's own region is held by the session and counts as one of the two. A failed request is tried again only once its region has been let go and is wanted again.

### 7.6 Entry, return, and what interrupts them

A mission starts when its room is joined, so a camera flight after joining would spend the opening offscreen. The order is therefore fixed:

1. **Overview.** The campaign board stays the control and the accessible reading ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §14); the atlas is drawn behind it. The briefing opens over the same world. The camera holds one frame there: north, 55°, from **24 km**, on the middle of Sorrowgate's map, so the drowned city is mid-frame with the plateaus' foot and the fields' slope at its edges. It does not move and takes no input, because the board is the control and nothing on the board moves. The world loads when the board opens, behind a board already drawn; on a device without WebGL the board is drawn alone, as it was before the world existed. A briefing the Tutorial door opens is drawn as it is today, off the world. The board, its chart and the briefing are inked in the player's colour-vision palette ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §11) from the page's first frame, whether or not a match has opened yet. Their captions are the world's: the chart's names the lit slot's ground, and the travel's bar names the destination as it does. The only sound under the board and the travel is the port's music bed ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §14), which carries no fact, so §11's parity table owes it no row and nothing else is owed a caption.
2. **Travel.** Launching moves the camera to the mission's home frame — north, 55°, at the **2,600 m** dolly a commander's first sight of their force opens at, focused on the spawn because the force is not public before the join; an entry with no travel opens at the same dolly on the force itself — over **2.5 s** while the match's own code and the region's public ground and assets load: the overview's skin and the models of the props the region's map ground stands. A piece that fails to load counts as loaded, so a launch is never held on it. The session then begins on that frame: a travelled entry is neither refitted to the map when its ground arrives nor recentred on the force when it is first seen. Skippable at any point, and a cut under reduced motion. A bar names the destination as the board's caption does and carries two controls: **Skip**, focused, which cuts to the end, and **Cancel**, which Esc also presses.
3. **Join.** Only when travel and preparation have both finished is the room joined, once. Commands, HUD, input and audio attach when the first server state arrives; until then the frame is held and nothing can be ordered.
4. **Return.** **Return to port** from a mission the board launched goes back to the board, wherever it is offered — the result, the Esc menu, or the overlay of a lost signal or of a device with no WebGL: the session detaches — network, HUD, input and audio — the fade ramps out where the camera stands, and the camera then travels back to the overview frame the player left: the same 2.5 s, a cut under reduced motion. The board reopens on the slot the mission was launched from, with focus ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §14, "Keyboard"). The record keeps its own door, off the world; its Back reaches the board at the board's frame. The session's ground stays drawn through the ramp-out while the region's baseline detail is built hidden, and gives way to it in the first frame after the ramp-out that the baseline is built, under the travel's motion. A baseline that fails to build gives way to the overview. Under reduced motion the session's frame is held, the fade already out, until the baseline is built or has failed, and the cut to the board's frame lands in the same frame as the swap. A baseline built only after the travel has arrived gives way on the board's frame.

**Cancel** during travel or preparation goes back to the overview with nothing joined, by the same return travel. **A load or join failure** holds the mission frame, says what failed and offers Retry and Back; at most one join is ever in flight, so a retry never doubles one. Both are said on a bar at the frame's foot, as the travel and the wait for the room are, with Retry focused and Back going back to the board by the return travel. A load failure is caught before the join, on the travel's own bar, so no room is joined for a match whose code is not there; its Retry reloads the page, because a browser keeps a failed module fetch for the life of the page, and a board launch reopens on the board. A join failure is said once the room has refused or not answered, the HUD still detached, and its Retry joins again in place. Every other launch keeps its glass over the frame, with the same two controls, and Back goes where its Return to port does. **A disconnect** keeps the world view and re-attaches through the existing reconnection grace; the ground is re-sent by the server, never restored from the atlas, which would resurrect a fallen arch. **Resume** from the title screen enters at the home frame with no travel, because the server has already spent the opening ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §14, "Resume").

Fading them in at the join and out at the swap would still change a held frame.

### 7.9 The other maps, before they join the world

Sorrowgate was the first placed map, the Banding Ground the second ([#1598](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1598)), the Western Margin the third ([#1612](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1612)), the Shallow Band the fourth ([#1615](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1615)), the two Ninefold maps, Face Six and the Upper Workings, the fifth and sixth ([#1626](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1626)), the Fourth's Foot the seventh ([#1628](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1628)), Marr Plateau the eighth ([#1630](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1630)), the Kell Shoulder the ninth ([#1633](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1633)), the Furrow the tenth ([#1638](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1638)), the Rim the eleventh ([#1640](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1640)), and Board Country and the Underworks the twelfth and thirteenth ([#1623](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1623)). This inventory, taken for [#1463](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1463) at `2535d6ee` and amended by [#1469](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1469), lists what each of the nineteen other mission grounds and the three skirmish maps would need before it is placed. It decides nothing beyond the calls its paragraphs record. Each row is where a migration starts, and §7.2's anchor table changes first, by its own rule.

**What every migration moves.** `worldRegionForMission` in `packages/shared/src/world.ts` answers for the prologue, *Intake*, *Exposure*, *Trench Awakening*, *Shift Change*, *Asset Recovery*, *The Dome*, *Tend*, *Convocation*, *Thin Water*, *Shallow*, *Deep Furrow*, *In Writing*, the Rim's five, *Item Nine* and *Tolerance*, and the world's tests assert that every other mission is unplaced. A skirmish map needs no line there, since `worldRegionForSession` places it by its map id once its region exists, but it moves one thing more: the client's world view, which opens by mission alone until then. The placed regions beside it hold Sorrowgate, the Banding Ground, the Western Margin, the Shallow Band, Face Six, the Upper Workings, the Fourth's Foot, Marr Plateau, the Kell Shoulder, the Furrow, the Rim, Board Country and the Underworks, and so do the placed ground in `packages/shared/src/ground.ts`, the only ground to have left the backend, and the authored surrounds in `packages/shared/src/rift.ts`. A map joins by all four, and the room names its region at the join as it names Sorrowgate's (§7.1). The page's one world view stands in its latest session's region, and a session placed elsewhere moves it there and dresses it in its own mission's look ([visual-reboot.md](visual-reboot.md) §2) before its ground arrives, so nothing on screen moves. The board's frame and each travel's are restated in the metres the view stands in ([#1598](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1598)).

**What blocks none of them.** Every map's props are placed from its biomes by the same placer the pilot uses, and every one is a model of the approved vocabulary: 44–141 a mission map and 225–367 a skirmish map, under the layer's cap of 600. A mission's structures are its parties, never atlas ground. Live ground is rare and interior: besides Sorrowgate's arch, only *Asset Recovery*, *Deep Furrow* and *In Writing*, and the Directorate's *Conclave* repaint ground, and none of their regions touches a map edge.

**Placing a map.** "Placed at its anchor" below puts the map's centre on its §7.2 anchor, or the place the anchor names on it where the map draws that place. "Fits" means the map lies inside its region's rectangle, and its whole §7.3 surround inside the atlas, which `worldRegionProblems` checks. The nearest anchor stands in for a map with none.

**Four grounds are drawn twice.** The Fourth Trench and the Foot, the galleries and the Upper Terraces, the Underworks and Board Country, and the two Ninefold maps each draw one ground in two literals. A heightfield holds one floor a cell, so each pair overlaps where their cells agree, by §7.2's rule ([#1527](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1527)). The two Ninefold maps draw the Vein's field in two literals but are placed a row apart, so they share no cell ([#1626](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1626)). Merging a pair into one region would rewrite a mission's simulated ground, and moving an anchor breaks §3, where Face Six and the Upper Workings share the Vein. A pair whose shared cells disagree falls back to moving an anchor, row by row in §7.2's table, and no mission map changes to make a pair agree. **The Fourth pair is the exception, placed as one map ([#1505](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1505), the owner's call).** At one origin the Trench and the Foot differ in 8 cells, the Trench's last row of margin water at 1,450 m where the Foot's Fan opens at 2,000 m. So the Foot is placed alone, holding the Trench's ground with the deep it opens into, and *Baffle* keeps today's presentation ([#1628](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1628)). Placing both and letting the session's map take the 8 cells would amend #1527's rule, and standing them apart would draw one ground in two places. The Underworks and Board Country fell back so: their 84 shared cells all disagreed, so the Underway's anchor moved 1,750 m north and the two maps meet edge to edge, sharing no cell, with Sector Vayle's anchor moved into the Underworks' Upper Berths ([#1623](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1623), the owner's calls). The Western Margin and the Shallow Band were a fifth pair until they fell back: the margin's worked ground is Abyssal Trench where the band's rim is Coral Ruins, so the margin's new anchor stands it beside the band rather than over it, and the two share no cell ([#1565](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1565)).

**Surrounds overlap almost everywhere.** Placed at their anchors, about two dozen pairs of detail rectangles overlap, the Fields' three and the Fourth's stack by 45–99 km². §7.5 holds two regions resident at once, and each surround is authored alone, so two drawn together disagree wherever they share cells. Each shared cell goes by place, §7.3's rule ([#1528](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1528), the owner's call): a mission patch beats any surround, and between two surrounds the nearer map rectangle wins. The session's region winning would make the ground under the eye depend on travel order. One authored surround per overlap would never disagree, but costs about two dozen patches in `rift.ts`.

**A skirmish is placed by its map id ([#1529](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1529), the owner's call).** A `?map=` boot has no mission, so §7.1 places it by its map id when the map has a region. None of the three has one, so each keeps today's presentation and the Ventfront Divide stays §7.7's non-target control. Keeping every skirmish out of the world was the recommendation, cheapest to reverse; a detached surround of the map's own base ground, outside the atlas, would have brought back the pre-#1457 apron. Each of the three is cut from a place rather than being it, so placing one still starts from its row above.

**Shared maps: every mission travels ([#1505](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1505), the owner's call).** A region is a place, so every mission on a placed map is drawn there. Radicals plays Sorrowgate's map and stays §7.7's non-target control, keeping its own presentation (§7.1). The Rim carries five missions, and Marr, the Kell Shoulder, the Furrow and the Outer Formations two each. *Tend* and *Convocation* were the first pair placed this way ([#1630](https://github.com/gunnargehtab/Echoes-of-the-Abyss/issues/1630)). Placing only the first mission on each map, or deciding map by map, were the options not taken.
