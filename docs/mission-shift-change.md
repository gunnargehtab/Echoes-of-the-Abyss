# The Ledger 2 — Shift Change

> The second mission of the Consortium campaign ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §4), specified. Written
> to the pattern [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) sets and
> [mission-asset-recovery.md](mission-asset-recovery.md) continues: everything here is authored —
> the forces, the water, the beats, the numbers and the text — and code transcribes this document.

**Setting:** The Ninefold Vein's upper workings — Face Two and the Fivewell rail transfer, 214 PC,
three tides after the Face Six recovery ([world-map.md](world-map.md); [timeline.md](timeline.md)).

**Mission id:** `ledger-shift-change` — namespaced by campaign, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §1.

---

## 1. What Face Two Is

Face Two is the second-oldest working on the Vein, producing since 9 PC, and its seam is done.
The survey has said so for a year; the crews have known longer, because crews always know first.
The Board's closure review is drafted, graded, and scheduled for announcement at the end of this
shift. Nobody on the face has been told, because telling them is the announcement, and the
announcement is not Osk's to make.

Three facts about the water decide the whole mission:

**The Vein straddles the layer.** The thermocline sits at 1,200 m, and the Vein's faces work
below it while its rail runs above ([world-map.md](world-map.md); [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md)
§3). Sound crossing the layer is cut to a third; the faces and the rail are, acoustically,
different worlds, and everything on this field that matters happens in the hundred metres of
climb between them. The mission never says this. The mission is built out of it.

**A working field is its hum.** Four harvesters, a refinery, and the deliveries between them
leave a slow-decaying industrial hum that reads as throughput to anyone with the ears for it
([economy.md](economy.md) §5; [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §7). A listener over the field
does not need to find your hulls. They read your budget off the seabed, and a budget that
changes shape is a budget that is telling somebody something.

**What the register does to a closed face.** A crew reassigned from a *producing* face
transfers at grade — their berths, their rates, their standing move with them. A crew surplussed
from a *closed* face reverts to pool rate, which is the bottom of the debt-berth ladder with the
word "temporary" attached, and the Vein's crews have watched "temporary" before. Between this
shift and the announcement there is a window in which Face Two's crews are still, on paper,
working crews. Osk intends to move all three watches through it.

Nobody in this mission is doing anything wrong. The Board audits a dying field because that is
what diligence is. The crews ring nothing and hide nothing; they work their shift. Osk moves
people he is responsible for through a gap in a procedure he is not breaking. The pressure is
three parties being severally correct, which is the only way this campaign builds pressure
([campaign.md](campaign.md) §2, rule 1).

## 2. Whose Hulls the Player Commands

**The player commands Face Two's working shift: four harvesters on the face, three crew barges
at the muster, and the shift's number to make.**

This is Foreman Corwin Osk's mission — the campaign hands the field to the man the writ told
the column to listen to ([mission-asset-recovery.md](mission-asset-recovery.md) §2), and hands
the player his problem whole: run the shift so that it reads, to every instrument pointed at
it, as exactly the shift it always is, while the thing the shift exists to protect leaves
through the middle of it. He is on the channel throughout. He does not raise his voice once.

**Engine bound, stated so nobody corrects it into a bug.** The shift carries the Consortium
faction value. The audit pair is a scripted party and carries it too — two Consortium delegations
in one water, which the engine expresses as two slots, exactly as the prologue seated two
Consortium-adjacent parties around one exchange. The Drift is not a party.

## 3. The Shift

| Hull | Count | Stats | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harvester | 4 | SIG 18 idle / throttle 12–25–45–68 mining · HYD 30 · PR-2 ([units.md](units.md)) | The face crew's hulls, and the mission's instrument: the throttle is the system this mission teaches, and it is in the player's hands from the first tick |
| Crew barge | 3 | Harvester hull, dredge struck, berths rigged · SIG 18 idle · HYD 30 · PR-2 | One per watch. Deaf, slow, and carrying everything this mission is actually about. The manifest lists them as hull movements; the berthing lists are §5's |

### What the shift does not carry

1. **No combat hulls, and nothing armed.** A working face under grid protection fields no
   guns of its own; the Vein's standing escort is the grid's business and the grid is not in
   this mission. Weapons stay locked with the reason attached: *not fielded — the shift is a
   works order, not a writ*.
2. **No active sonar.** [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 withholds the ping until mission 3, and
   the manifest is again the diegetic form: a survey array is not shift equipment. The player
   spends a second mission listening rather than asking.
3. **No construction.** The face is producing, not expanding. A closure review is running, and
   a new structure on a face under review is a sentence in somebody's minute.
4. **Silent Running is present, and this time it is sometimes right.** Mission 1 taught that
   running silent beside loud freight feeds the freight to the Drift. This mission's freight is
   the loud half and the barges are the quiet half, and a barge berthed dark at the rail while
   the audit walks past it is the button used correctly for the first time in the campaign.
   Nothing tells the player this. The prologue taught them to feel it.

## 4. The Throttle

The system this mission teaches, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2: one system, introduced in
the first three minutes, load-bearing by the last five. Mission 1 used the throttle table's
loudest row as an authored constant and kept the lever out of reach; this mission hands the
lever over, all four positions ([economy.md](economy.md) §3), and builds its whole problem out
of what the lever actually moves:

1. **The throttle sets the load, and the load is the hum.** Yield per trip is what the setting
   changes, deliveries are what the hum integrates, and the audit reads the hum. A shift at
   Standard hums like every shift Face Two has run for two centuries. That is the cover, and it
   is the only cover on the field.
2. **Trickle is not quiet here — it is loud.** The instinct every tutorial teaches is to
   throttle down when somebody is listening. On this field the listener is an auditor whose
   baseline is two hundred years of Standard, and a dying face that goes quiet the tide the
   audit arrives has announced the closure better than the Board could. The quiet option is
   the tell. The player who creeps fails the number and files the report both.
3. **Overburden spends the field faster** — around thirty per cent faster off the ground
   ([economy.md](economy.md) §3) — and Face Two is dying anyway. Running the last seam at
   Overburden to fatten the hum while the barges cross is legitimate, audible, and exactly the
   kind of arithmetic this faction's campaign keeps handing the player: spend the thing that is
   already spent to buy the thing that is not.
4. **The layer prices all of it.** Below 1,200 m the faces can run any throttle they like at the
   audit's ears — the crossing cuts the sound to a third before the vein's own roar takes the
   rest. The hum the audit reads is the hum that reaches the rail. What crosses the layer is
   what the player sends across it, and the only things that have to cross are the barges.

### The SIG budget, as a working level again

**SIG budget: 50** — the Standard-throttle band, the level a producing face holds
([economy.md](economy.md) §2). Mission 1 inverted the budget against the player who creeps;
this mission does it again with the stakes moved from the Drift to the register: the budget is
what the field is *supposed* to sound like, and the mission is playtested against the player
who runs under it, because running under it is the observable event.

## 6. The Audit

**The Board audits a face in terminal decline because that is what the Board is. The pair
files its transit plan in advance, walks the High Road twice, docks at the rail between
passes, and listens the whole way. Nobody is hunting anybody.**

The mechanism is the sweep of [mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §6, played from the other
side of the ledger. Tend's player was the garden the survey walked through; this player is the
concern's own field being audited by the concern's own instruments, and the lesson inverts
with the seat: the sweep does not have to be evaded, because it cannot be — it has to be
*read*. Its plan is on the board at 00:00. Its windows are the two road passes. Its ears are
good, its baseline is two centuries of this exact field, and what it must not resolve is a
crew barge in the open water above the layer while the face it belongs to is still, on paper,
working.

- **What the audit hears is priced by the model, not by a rule.** The pair listens through the
  same propagation the Echo Layer prices everything with: the vein masks, the layer cuts, the
  hum integrates. A barge that crosses between passes crosses free. A barge that crosses
  during one, high and under way, is a contact in an auditor's log.
- **A filed movement is not a mission failure.** It is a sentence in the audit's minute, and
  the minute is read at the close, appended to whatever the shift earned — the arrangement
  [mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §8 built, reused whole. The transfer grades do not
  survive an audited irregularity; the crews still get out; the arithmetic of what that costs
  them is left where this campaign always leaves arithmetic, in the player's lap.
- **The docked interval is not a window.** Between passes the pair sits at the rail and reads
  the registry, and the registry is not a hydrophone. The rail berths in front of them hold
  whatever is berthed there, dark and unremarked. An auditor reads documents at a dock; the
  water is only evidence while the pair is on the road listening to it.

The audit is not the antagonist. The audit is the register doing what the register does, two
missions after the player used the same register to price a chamber's rescue. The campaign
does not change its mind about the instruments; it changes which side of them the player is
standing on.

## 7. What Answers

**Nothing. This is the Consortium's working day, and the mission does not manufacture a
threat to decorate it.**

The pack that shadows the Downworks is the same pack that shadows every producing face on the
Vein — present, audible at the edge of hearing, and uninterested in a field that is merely
working ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §4). It commits to nothing in this mission, because nothing
in this mission gets loud enough to invite it. It is on the field so the player's ears learn
what a pack at rest sounds like, and so the player who overburdens the last seam spends the
mission glancing at it. The glancing is the design.

[mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §8 established that a mission whose only threat is a ledger
has no failure to make audible — it has outcomes, and the close is a conclusion rather than a
timer. Shift Change is the Consortium's Tend: the tide ends, the announcement arrives, and the
Board reads what the shift did with the window it never knew it had.

## 8. The Objective

**Run the shift: make the number, and move the watches.**

Two objectives, both real, pulling against each other through one lever:

**The number.** Face Two reports 3,600 nodules this shift — four harvesters' standard tide
with weather in it. The quota is not a pretext: the Vein is on the insolvency projection and
every shift's number is a row in it. It is also the cover, because the number *is* the hum,
and the hum is what tells the audit that Face Two is a face and not an evacuation. A player
who creeps the throttle protects nothing and starves both.

**The watches.** Three crew barges from the muster, up through the layer, to the Fivewell rail
transfer — released in watch order across the shift, threaded through the audit's charted
passes. Each barge that berths at the rail before the announcement is a watch transferred at
grade. Each one still on the face when the announcement lands reverts to pool rate with the
closure, and the mission does not soften what that means.

### Results

| Result | Condition | The reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shift complete** | The number made and all three watches berthed | "Face Two reports three thousand six hundred, of quota, on schedule. Seventy-one berths transfer at grade under standing works orders. The closure review will find the face orderly, current, and empty. The Board's announcement follows this report by one bell." |
| **Half the business** | The number or the watches, not both | "The shift closes on half its business. What was made is entered. What was moved is entered. What was neither is entered too, and the review will read all three columns." |
| **The shift fails** | Neither | "Face Two reports a failed shift in its final tide. The review notes that the face was neither productive nor orderly, and prices the closure accordingly. The berthing lists revert to pool." |

Each result carries the watch-by-watch and quota readings beneath it, assembled from what
actually happened — the close reads back which columns filled, because the mission authors
both sentences for each and the run picks ([mission-attendance.md](mission-attendance.md) §13
asked for an epilogue that assembles; this mission extends the same arrangement to its
objectives). A filed audit minute is appended to any of the three: filed and unfiled cross
with the work freely, exactly as Tend's ledger does.

There is no keystone. Mission 1 hung its whole count on one asset because the writ did; this
shift's two halves are priced separately because the register prices them separately, and a
run that made the number while the watches stayed is a real result the Board can read — it is
just not the one Osk was working for.

## 9. Length, SIG Budget, and the Beats

**Length: sixteen minutes** — one shift, muster to announcement. Inside
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's 12–25.

**SIG budget: 50, as a working level** — §4. The playtest adversary is the player who creeps.

| Time | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 00:00 | The shift opens. The audit's transit plan is on the board — two road passes, docked between. The pack is on the Downworks, at rest. The hum starts building from the first delivery |
| 01:00 | Osk, on the channel: the shift briefing that is not in the minutes (§12) |
| 03:00 | **First watch stands down.** Barge One is released from the muster. The audit is not yet on the road; the first crossing is the free one, and the player learns the climb — slow, silent, and long enough to respect |
| 04:00 | **Pass one.** The pair enters the High Road, listening. Whatever is above the layer and under way is theirs to resolve |
| 06:30 | The pair docks at the rail. Window closed. Tull reads the registry; the water in front of him is not his instrument (§6) |
| 08:00 | **Second watch stands down.** Barge Two released — into the docked interval, if the player has kept the schedule in mind, or into pass two if they have not |
| 10:00 | **Pass two.** The pair walks the road back, south to north, the same ears the other way |
| 11:00 | **Third watch stands down.** Barge Three released, with the pass still on the road — the mission's one genuinely tight thread: hold at the layer and wait it out, or berth it dark short of the rail, or gamble the climb. All three are right answers priced differently |
| 13:00 | The pair clears the road and departs for the Holding. Free water to the whistle |
| 13:30 | Vail, loaded, one line (§12) |
| 16:00 | **The whistle, and the announcement.** The shift ends; the Board's word arrives one bell behind the report; the close reads what the shift did (§8) |

The audit's legs are authored beats and its plan is shown at 00:00, because the mission's
skill is *reading a schedule*, not discovering one. The tension is never "where is the
audit" — the audit files its whereabouts in advance, as audits do. The tension is that the
watches stand down on the shift's clock, the passes run on the Board's, and the player owns
the only lever that threads them.

## 10. What It Teaches

One system, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10: **the economy as a sound you are responsible
for** — the throttle's four positions, the hum they integrate to, and the layer that decides
who hears it. The four movements of §4 land across the beat table: the lever is in hand by
03:00, the creep instinct is punished by the number continuously, Overburden is a real answer
by the second crossing, and the layer is load-bearing from the first climb to the last.

What this mission deliberately does not teach:

- **Masking as a tool** — mission 3, *Baffle*, where the Consortium bends the water on purpose.
  This mission's masking is all inherited ground: the vein's roar and the layer were here
  before the Consortium was. Shown before operated, the campaign's standing sequence.
- **The ping** — mission 3, with the doctrine finally old enough to afford it.
- **Depth as a bleed** — the faces sit at the bottom of Mid-Water and the shift is rated for
  all of it. The crush ledger waits for mission 5, where the arithmetic stops being about
  berths.
- **Combat** — nothing in this mission fires. The campaign's first and only wholly unarmed
  mission after the prologue, and the Consortium's answer to Tend: the faction that is loudest
  at war is also loud at peace, and the player should hold what that peace sounds like before
  mission 3 spends it.

One spawn, at Face Two's muster: 875, 2250. **Two nodule fields** — thin at Face Two, rich at
Face Five, because a dying face still reports and a shift makes its number where the number
is. The refinery stands in the Downworks between them, below the layer, so the hum and its
residue live in the masked half of the map. No hazard sites: this field's weather is the
audit.

Every rectangle lands on the 250 m cell grid and paints exactly the metres it reads
([maps.md](maps.md), "How a map is written"). The layer at 1,200 m is not authored — it is on
every map — but this map is *placed* against it deliberately: floors above it north of the
workings, floors below it south, and no region whose water spans it, so every crossing is a
climb somebody ordered ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §3).

**The Upper Workings is a mission map and is not in the public catalogue.** One seat, not
balanced, resolved by mission id and nothing else ([maps.md](maps.md)).

## 12. The Briefing

Osk, to the shift command, at muster. Not read from anything. The Consortium's register is
defined in [culture.md](culture.md) §3; this is what it sounds like two grades down from the
Board, from the one manager who has ever refused it anything.

> Works order for the tide: Face Two runs its shift, reports its number, and stands down its
> watches on the bell. Quota is three thousand six hundred. The seam will cover it if the seam
> is worked, and Five will cover what Two cannot. That is the shift. It is the same shift as
> yesterday, and it will read like it.
>
> Risk and Actuarial are on the field today. Their transit plan is filed and it is on the
> board: two passes on the High Road, docked at the rail between. An audit that files its plan
> is telling you it has nothing to hide, and expecting the same. Give them a working face.
> That is not a trick; a working face is what this is. Run your throttles like it is any tide,
> because it is.
>
> Watches stand down in order. Berthing is rigged on the barges and Vail has the lists. The
> transfer paperwork is standing works orders, hull movements between sites, current and
> correct. If anyone asks a barge's business, its business is on its manifest, and its
> manifest is right.
>
> One more thing, and then the bell. The climb to the rail is long and the layer is where it
> has always been. Nobody crosses it under way while the road is listening unless I say so, or
> unless you have worked out for yourselves why I would not. You have all been on this face
> long enough to have worked it out. The shift is open.

### Objective readings, in play

- *Make the number. The face reports what it always reports.*
- *Stand the watches down in order. Berth them at the rail, at grade.*

### The voices on the channel

**Foreman Corwin Osk — 01:00**

> The review will read this shift twice, so we run it clean twice over. Number on the board,
> watches on the bell, and the road hears a working face. It is not a performance. It is the
> face working. There is a difference, and the difference is what an auditor is paid to hear.

**Underwriter Baen Tull, on the audit channel, docked — 06:30**

> Registry current. Throughput within band of projection. Note for the file: the projection is
> the terminal one, and the face is meeting it. Meeting a terminal projection is not an
> anomaly. It is the only compliance this face has left.

**Foreman Corwin Osk, as the third watch stands down — 11:00**

> Three's away when Three is clear, not when Three is due. The rail has kept berths empty for
> two hundred years; it can keep them one more pass.

**Lift Foreman Dessa Vail, loaded — 13:30**

> Berthing lists closed and under way. Twenty souls and their grades aboard, and the grades
> are the cargo.

Each line fails [culture.md](culture.md) §3 for the other three factions, which is that
document's own test (§6): Osk's briefing states a deception without once lying, in a register
where "the manifest is right" is both true and doing all the work — the Commune could not say
it without turning it, the Knights not without courtesy toward the audit, the Directorate not
in the active voice at all. Tull's line prices a dying face's obedience without a gram of
sentiment, which no other register could resist. Vail's names the cargo and the cargo is a
rate.
