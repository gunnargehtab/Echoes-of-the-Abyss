# The Second Chord 2 — Standing Wave

> The second mission of the Knight campaign ([campaign.md](campaign.md) §7), specified. The
> thirteenth mission document the bible carries, to the pattern
> [mission-sorrowgate.md](mission-sorrowgate.md) sets: everything here is authored — the
> forces, the water, the beats, the numbers and the text — and code transcribes this document.

**Setting:** the Fifth, a crystal defile on the Resonance Fields' southern margin where crystal
country breaks toward the trenches, 214 PC ([world-map.md](world-map.md);
[characters.md](characters.md)).

**Mission id:** `chord-standing-wave` — namespaced by campaign after `chord-aptitude`, per
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §1.

**This is the first Knight mission with anything built in it.**
[mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §3 withheld the whole of it deliberately — "no base,
no economy, nothing built", six hulls inside one afternoon, no resource fields on the map and
the build pages not offered. So this is where the Order stops being a tuning party and becomes
a faction that constructs, on the thinnest economy in the game: a flat tithe, independent of
map control, paid per commander rather than per structure ([economy.md](economy.md) §6). §4 is
longer than its counterpart in the other twelve documents because that arrangement — a mission
whose one system is *placing things*, handed to the one faction whose income does not grow when
it takes ground — is the mission rather than an awkwardness in it.

## 1. What the Fifth Is

A canyon in crystal that rings at a fifth, and has been called that for a hundred and forty
years by people who meant it literally.

The Resonance Fields seam the eastern slope from 1,400 m down to 2,900 m, and at their southern
margin they break toward trench country ([world-map.md](world-map.md)). The break is not a cliff.
It is a defile — three kilometres of crystal-walled ground, a kilometre wall to wall at the
narrows, running north from the Seam up into the Third Chapter-House's country. The walls are
seamed formation, the same crystal the chapter-houses are cut into, and under any pressure change
they ring. What they ring at is a perfect fifth, and the Order named the ground for the interval
the way it names a person for the interval their name resolves to ([culture.md](culture.md) §5).

**Three facts about this water decide the mission.**

**It is the only road.** Crystal territory ends where Directorate water begins
([world-map.md](world-map.md)), and between the two there is trench country on one side and
the whole of the open east slope on the other. The Fifth is four kilometres of covered
ground between them, and it is covered in the Order's sense rather than anybody else's: PF 0.70,
scattered, bearings that lie ([environments.md](environments.md)). A column that wants to be
somewhere without having been heard crossing to it uses the Fifth. Everyone has always used the
Fifth. The Order has never written down that it is theirs, for the same reason Vrey gives about
the Resonance Fields in [mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §12, and the not-writing-down
is the thing this mission spends.

**Its southern end is a trench.** The South Mouth opens onto the Seam at 1,780 m — Abyssal
Trench water, PF 1.60 down the axis, "no secrets, only distances"
([environments.md](environments.md)). Anything loud in the Fifth is heard down the axis by the
deep houses, by trench country, and by whatever is further down than either. **A megaphone built
in the Fifth is pointed at the bottom of the Rift**, and the document says that once, here, and
does not say it again.

**Nothing in it is deep.** Rim 1,450 m, floor 1,700, mouth 1,780 — every metre of the map is
Mid-Water ([systems-depth.md](systems-depth.md) §1), the thermocline duct ends four hundred
metres above the shallowest ground, and the party is PR-2 throughout. That is arranged, exactly
as [mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §11 arranged it: one system per mission means the
other two axes have to be given nothing to say, and the cleanest way to arrange that is ground
on which they have nothing to say. §3 turns the same arrangement to a second purpose that
mission 1 did not need.

## 2. Whose Hulls the Player Commands

**The player commands the same six hulls as mission 1, under the same officer, with a works
order attached — and the works order is from the Ninth.**

Voice Ren Kalliso is thirty-one, the Order's best field officer and its most ordinary person, and
in [mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) she spent an afternoon reading formations for
Chapter-Master Vrey ([characters.md](characters.md)). This is the next thing she is told to do,
and it is not told to her by Vrey. **Choirmaster Ivane Sull speaks in this mission**, from the
Ninth, and it is her first appearance in the campaign as a voice rather than as a name other
people are careful around.

What she orders is a closure, and what makes it hers rather than anybody's is the second half of
it: *close it against us as well*. §12 is where she says so. The reasoning is in §8, priced into
the objective rather than left in the briefing, because [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 1 does
not exempt the player's own faction and a doctrine that only holds in the briefing is a doctrine
the mission has quietly written out.

**Chapter-Master Vrey is not in this mission.** He has the standing to call a conclave and he does
not use it; that is §7 row 4, *Conclave*, and it is two missions away
([characters.md](characters.md)). A mission 2 that put the Order's internal politics on the
channel would spend mission 4's only material to decorate a works order. Vrey is told about the
Fifth afterwards, off-screen, and the campaign is the poorer for nothing.

**The nineteen are not in this mission either.** A reader who knows that the 211 PC crystal raids
came up out of trench country will draw a line from that to a canyon the Order is closing against
trench country, and the line is real and the document will not draw it.
[mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §2 is explicit that mission 3 spends the cadre and
mission 1 does not, and the same rule binds here: **Kalliso does not mention them, nobody mentions
them to her, and the arithmetic of six is allowed to be felt without being explained.**

**Engine bound, stated so nobody corrects it into a bug.** The party carries the Knight faction
value and its own slot. One other party is seated — a Directorate transit column, §5 — with its
own hulls and its own standing, placed by the mission rather than by the map
([maps.md](maps.md)). Two factions, one mission, no lobby. The Drift is not populated: no fauna
are seeded and none is authored, for the reason §10 gives.

### The node is the Sounding Spire, and this document did not decide that

The bible already says so in three places, and the mission takes it rather than inventing a
fourth structure beside it:

- [units.md](units.md) stats the Spire as "**Projected depth access / resonance node**", and its
  effect line reads "Grants PR+1 to allied units within 600 m **and can form Standing Wave
  corridors when two nodes pair**."
- [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) names the faction unique as "a **placed resonance node**
  that, when a second node is placed within 1,500 m, creates a line of sonic damage between
  them", which is the pairing range this document spends.
- [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8, excluding structures from the directional term, says the
  Spire's "directional" in [units.md](units.md) "is a different claim — it is aimed at its *pair*,
  and the Standing Wave corridor between two nodes is where that aim goes."

So *node* is a role a Spire is in, not a second structure. [mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md)
§3 reads the same way once you take it literally — "**No Standing Wave, no Sounding Spire**" is one
withholding stated in both its halves, and mission 1 withheld both because it wanted neither.

**The reading this replaces is recorded, because a reviewer will reach for it.** A separate,
cheaper Standing Wave structure — its own `StructureKind`, its own stat line, no depth aura —
would be a better *mission 2 item*: it would cost less than 750, it would not carry a PR grant
that has to be arranged into silence, and the Order would get a constructible that is only ever a
weapon. It would also make three canon sentences wrong at once and put a second crystal-gated
structure in a faction [economy.md](economy.md) §6 calls the thinnest in the game. The document
takes the cheaper doc change over the cheaper mission, and §13 carries the row.

### What the party does not carry

1. **No active sonar**, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's mission-3 rule, and for this faction
   it is the same argument mission 1 made and does not need remaking: §5 of
   [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) fixes the ping at SIG 95 **omnidirectional** and §8 exempts
   it from the directional term, so it is the one emission the Order owns that has no bow. A
   faction arguing that loudness has a direction would call it *discourteous*
   ([culture.md](culture.md) §3).
2. **No production, and nothing to extract.** The build pages open on exactly one entry. There is
   no Foundry, no Refinery, no reinforcement and no resource field anywhere on the map: the
   Knight economy is a stipend, not an extraction (§4), and a crystal node in the Fifth would
   make the mission about cutting crystal in a canyon rather than about closing one.
3. **No projected depth.** The Spire's PR+1 aura is live, granted, and inert, because every metre
   of the map is Mid-Water and the party is PR-2 (§1). This is the one deferral mission 1 flagged
   as a *campaign* gap rather than a mission one — no mission in [campaign.md](campaign.md) §7
   teaches projected access ([mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §10) — and it stays
   flagged. A Spire in a canyon whose floor the party can already reach fills nothing by accident.
4. **Silent Running is present and is not the answer, for a new reason.** Mission 1 priced it out
   by arithmetic. Here it is worse than wrong: it throttles hulls, and the two loudest things in
   this mission are a construction site and a structure, neither of which has a throttle to pull
   ([units.md](units.md) design notes). The button works on the six hulls and says nothing about
   the seventy the works are making.

## 4. Paired Nodes, and an Income That Does Not Grow

The system this mission teaches, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 2: one system, introduced
in the first three minutes and load-bearing by the last five. The system is **the corridor** — two
nodes, the line between them, and what that line does to everybody standing in it.

### What a pair is

**A node pairs with the nearest completed, unpaired node of the same commander within 1,500 m, at
the moment it completes. The pairing is decided once and is never re-decided. A node with no
partner is silent.**

That is one sentence and it settles the three questions the format leaves open, so it is worth
saying what each of them costs.

- **Pairing is automatic, not chosen at placement.** The player already chooses the thing that
  matters — *where the two ends are* — and a pairing UI would ask them to choose it twice.
  [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md)'s own wording is "when a second node is placed within
  1,500 m", which is a proximity rule and not a selection.
- **Nearest-unpaired, not all-pairs.** Three nodes under an all-pairs rule are three corridors and
  a triangle, four are six, and the mission would be a combinatorial diagram rather than a canyon.
  Nearest-unpaired gives *n* nodes exactly ⌊*n*/2⌋ corridors, which is what "paired emitters"
  means when you read it as a plain English noun.
- **Decided once.** A pairing that re-solved when a node died would let a third node inherit a
  dead node's partner and swing a kill-line across ground the player was standing on. The Order
  builds instruments; an instrument does not re-tune itself while you are inside it.

**The odd node is the teaching beat.** The first node completes at about four minutes, alone, and
does nothing at all: no line, no PF, no damage, 30 SIG of hum and 750 nodules gone. The objective
reads *One voice. A voice is not an interval.* — and a player who has understood the sentence has
understood the mechanic before the second site is placed.

### What the corridor is

Between paired nodes: **a line of sonic damage, and local PropagationFactor raised to 2.00**
([factions.md](factions.md); [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md)). Both halves are symmetric.
Neither has an owner.

**"Including them" is a mechanical claim and this document treats it as one.** The line damages
Knight hulls at the Knight rate. The PF applies to Knight emissions in both directions. There is
no friendly-fire exemption, no reduced coefficient, and no line that goes quiet when a Knight
crosses it — and §8 puts the player's own withdrawal *through* that geometry into the terminal
objective, so a run that ignores the symmetry loses the mission rather than a paragraph of
flavour. [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 1 is usually a constraint on how the opponent is
written. Here it turns inward.

**A held interval is an active Spire.** [units.md](units.md) defines the Spire's active figure as
the depth grant being load-bearing, and on this map nothing is ever below its PR, so under that
definition the corridor would stand at **30 SIG** — a lethal kill-line that hums like a
refrigerator. This document widens the definition instead: **a Spire holding a corridor is active,
at 80, at both ends, for as long as the interval is held.** Two reasons, and the second is the
important one. It is doctrinally right — the Order's weapon announcing the Order's position is
[factions.md](factions.md)'s entire argument about sound. And a hazard nobody can hear is not
dread, it is confusion, which is the one thing `CLAUDE.md`'s target emotion rules out. The
widening is a [units.md](units.md) edit, not a mission one, and §13 routes it.

### The economy, and whether the thinness is the difficulty

[economy.md](economy.md) §6 gives the Knights a **flat tithe** — 1 nodule per second, paid per
commander while the Bastion stands, independent of extraction and independent of map control. A
Spire costs **750**. The mission opens the player on **1,530 nodules**: two nodes and thirty over.

So the arithmetic of the whole mission is on one line. **Two nodes are paid for at 00:00. A third
costs twelve and a half minutes of stipend.**

That is the difficulty, and it is the doctrine rather than an accident, which is the question the
mission is here to answer:

- **A faction whose income does not scale with ground has exactly one way to afford more, and it
  is time.** Every other faction in the game answers "I need another building" by taking another
  field. The Order answers it by waiting. In a mission with a column moving up a canyon on a
  schedule, waiting is the one currency that is also the stake.
- **It makes placement irreversible in the way the Order's whole position is irreversible.** 750
  nodules is not a mistake you correct; it is a mistake you live beside for the rest of the
  eighteen minutes. [economy.md](economy.md) §6's own phrase is that the Knight economy "is really
  a *budget*, spent once", and this mission is the first place a player spends it by hand.
- **And it is why the mission is winnable with two nodes and better with three.** The third node
  is not a bonus. It is a *stepping stone* — §11's geometry means the corridor can only be laid
  where the build radius reaches, and reaching further south costs a structure whose only purpose
  is to have been closer.

**What it must not become is a resource mission.** There is nothing to harvest, no throttle, and
no second income; the stipend arrives whether the player does anything or not. The economy is a
clock the player reads, not a system the player operates, which is what keeps rule 2's *one
system* honest.

### The SIG budget

**SIG budget: 70, emitted — and 140, carried, inside the thing the player built.**

The figure is the construction site. `CONSTRUCTION.SITE_SIG` puts a site at **70 sustained** for
the whole 150-second commissioning, which makes it the loudest thing the player owns by a
comfortable margin — louder than the Voice live in her own cone (65), louder than the Bastion
(35), louder than five corvettes cruising together. It is also not optional, not throttleable and
not directional: a site has no bow ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8 excludes structures),
so mission 1's answer does not apply to it. **The Order cannot build quietly, and it is building
in front of the best ears in the Rift.**

The second figure is the one [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 does not normally ask for, and this
mission has to state because a PF multiplier is not an emission. A budget that reported only what
the player emits would report 70 in a corridor that delivers 140, and the mission would have
published half its own arithmetic. §7 is the table.

**The column is not an attack and the mission never lets it become one.** Adze is moving hulls
from trench country to the northern slope by the covered route everybody uses, on a route order
filed before the works order existed. [campaign.md](campaign.md) §2 rule 1 forbids villains and
this is what it costs to keep: the opponent's plan is *correct*, the opponent hears the
construction inside the first minute — they are the Listeners, and the site is at 70
([factions.md](factions.md)) — and the opponent's response to a corridor that stands is to **turn
around**, because a road that costs more than it saves is not a road. Nobody in the column is
stupid and nobody in it is cruel. They are simply on the other end of an instrument.

**The Cantor is the reason the mission is hard.** A 1,200 m Tier-3 listening dome
([units.md](units.md)) travelling up a canyon a kilometre wide means the column's ears cover the
width of the Fifth wherever it is, and there is no flank in a corridor to give them. Mission 1's
doctrine still works — §7 — and for the first time it works against something that was built to
make it not work.

Naming follows [culture.md](culture.md) §4. **Cohort-Prime Adze** carries one name, which the
Directorate regards as an honour and everyone else finds chilling, and speaks twice, both
times procedural. **Choirmaster Ivane Sull** speaks from the Ninth at the top, once at the pairing,
and at the close. Kalliso speaks once, to nobody, as she did in mission 1.

## 6. The Column's Walk

Seven hulls on one route, four kilometres of it, in a canyon that is a kilometre wide at the
narrows and rather more at both ends. The walk is authored as legs and windows — the sweep
mechanism [mission-tend.md](mission-tend.md) §6 built and
[mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §11's picket extended — and it has three properties the
player can learn and one they cannot.

**It is slow, and it is slow on purpose.** Directorate doctrine is to know first and arrive in
numbers ([factions.md](factions.md)); a cohort in transit moves at the speed of its Cantor and
does not spread out, because spreading out is how you stop being numbers. From the South Mouth to
the North Gallery is a little over sixteen minutes at transit speed, which is the mission's
length and is not a coincidence.

**It is audible the whole way, and it is audible because of the water rather than because of
them.** The column crosses the South Mouth's trench water at 09:00 by the clock — Abyssal Trench,
PF 1.60 axial — and a Cantor at 35 idle carries as 56 down that axis. Inside the Fifth the same
column drops to 0.70 water and gets quieter as it gets closer. **A player who tracks the column
by loudness rather than by bearing will conclude it turned back at about the four-minute mark**,
and nothing in the mission corrects them.

**It goes to formation transit at 16:30, and that is the sixty seconds.** A cohort closing the
last leg moves in step, which is loud, regular, and the single most legible sound in the mission.
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10 requires every failure to be audible sixty seconds out; this one is
audible sixty seconds out and it is the column announcing it rather than the mission warning
anybody.

**What the player cannot learn is whether the column will press.** Adze reads the corridor when it
stands and decides, and the decision is a conditional beat rather than a clock (§9). The mission
does not tell the player which way it will go, and the mission's own answer — *turn* — is not
knowable until it happens, which is the difference between building a deterrent and building a
trap.

## 7. What Is Heard

Everything below is the model's arithmetic, not an estimate: PF scales a signature on the path,
and [systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §8's quartered circle divides it by where the emitter is
pointed. The two compose, which is the whole of what this mission has to teach a player who
already sat mission 1.

**What a Knight corvette at 28 in the cone carries as:**

| Water | PF | Cone | Flank | Wake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The shoulders, either side of the defile | 0.70 | 19.6 | 6.9 | 2.0 |
| **The Fifth, before the corridor** | **0.70** | **19.6** | **6.9** | **2.0** |
| **The Fifth, inside a standing corridor** | **2.00** | **56.0** | **19.6** | **5.6** |
| The South Mouth, down the trench axis | 1.60 | 44.8 | 15.7 | 4.5 |

**And what the works carry as, which is the number the mission is actually played against:**

| Source | SIG | In the Fifth (0.70) | In the corridor (2.00) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Construction site, sustained for 150 s | 70 | 49.0 | 140.0 |
| A Spire holding an interval (§4) | 80 | 56.0 | 160.0 |
| A Spire idle, unpaired | 30 | 21.0 | — |
| The Bastion, all mission | 35 | 24.5 | — |

Three readings come out of those two tables, and they are the mission:

1. **The corridor is worth almost three of itself.** 0.70 to 2.00 is ×2.86, and a corvette that
   was carrying as 19.6 — quieter than the Bastion — is carrying as 56 the moment the interval
   closes over it. Nothing about the hull changed. This is
   [mission-thin-water.md](mission-thin-water.md) §1's distinction reached from the other
   direction: there the water stopped protecting a column; here a player builds the thing that
   stops protecting them.
2. **Facing still divides it, and that is why mission 1 came first.** 56 in the cone is 19.6 on
   the flank and 5.6 in the wake. The doctrine is not repealed inside the corridor — it is
   charged at three times the price, and a party that keeps its bows off the line is carrying
   roughly what an ordinary hull carries in ordinary water. **Mission 1's lesson is what makes
   mission 2's mistake survivable**, which is the argument for teaching them in this order.
3. **The site is the loudest thing in the mission and it is loudest where it matters.** 49 in
   crystal water, from a fixed point, for two and a half minutes, in front of a 1,200 m Tier-3
   dome. There is no version of this mission in which the column does not know where both nodes
   are going. The secret is never *whether*; it is only ever *how far south*.

**And what the corridor does to scatter.** The Fifth is scattered water
([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §3, "Scattered water"): every contact the column resolves on
the party is reported up to 30° off its bearing and up to 15% long, and so is every contact the
party resolves on the column, at every tier including the dome's Tier 3. None of the figures
above move — scatter never touches loudness — but the *site* the column can hear from 1,200 m is
placed on a bearing that lies, which is the one mercy the water shows the works. **A standing
corridor un-scatters the cells it writes.** Crystal ringing at one interval is the opposite of
scatter, so inside the line bearings are true and a ping transmitted from it returns no
phantoms. The corridor is therefore a megaphone in both senses: it carries the party at 2.00,
and it is the one water on the map where the column's instruments tell the truth about them. A
party that keeps its bows off the line is carrying as an ordinary hull; a party that keeps its
*hulls* off the line is also lied about, and reading 2 means choosing which of those to give up.

## 8. The Objective

**Close the Fifth, and be outside it when it closes.**

Two terminal rows, and the second one is the whole doctrine:

1. **A corridor stands at the close.** The format cannot currently ask this — there is no
   predicate over what the player has built — which is §13's union row and the reason this
   document ships further ahead of its literal than any before it.
2. **All six hulls are in the North Gallery at the close** —
   `{ kind: 'extract'; role: 'works'; region: 'north-gallery'; count: 6 }`, every hull back above
   the line it laid.

**The second row is `extract` rather than `survive`, and the substitution is the point.** Survival
is the shape a reviewer expects and it is the wrong one: six hulls alive inside their own kill-line
is not the mission being met, it is the mission being ignored. The ask is *withdrawal*.

### Why the withdrawal is the objective and not the reading

The issue this mission answers put the constraint sharply: *the corridor cannot be a trap that
only catches the enemy, or the doctrine [factions.md](factions.md) states is false and the mission
has quietly written the Knights a free win.* The document's answer is that **the symmetry has to
cost the player a thing they wanted, at the moment they want it, or it is decoration.**

An objective that only counted the closure would let a player park all six hulls in the corridor's
mouth, hold the ground, take whatever the line does to them, and win. The line would be a
downside the player was permitted to ignore. Making the withdrawal terminal means the corridor is
**a piece of ground the player has to give up in order to have built it**, and that is a decision
in every single run: the further south the line is laid, the further the hulls have to come back,
and the ground they are coming back through is the ground they made lethal.

**A breach, named so it can be recognised.** If a competent player can lay the line and leave
without routing around anything — if the corridor is somewhere no Knight hull ever needed to be —
then the mission has failed at its own subject and the map is wrong rather than the rule. §11's
geometry is arranged against exactly that: the buildable band is north of the narrows, the
withdrawal is north through it, and there is no eastern or western way around inside the defile.
**The transcription found the breach anyway, one step over from where this paragraph looked for
it.** The engine has no builder hull — a structure is a command with a position, buildable from
anywhere inside an own structure's radius — so the line could be laid from a Gallery no Knight
hull ever left. The literal closes it with one rule and §13 records it: a site needs one of the
works within 400 m of it, the sounding radius, the distance at which a Knight hull is doing a
thing to the water by hand. So the line is laid from inside the ground it makes lethal, and the
six come back out through it, which is the mission.

### The failure, and the sounds that precede it

**The mission is not failed on a timer and does not end on one.** It is failed by the column
reaching the North Gallery with no corridor standing behind it, and the column arrives at 17:30
by a walk that has been audible since 00:40 (§6).

The sixty seconds are the column's own: **formation transit begins at 16:30**, at the narrows,
1,800 m out, and a cohort moving in step through 0.70 water is the most legible sound in the
mission. There is no alarm and no line of dialogue attached to it. It is
[campaign.md](campaign.md) §10's requirement met by the opponent doing its job loudly, which is
the same way [mission-thin-water.md](mission-thin-water.md) §8 met it and, on this map, the only
honest way available.

**A second warning exists and belongs to the player's own instrument.** A node under fire detunes
below 40% of its HP: the interval it holds goes flat, audibly, and — because the corridor carries
at 2.00 — audibly to the people shooting at it as soon as they hold a *track* on the node they are
shooting. Not before. A flat is a damage state, and damage state is Tier 4's disclosure and nobody
else's: classification says a Spire is there, a track says how it is
([audio-direction.md](audio-direction.md) §9). **This paragraph used to say "everyone on the map",
and it promised more than the wire has ever carried** — the correction is #402's, and it is the
better rule as well as the honest one, because it makes the column's warning a thing the column had
to earn rather than a gift from the mission. The objective reads *The interval is sour*. From detune to loss is not less than sixty seconds against anything the
column carries, so a corridor that falls announces its own falling first. **A player is never
told that their kill-line is failing by seeing a health bar; they are told by hearing it go
flat**, which is the mission's one piece of pure register and its only concession to comfort.

## 10. What It Teaches

One system, per [campaign.md](campaign.md) §10: **paired nodes, corridors and PF 2.00** — a place
the player builds that changes how sound moves through it, for everyone, permanently, at the
price of a budget that does not refill. It lands in order across the beat table: the cost (the
first 750), the pairing rule (the odd node, taught by having it do nothing), the effect (the
interval closing over ground the player was standing on), the symmetry (the withdrawal), and the
economy (the third node, or the twelve minutes not spent on one).

Underneath it, the campaign's real subject, which is the same trick rule 2 plays on every faction:
**the Order is told nothing about being few, and is now told nothing about being poor either.**
Six hulls against a canyon, 1,530 nodules against a road, and a stipend that arrives at the same
rate whether the player is winning or losing. Nobody says *we cannot grow*. The player reaches it
by looking at a number that does not move.

And a third thing, which is why the mission is called what it is. **A standing wave is the one
kind of wave that does not go anywhere.** It is what you get when a sound meets itself coming
back, and its defining property is that it has places where nothing moves at all. The Order has
built its entire doctrine on aiming loudness, and the first thing it builds in this campaign is a
loudness with no direction in it — a thing that is exactly as loud behind you as in front. A
player who works that out is holding the Order's whole problem: a faction that has spent a
century making noise directional, whose only constructible weapon is the one shape of noise that
refuses to be.

What this mission deliberately does not teach:

- **Permanent loss** — mission 3, *Nineteen*. Hulls lost here are lost for this mission and no
  further, and §8's "Untouched" reading is a *reading* rather than a stake for exactly that
  reason. [mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §10 is right that "six is a small number to
  work with" and "six is a small number to have" are different lessons, and a mission 2 that
  made hulls unreplaceable would have spent mission 3 to make its own last five minutes tenser.
- **Projected depth access** — §3. The Spire's PR+1 is live and inert on a Mid-Water map, and the
  campaign gap [mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §10 flagged stays flagged rather than
  being half-filled by a structure that happened to be in the mission for another reason.
- **Fauna, and what a PF 2.00 corridor does to them.** A megaphone in a canyon is the loudest
  standing invitation in the game and the Drift would come to it ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md) §2).
  That is a genuinely good mission and it is the Directorate's — §6 row 5 of
  [campaign.md](campaign.md), *Trench Awakening* — and seeding one pack here would teach fauna
  aggro by accident in a mission that has a system already. The map is authored with `fauna:
  false` and §13 says so.
- **Active sonar** — §3, and the campaign's oldest argument, which for this faction is also its
  shortest.

## 11. The Map

`the-fifth` · **The Fifth** · one seat · 5,000 × 4,000 m · cell 250 m · base floor 1,700 m.

The Fields' southern margin, below the thermocline and above the Abyssal line: **every metre of

not enter the mission either ([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md) §3). Both are deliberate, and
both are [mission-aptitude.md](mission-aptitude.md) §11's argument reused: one system per mission
means the other two axes have to be arranged to say nothing.

### The geometry is the mission, and it comes out of two numbers already in the code

`CONSTRUCTION.BUILD_RADIUS_M` is **1,500 m** — "new structures must rise within this range of an
existing own structure" — and the pairing range is the same **1,500 m**
([systems-echo.md](systems-echo.md)). One number, against a Bastion at (2500, 400) and a
defile a kilometre wide, produces the whole decision space without anything being scripted:

- **The buildable band is the northern thirteen hundred metres of the Fifth.** Both walls are
  inside 1,500 m of the Bastion down to about y = 1,800; below that, nothing can be sited without
  something else being sited first. (It was the northern eleven hundred, to y = 1,400, when the
  radius was 1,200 m — #372 moved it, and §4's stipend timing has not been re-tuned against the
  wider band.)
- **A corridor laid wall to wall is 900–1,000 m** — inside the pairing range with room to spare,
  and long enough to span the defile. Nodes set in from the walls leave a gap, and a gap is a
  road; the column will use it and the mission will not mention that it did.
- **Reaching south of the narrows costs a third structure**, sited as far south as the band allows
  and paid for by the stipend at 12:30 (§4). That is the only reason a player would ever want a
  third node, and it is why the economy and the geometry are the same decision rather than two.
- **The build radius and the pairing range are the same number**, by decision rather than
  coincidence (#372): a node a commander can site off its partner is a node that pairs, so the
  longest corridor the rules describe is also the longest one that can be built. The mission was
  arranged to work under the smaller of the two numbers while they disagreed, and still does.

**Every rectangle lands on the 250 m cell grid and paints exactly the metres it reads.** The
shoulders are painted first and the three cut into them in the order of the table, so the Gallery
and the Mouth overwrite the Fields the way [maps.md](maps.md) requires.

## 12. The Briefing

Spoken by Choirmaster Ivane Sull from the Ninth, at the top of the works. The Knight register is
defined in [culture.md](culture.md) §3: precise, courteous, slightly archaic, musical and
mathematical metaphor used literally rather than poetically, and unfailingly polite in a way that
reads to everyone else as condescension.

**Choirmaster Ivane Sull, setting the works — 00:00**

> The Fifth has been a road for a hundred and forty years because we never wrote down that it was
> ours. I am not going to write it down now. I am going to make it a poor road.
>
> Two nodes, either wall, and the interval between them. You know what that does. It is worth
> saying the whole of what it does rather than the flattering half: the line does not know whose
> hulls are in it, and the water inside it will carry you exactly as far as it carries them. That
> is not a defect I am asking you to tolerate. **It is the specification.** A door that only shuts
> one way is not a door, it is a preference, and a preference is the sort of thing an Order talks
> itself out of at three in the morning eleven years from now.
>
> So: build it, and then be north of it. Both halves. If you bring me a closed Fifth and four
> hulls I will thank you and I will mean it and I will also have to explain the arithmetic to the
> Third, and Halden will be courteous about it, which is worse.
>
> You have the stipend and the stipend does not hurry. Two voices are paid for. A third is twelve
> and a half minutes, and I would rather you spent them than spent the Fifth.

### Objective readings, in play

The Order states intervals and conditions, never tasks, and is courteous to the point of
formality:

- *The Fifth is open. No voice stands.*
- *One voice. A voice is not an interval.*
- *The interval is held. The Fifth carries at two.*
- *The interval is sour.*
- *The interval is held and the works are north of it.*

### The voices in the water

**Cohort-Prime Adze, on the cohort's open channel — 03:00**

> Route is filed. Works are audible at bearing three-five-two, seventy, sustained, which is a
> commissioning and not a hull. The route does not change on account of a building. It changes on
> account of what the building is for, and we will know that in four minutes.

**Choirmaster Ivane Sull, at the pairing — on the interval closing**

> There. That is the Fifth, and that is the last time anybody hears it the way it was. Somebody
> should write down what it sounded like before. I am aware nobody will.

**Voice Ren Kalliso, once, to nobody in particular — on the first node completing**

> One of them does nothing. Of course it does nothing. Half a chord is not a quieter chord, it is
> a note, and a note is just a fact about a string.

**Cohort-Prime Adze, reading the corridor — 09:00**

> Corridor stands at two. Transit cost through it exceeds the route's value by a factor I am not
> going to argue with. We turn at the Mouth and file the ground as closed. Log it as closed by
> them, not against us — the distinction will matter to somebody who was not here.

**Choirmaster Ivane Sull, at the close — 18:00**

> The reading of the Fifth, per §8, and — in every ending but the last — one more sentence: "Go
> and be dry."

Each line fails [culture.md](culture.md) §6's register test for the other three factions, which is
that document's own bar. Sull's briefing specifies a weapon's symmetry as a *feature* and then
gives a domestic reason for it — what the Order might talk itself into at three in the morning —
which the Consortium would have costed, the Directorate would have stated as sufficiency, and the
Commune could not have phrased at all without opening it for discussion. Adze's 03:00 line
distinguishes a commissioning from a hull by its signature alone and says so as a matter of
record, which is a sentence only the Listeners have the vocabulary for; the 09:00 line files a
defeat as a fact about somebody else's ground and is careful about the wording for the benefit of
a reader who does not exist yet, which is the Directorate's whole relationship with its own
archive. And Kalliso's one line is a soldier explaining an instrument to herself in the plainest
words available, which is the thing that marks her out in [characters.md](characters.md) as the
Order's most ordinary person — the Choirmaster would have called it an unresolved interval and
meant something by it.
